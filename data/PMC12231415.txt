Abstract Background and aims Individuals with Internet Gaming Disorder (IGD) exhibit a heightened reward responsiveness to gaming-related rewards, alongside a diminished response to natural rewards. However, the temporal dynamics and neural correlates underlying this imbalanced processing remain unclear. Methods This electrophysiological study investigated the neural responses associated with reward processing and their relationship to self-reported reward responsiveness. Using an Incentive Delay Task, we compared neural responses to gaming and monetary rewards between 25 IGD participants and 32 recreational game users (RGUs). Self-reported reward responsiveness was assessed with the Behavioral Approach/Inhibition System (BAS/BIS) scales. Results The IGD group scored higher on the BAS-responsiveness subscale. Correlation analysis indicated that enhanced BAS-responsiveness was associated with automatic attention (N1) to gaming feedback, but not with feedback monitoring (FRN) or emotional arousal (LPP). No such correlation was found in the monetary condition. Following initial automatic attention, the IGD group demonstrated selective feedback monitoring (FRN) for gaming rewards while neglecting monetary feedback. Discussion and Conclusions Gaming stimuli automatically capture the attention of individuals with IGD, triggering less top-down monitoring of other potential positive feedback. These findings suggest that attentional bias toward game-related stimuli serves as a sensitive biological marker of heightened reward responsiveness in individuals with IGD. Keywords internet gaming disorder event-related potentials reward responsiveness automatic attention National Natural Science Foundation of China 32161133004 For subsequent analysis, we focused on unexpected feedback, which refers to instances where the outcome was inconsistent with the participant's choice —— either selecting “Yes” followed by reward omission, or selecting “No” followed by a reward. Reward pictures The gaming images used in this study were sourced from Honor of Kings and were captured from live gameplay by non-professional players, specifically during moments when the player was in a favorable position, such as pushing a tower or achieving a kill. The monetary images depicted various denominations of currency. To ensure that the images represented generalized rewarding attributes, we recruited 18 active Honor of Kings players assessed the valence and arousal of the images using a 10-point scale. A total of fifty gaming images (valence: M = 7.34, SD = 1.01; arousal: M = 7.31, SD = 0.54; 960 × 540 pixels) and fifty monetary images (valence: M = 7.23, SD = 0.89; arousal: M = 7.16, SD = 0.73; 960 × 540 pixels) were selected. No significant differences were found between gaming and monetary rewards in terms of valence ( t (98) = 0.26, p = 0.796) and arousal ( t (98) = 1.20, p = 0.232). Questionnaire The Internet Addiction Test (IAT) was used to assess the severity of IGD ( Young, 1998 ). The Chinese version employed in this study was specifically adapted for internet gaming ( Li, 2018 ), and has demonstrated robust validity in previous research ( Dong et al., 2017 , 2020 ). Items are rated on a 5-point scale, with total scores ranging from 20 to 100. In the present study, the Cronbach's α for this scale was 0.93. The Generalized Anxiety Disorder Scale (GAD-7) was used to measure anxiety symptoms. Originally developed by Spitzer et al. (2006) , the Chinese version was translated and validated among Chinese populations by He, Li, Qian, Cui, and Wu (2010) . It consists of 7 items that assess the frequency of core anxiety symptoms over the past two weeks, with scores ranging from 0 (not at all) to 3 (nearly every day). In the present study, the Cronbach's α for this scale was 0.92. The Patient Health Questionnaire-9 (PHQ-9) was used to assess depressive symptoms ( Manea et al., 2015 ). This 9-item self-report scale evaluates the frequency of depressive symptoms over the past two weeks, with scores ranging from 0 (never) to 3 (every day). It is a valid and efficient tool for screening depression in Chinese populations ( Wang et al., 2014 ). In the present study, the Cronbach's α for this scale was 0.89. The Behavioral Activation System/Behavioral Inhibition System (BAS/BIS) scales ( Carver &amp; White, 1994 ) were used to measure reward responsiveness. The BAS scale includes three subscales: Reward Responsiveness (positive reactions to rewards), Drive (persistence in pursuing goals), and Fun Seeking (desire for rewards and willingness to approach rewarding events). BAS is associated with approach behaviors, while BIS regulates avoidance. Each item is scored on a scale from 1 (almost never) to 4 (almost always). The Chinese version used in this study was adapted by Ying, Jiang, Yu, and Lu (2016) . The Cronbach's α for the subscales ranged from 0.52 to 0.76. EEG recording and analysis Brain electrical activity was recorded by a 64-channel wireless amplifier (NeuSen.W64, Neuracle, Changzhou, China) with a sampling frequency of 1,000 Hz. The reference electrode was placed at CPz, and the ground electrode was placed at AFz. All electrode impedance was kept below 10 kΩ. EEG data were re-referenced offline to the average of the mastoids, band- pass between 0.1 and 30 Hz, and segmented from 200 ms before to 800 ms after outcome presentation. Epochs were baseline-corrected (−200 to 0 ms) and averaged for each condition. Independent component analysis (ICA) was performed to remove signal artifacts mainly due to eye movements ( Makeig, Bell, Jung, &amp; Sejnowski, 1996 ). The analysis was performed using MATLAB 2020a and the EEGLAB toolbox. We focused on N1, FRN, and LPP components at the frontal and parietal regions. For each trial type, N1 was identified as the most negative peak between 70 and 150 ms on Cz after outcome onset ( Duncan et al., 2022 ; Yu, Sun, Xu, &amp; Ma, 2018 ). FRN was measured as the average amplitude at FCz within 250–350 ms post-outcome onset ( Sambrook &amp; Goslin, 2015 ). LPP was measured as the average amplitude at Pz, P3, P4, PO3, and PO4 from 400 to 800 ms ( Sadeh &amp; Verona, 2012 ). Statistical analysis H1 : To examine automatic attention, feedback monitoring and emotional arousal (N1/FRN/LPP) in IGD, mixed-design ANOVAs were conducted for each ERP component, with feedback type (game/monetary) as the within-subject factor and group (IGD/RGU) as the between-subject factor. Greenhouse-Geisser correction was applied when sphericity was violated (Mauchly’ s test p &lt; 0.05). Effect sizes ( η p 2 ) and significant (at p &lt; 0.05) effects were reported. H2 : To assess stage specificity, Pearson correlations between BAS/BIS scores and ERP amplitudes (N1/FRN/LPP) during the gaming condition were calculated. H3 : To assess type specificity, Pearson correlations between BAS scores and N1 amplitudes were calculated separately for gaming and monetary conditions. Ethics Written informed consent was obtained from all participants. The study adhered to the Declaration of Helsinki and was approved by the Institutional Review Board of the Institute of Psychology, Chinese Academy of Sciences (reference No. H21099 ). Results Demographic information and questionnaires Matched samples Table 1 showed the demographic data for the IGD and RGU groups. There were no significant differences between the groups in terms of age, gender, or educational level ( p &gt; 0.05). Most participants primarily played Honor of Kings . The IGD group scored higher on the IAT scale ( p &lt; 0.001). Higher BAS responsiveness in the IGD group Compared to the control group, the IGD group exhibited a trend towards higher total BAS scores ( p = 0.074), with a significant difference on the responsiveness subscale ( p = 0.039). In contrast, both groups had similar scores on the BIS subscale ( p = 0.923), indicating that IGD participants displayed greater sensitivity to reward-seeking than to punishment avoidance. Manipulation check Manipulation check was performed to confirm that the task generated a sufficient number of unexpected feedback trials ( Table 2 ). Our result indicated that these trials met the criteria for reliable ERP component analysis ( Cohen &amp; Polich, 1997 ; Marco-Pallares, Cucurell, Munte, Strien, &amp; Rodriguez-Fornells, 2011 ). ERP results Enhanced automatic attention to both gaming and monetary feedback A Group × Type ANOVA on the N1 amplitudes revealed a significant main effect of group, F (1, 55) = 5.62, p = 0.021, η p 2 = 0.09, with higher N1 amplitudes in the IGD group versus the RGU group (−3.90 μV vs. −2.76 μV). However, the main effect of type and the Group × Type interaction effect were not significant, p = 0.182 and p = 0.471, respectively. This suggested that individuals with IGD showed elevated automatic attention to reward, regardless the type ( Fig. 2 ). Selective feedback monitoring to gaming feedback A Group × Type ANOVA on the FRN amplitudes revealed a significant main effect of type, F (1, 55) = 18.56, p &lt; 0.001, η p 2 = 0.25, with higher FRN amplitudes in response to gaming versus monetary feedback (−2.43 μV vs. −0.80 μV). Notably, the Group × Type interaction, F (1, 55) = 5.49, p = 0.023, was significant. A simple effects analysis showed that FRN amplitude elicited by monetary feedback in the IGD group was smaller than gaming feedback (0.06 μV vs. −2.46 μV), p &lt; 0.001, while the RGU group showed no significant difference in FRN amplitudes between reward types ( p = 0.144). This suggested that individuals with IGD selectively monitor gaming feedback while neglecting other feedback type ( Fig. 3 ). Undifferentiated emotional arousal to both gaming and monetary feedback A Group × Type ANOVA on the LPP amplitudes revealed a significant main effect of type, F (1, 55) = 4.97, p = 0.030, η p 2 = 0.08, with higher LPP amplitudes in response to gaming versus monetary feedback (5.16 vs. 4.28 μV). However, the main effect of group and the Group × Type interaction effect were not significant, p = 0.812 and p = 0.167, respectively ( Fig. 4 ). Correlations among self-reported reward responsiveness and ERPs The IGD group exhibited heightened reward responsiveness and altered neural responses to rewards, as indicated by the findings above. To further explore the neural basis of this increased reward responsiveness in the IGD population, a correlation analysis was conducted between self-reported BAS responsiveness scores and ERP measures. Higher self-reported BAS responsiveness was correlated with automatic attention to gaming feedback rather than later stages The correlation results for the total sample were shown in Table 3 . The amplitudes of N1 in gaming conditions were negatively correlated with the BAS-responsiveness scores, p = 0.001. Such correlations were not found in FRN and LPP, p = 0.070, p = 0.840, respectively. Since N1 is a negative-going component, the negative correlation means that higher BAS-responsiveness scores were associated with larger N1 amplitudes. Furthermore, the FRN in gaming conditions was positively correlated with the BAS-drive scores, and negatively correlated with the BIS scores in gaming conditions. Higher self-reported BAS responsiveness was correlated with automatic attention to gaming feedback rather than monetary feedback We calculated the correlation between the amplitude of N1 to gaming or monetary feedback and BAS-responsiveness scores in each group, respectively. In the gaming condition, the correlation was significant only in the IGD group, r (23) = 0.58, p = 0.002, and not in the RGU group, r (30) = 0.24, p = 0.184 ( Fig. 5 ). In the monetary condition, the correlations were not significant in both IGD group, r (23) = 0.19, p = 0.364, and the RGU group, r (30) = 0.09, p = 0.616 ( Fig. 5 ). Discussion In this study, we found that individuals with IGD exhibited higher self-reported reward responsiveness, which was specially correlated with the enhanced automatic attention to gaming feedback. In addition, blunted monitoring ability to monetary feedback was also found. This study enhances our understanding of the neural mechanisms underlying reward responsiveness in IGD, emphasizing the role of automatic attention to gaming stimuli. At the automatic attention stage of reward processing, IGD individuals exhibited a more negative N1 component. Previous studies suggest that N1 reflects unconscious discrimination processes, indicating that individuals quickly detect salient stimuli and automatically direct attention based on prior experiences ( Gable &amp; Harmon-Jones, 2013 ; Vogel &amp; Luck, 2000 ). Contrary to our hypothesis, the enhanced N1 was not specific to gaming. Our findings suggest that IGD individuals are more attentive to both gaming and natural rewards, consistent with evidence linking IGD to poor cognitive control, heightened impulsivity, and increased sensation-seeking ( Weinstein, Ben Abu, Timor, &amp; Mama, 2016 ). During the feedback monitoring stage, the FRN was smaller for monetary feedback than for gaming feedback in the IGD group. This aligns with previous studies showing that IGD individuals exhibit diminished sensitivity to non-gaming rewards, such as money ( Raiha et al., 2020 ; Yao et al., 2020 ; Zhang, Zhao, Mao, Zhang, &amp; Xing, 2024 ) or food cues ( Zhou et al., 2021 ). FRN is typically associated with performance monitoring following unexpected events, helping guide future behavioral adjustments ( Hajcak, Moser, Holroyd, &amp; Simons, 2007 ; Paul, Vassena, Severo, &amp; Pourtois, 2020 ). The reduced FRN suggests that IGD individuals selectively monitor gaming-related feedback and may struggle to learn from general reinforcements in daily life, potentially contributing to negative psychological and physical outcomes ( Matias et al., 2023 ). At the emotional arousal stage, no significant group differences were observed. LPP reflects motivational activation, with higher amplitudes indicating more cognitive resources allocated for detailed processing ( Broyd et al., 2012 ; Hajcak, Moser, &amp; Simons, 2006 ). Contrary to prior studies showing larger LPP responses to gaming stimuli in IGD individuals compared to non-players ( Kim et al., 2021 ), our results revealed that both IGD and recreational game users exhibited a greater LPP response to gaming feedback. This suggests that heightened emotional arousal may be a general characteristic of gamers, not a specific biomarker of IGD. We also found that elevated BAS sensitivity, as indicated by BAS responsiveness, specifically correlated with an enhanced N1 component evoked by gaming feedback. Notably, this pattern was absent during the feedback monitoring and emotional arousal stages. While the cross-sectional design of the current study prevents causal inferences, the observed correlations can provide insights into potential mechanisms. Our findings reveal that IGD individuals have a pronounced tendency to approach rewards, which aligns with a meta-analysis of longitudinal studies identifying sensation seeking as a risk factor for developing IGD ( Zhuang et al., 2023 ). Furthermore, certain reward-related gene variants, such as the DRD2 Taq1A1 allele, have been shown to predict IGD ( Han et al., 2007 ). When exposed to the abundant positive experiences in structured gaming environments, they may become more preoccupied and allocate disproportionate cognitive resources to gaming. Over time, gaming rewards may dominate their attention systems, overshadowing other potential positive rewards in the environment ( Bai, Elhai, Montag, &amp; Yang, 2023 ; van Holst et al., 2012 ). Importantly, unlike substance use disorders involving alcohol, nicotine, or illicit drugs, IGD show unique abnormality in sensory processing ( Dong et al., 2020 ; Han et al., 2015 ; He et al., 2019 ). For example, regions associated with visual and auditory processing, such as the superior and inferior temporal gyri, exhibit negative connectivity to executive networks in individuals with IGD ( Han et al., 2015 ). Additionally, the precuneus serves as a critical hub for integrating information from addiction-relevant cues processed by the visual system and motivated behavior systems, with its impairment correlating with IGD severity ( Dong et al., 2020 ). Our findings suggest that heightened reward approach sensitivity in individuals with IGD is linked to increased automatic attention to gaming rewards, rather than reward monitoring or emotional arousal, underscoring the dysregulated sensory processing in this population. Overall, our findings support the incentive sensitization theory ( Robinson &amp; Berridge, 2008 ), which posits that individuals with addiction develop heightened responsiveness to addiction-related rewards. In IGD, this manifests as exaggerated automatic attention to gaming feedback, but not during later stages of reward processing. However, several limitations of this study should be acknowledged. First, the relatively small sample size limits the generalizability of the results. Future research with larger and more diverse samples, including different age groups and clinical populations, is needed to confirm these findings. Second, while Honor of Kings is a representative game of the Multiplayer Online Battle Arena (MOBA) genre, previous studies have identified nuanced characteristics among different gaming genres, such as online role-playing games and first-person shooters ( Rehbein, King, Staudt, Hayer, &amp; Rumpf, 2021 ). Therefore, the results should be interpreted with caution when generalizing to players of other game genres. Third, our study specifically contrasts gaming rewards with monetary rewards as a proxy for natural rewards. Further research is needed to validate these findings across other types of non-gaming rewards (e.g., social interactions). Conclusions This study reveals that individuals with IGD exhibit heightened BAS responsiveness, which is associated with increased N1 amplitudes in response to gaming feedback, indicating enhanced automatic attention to gaming rewards. Additionally, individuals with IGD selectively monitor gaming feedback while neglecting monetary feedback, suggesting a diminished ability to adjust their behaviors based on non-gaming feedback. No significant differences were observed in the emotional arousal stage between IGD and recreational gamers. These findings underscore the significance of reward responsiveness in addictive disorders and provide valuable insights into the neurophysiological mechanisms underlying IGD.
