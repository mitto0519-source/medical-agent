In this issue, 1 review and 4 original articles have been published. 1. Occupational Risk Factors for Skin Cancer: A Comprehensive Review. Kang MY, et al. This review highlights the importance of identifying modifiable risk factors for skin cancer to aid in primary prevention. It summarizes epidemiological evidence on occupational risk factors, showing a significant relationship between non-melanoma skin cancers and exposure to solar, ultraviolet, and ionizing radiation, arsenic, and mineral oils. For melanoma, sufficient evidence links it to occupational exposure to pesticides and polychlorinated biphenyls, with higher risks reported among petroleum refinery workers and firefighters. This analysis provides a foundation for future research and targeted preventive measures against skin cancer in the working population. 2. Nationwide Target Trial Emulation Evaluating the Clinical Effectiveness of Oral Antivirals for COVID-19 in Korea. Jung J, et al. This study reevaluated the effectiveness of oral antivirals for COVID-19 in the context of widespread immunity and milder variants. It analyzed patients aged 60+ from January to December 2022 using data from the Korea Disease Control and Prevention Agency. The study involved 957,036 patients treated with nirmatrelvir/ritonavir and 243,360 with molnupiravir. Both treatments significantly reduced the risk of severe, critical, and fatal COVID-19. Nirmatrelvir/ritonavir showed higher effectiveness, especially in older adults, unvaccinated individuals, and during the late pandemic phase. The study concluded that these antivirals are effective in preventing severe disease progression in elderly adults. 3. Reported Adverse Events and Associated Factors in Korean Coronavirus Disease 2019 Vaccinations. Park B, et al. This study examined the adverse effects (AEs) of COVID-19 vaccines, using data from the Korea Disease Control and Prevention Agency. It analyzed 42,804,523 vaccinated individuals, with 365,900 reporting AEs like headache, muscle pain, and fever. Women reported AEs twice as often as men, especially after both doses. Middle-aged adults (50–64 years) reported more AEs than other age groups. Higher AE reports were linked to lower socioeconomic status and underlying health conditions, excluding dementia. The study highlights the need for ongoing monitoring to ensure vaccine safety and address anxiety, particularly among women, younger people, those with lower socioeconomic status, and those with health conditions. 4. Genome-Wide Association Analysis of Rapid Decline in Lung Function: Analysis From the Korean Genome and Epidemiology Study. Choi JY, et al. This study analyzed 6,516 individuals from the Korean Genome and Epidemiology Study (KoGES) to investigate the rapid decline in forced expiratory volume in 1 second (FEV1), a key indicator of chronic obstructive pulmonary disease (COPD) development. A genome-wide association analysis identified 15 nucleotide variants significantly associated with rapid FEV1 decline ( p &lt; 5.0 × 10 -8 ). Among these, 13 were newly discovered, while rs9833533 and rs1496255 had been previously linked to lung function development. The findings highlight 15 genetic variants related to rapid FEV1 decline, contributing to our understanding of lung function development and COPD progression. 5. Rescue Cerclage in Women With Acute Cervical Insufficiency and Intra-Amniotic Inflammation: A Retrospective Cohort Study. Park KH, et al. This study assessed the effectiveness of rescue cerclage in pregnant women with cervical insufficiency (CI) and intra-amniotic inflammation (IAI). Among 87 women, 65.5% had IAI, and 73.6% of them received macrolide antibiotics. Of the 57 CI patients with IAI, 40 underwent rescue cerclage, while 17 were managed expectantly. The cerclage group had significantly lower rates of spontaneous preterm birth (SPTB) at &lt; 28 and &lt; 34 weeks, longer latency periods, higher birth weights, and better neonatal survival rates. Rescue cerclage and macrolide antibiotics were associated with improved outcomes, suggesting the benefit of cerclage even in CI patients with IAI.
