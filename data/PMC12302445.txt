Abstract Background A Chinese multicenter prospective cohort study of supraclavicular oblique incision sternocleidomastoid intermuscular approach (SOI-SMIA) versus traditional low collar incision linea alba cervicalis approach (TCLI-LACA) in thyroid lobectomy for papillary thyroid carcinoma (LATERAL study) has been initiated. The aim is to compare surgical approach-related outcomes in thyroid lobectomy. We here outline the detailed protocol for this study. Methods PTC which can be treated by lobectomy is considered for inclusion in the study. Eligible patients will receive printed materials that detail the advantages and disadvantages of each management option, allowing them to select their preferred intervention. Thyroid lobectomy by SOI-SMIA or TCLI-LACA will be performed within 24 h after grouping. We designed a composite index of surgical approach-related trauma as the primary endpoint. It includes both the superficial (scar consciousness and appearance satisfaction) component and the deep component (neck impairment), and will be assessed at 1 month and 6 months after surgery. The LATERAL study will be conducted in three hospitals in China. We plan to recruit 80 patients per group and enroll patients consecutively over time and will conclude once the group with the smaller number of participants reaches 80 cases. Conclusion The results of this trial will contribute to confirming the role of SOI-SMIA in thyroid lobectomy for PTC, and provide evidence to support its wider adoption. Introduction Thyroid cancer (TC) is a malignant tumor of the thyroid and the most common malignant tumor of the endocrine system, originating from the thyroid follicular epithelium or follicular paraepithelial cells. Papillary thyroid carcinoma (PTC) is the most common in thyroid carcinoma, accounting for 85% of all thyroid malignancies [ 1 ]. PTC is notably more prevalent in women than in men, with studies indicating that women are approximately three times more likely to develop the disease. The incidence of PTC among women has been increasing over the past few decades [ 2 ]. Currently, thyroid lobectomy is the primary treatment for papillary thyroid carcinoma (PTC), and is frequently supplemented with endocrine therapy and radioactive nuclide therapy postoperatively [ 3 ]. Since the 19th century, when Theodor Kocher introduced the traditional low collar incision Linea Alba Cervicalis Approach (TLCI-LACA) and demonstrated its safety and efficacy, this surgical approach has been widely adopted. The advantages of this approach, such as high surgical safety and simplicity, have established it as the standard incision for thyroid lobectomy in clinical practice [ 4 ]. However, with increasing awareness of health and improved living standards, greater emphasis is now placed on postoperative cosmetic outcomes and quality of life, which are also key concerns in thyroid surgery [ 5 ]. TLCI-LACA frequently results in visible scars, which are difficult to conceal. Postoperatively, some patients may experience neck discomfort, swallowing dysfunction, and free flap area swelling [ 6 ]. As a result, non-cervical incisions with endoscopic surgeries and modified minimally invasive incisions, have emerged as two significant directions for the development of thyroid surgical techniques. Although endoscopic surgery offers the advantage of being non-invasive to the neck, its drawbacks, including significant surgical trauma, limited operative space, strict indications, and high cost limit its widespread adoption. It is often regarded as a cosmetic procedure rather than a minimally invasive procedure. In terms of modified minimally invasive cervical incisions, a notable innovation aimed at reducing postoperative neck discomfort and achieving better aesthetic results is the supraclavicular oblique incision sternocleidomastoid intermuscular approach (SOI-SMIA) [ 7 ]. SOI-SMIA thyroid lobectomy The incision in SOI-SMIA surgery is strategically placed along the lowest skin crease above the clavicle, approximately 1 cm superior to the clavicle, extending posteriorly and superiorly from the posterior edge of the sternocleidomastoid muscle by approximately 3 to 5 cm. Upon incision, the skin, subcutaneous tissue, and platysma are meticulously dissected. The dissection continues to free the lower anterior edge of the sternocleidomastoid muscle. Subsequently, the posterior edge of the strap muscles in the anterior neck region is mobilized. Retractors are then employed to achieve optimal exposure of the thyroid lobe requiring excision. A single-center retrospective study conducted at our institution, including 128 patients, showed that SOI-SMIA demonstrated superior outcomes in terms of cervical sensory function, neck mobility, and cosmetic satisfaction compared to TLCI-LACA at one month and six months postoperatively [ 8 ]. Additionally, SOI-SMIA showed advantages in surgery-related metrics, such as incision length, postoperative drainage volume, and hospital stay duration. This study preliminarily validates the safety and benefits of the SOI-SMIA in thyroid lobectomy. The advantages are primarily observed in both the superficial aspects of the neck incision (including incision length and cosmetic satisfaction) and the deeper aspects (including cervical sensory function and neck mobility). These factors are inherently related to the surgical incision and approach. However, this study is limited by its small sample size and single-center retrospective design. Currently, there is a lack of prospective clinical studies comparing the application of SOI-SMIA and TLCI-LACA in thyroid lobectomy. Therefore, we propose to conduct a multicenter prospective trial (LATERAL study) to investigate the impact of SOI-SMIA compared to TLCI-LACA on surgical approach-related outcomes in thyroid lobectomy for papillary thyroid carcinoma. Methods Study design This is a prospective, multicenter, non-randomized, open-label, controlled superiority clinical trial. The overall study design is shown in Fig. 1 . The study protocol was designed following the Standard Protocol Item Recommendations for Interventional Trials(SPIRIT) of 2013 [ 9 ]. The trial will adhere to the principles outlined in the Declaration of Helsinki and the Good Clinical Practice (GCP) guidelines established by the International Conference on Harmonization (ICH). Three referral hospitals in China are participating: Nanjing Tum Tower Hospital, Nanjing Central Hospital and Taikang Xianlin Drum Tower Hospital. The study protocol was approved by the ethics committee of primary sponsor (Nanjing Tum Tower Hospital). The protocol of this study is registered at the Chinese Clinical Trial Registry, ChiCTR ( https://www.chictr.org.cn/ , register number ChiCTR2500096758). Patients will be included from March 2025 to March 2026, and follow-up data will be collected until April 2026. Study population and enrollment PTC which can be treated by lobectomy is considered eligible for inclusion in the study. In the LATERAL study, the admission day will be defined as Day 0 and the second day as Day 1, and so on. The day when patients decide upon their treatment option will be defined as Day X. Patients with an eligible PTC will be informed of the possibility of taking part in the LATERAL trial at admission (Day 0). Patients meeting all the inclusion criteria, and no exclusion criteria will be given the printed material containing a detailed description of SOI-SMIA and TLCI-LACA in thyroid lobectomy (Day X-1). After careful consideration, patients will be asked to select their preferred surgical approach (Day X). They will be given up to 24 h to make this decision, allowing for an informed decision-making process. At the time of patient enrollment, physicians will explain the LATERAL protocol and obtain informed consent for participation. All the data stored in the electronic database are de-identified to guarantee patient’s privacy. Eligibility criteria The inclusion criteria are: Age between 18 and 75 years; PTC patients who need unilateral thyroid lobectomy according to the Guidelines for the Diagnosis and Management of Thyroid Nodules and Differentiated Thyroid Cancer(Second Edition)published by Chinese Society of Endocrinology, Thyroid and Metabolism Surgery Group of the Chinese Society of Surgery, China Anti-Cancer Association, et al [ 10 ]: Tumor diameter &lt; 1 cm: no history of adolescent head and neck radiation therapy, no family history of thyroid cancer, single lesion, confined to the thyroid, no clinically suspicious lymph node metastasis, no vascular invasion, and no distant metastasis. Tumor diameter between 1 and 2 cm: no history of adolescent head and neck radiation therapy, no family history of thyroid cancer, single lesion, confined to the thyroid, no clinically suspicious metastatic lymph nodes, no vascular invasion, no distant metastasis, and classified as low risk according to MACIS ((Metastasis, Age, Completeness of resection, Invasion, and Size) and other risk stratification systems. Tumor diameter between 2 and 4 cm: no history of adolescent head and neck radiation therapy, no family history of thyroid cancer, single lesion, confined to the thyroid with no minimal capsular invasion, no clinically suspicious metastatic lymph nodes, no vascular invasion, no distant metastasis, classified as low risk according to MACIS and other risk stratification systems, and the patient has a strong desire to preserve the contralateral thyroid lobe or is unable to undergo total thyroid lobectomy due to other underlying health conditions. The exclusion criteria are: Patients with severe underlying diseases affecting the heart, liver, kidneys, or lungs. Patients with a history of thyroid surgery. pregnant or breastfeeding patients. Patients with a tendency to develop keloids (keloid-prone). Any other conditions deemed unsuitable for enrollment by investigators (e.g., severe comorbidities, insufficient patient compliance, or other clinically relevant contraindications). Randomization and blinding method LATERAL study is non-randomized and does not involve any randomization methods. Participants will be assigned to groups or treatments based on predefined criteria rather than random assignment. In this open-label study, both the participants and the investigators will be aware of the treatment allocations, meaning no blinding method will be applied to them. However, to maintain objectivity in evaluating the results, the outcome assessors will be blinded to the patient allocation, ensuring they do not know which treatment the participants received. We implement a two-tiered approach to ensure rigorous outcome assessor blinding for subjective measures like appearance satisfaction and scar awareness: (1) Independent assessment teams completely segregated from surgical/clinical staff will conduct evaluations using de-identified digital records (photos and video interviews) with all treatment identifiers removed; (2) Restricted system access through role-based permissions prevents assessors from viewing surgical allocation data or clinical charts, with audit trails monitored weekly. Sample size, centers, and recruitment On the basis of our previous study [ 8 ], the SOI-SMIA group ( n = 39) and the TLCI-LACA group ( n = 89) were evaluated for neck appearance satisfaction at one and six months postoperatively, with satisfaction rates of 97.44% vs. 98.8% and 97.44% vs. 96.63%, respectively. Satisfaction with deep neck sensation and movement was assessed at the same intervals, showing rates of 92.31% vs. 73.03% and 100% vs. 92.13%. By using the larger difference between the two observation points for scoring, the composite endpoint related to the surgical approach showed the greatest difference at one month post-surgery (92.31% vs. 73.03%). A two-tailed test was conducted with a significance level (α) of 0.05, and a sample size ratio of 1:1 between the groups, with β set at 0.2, resulting in a power (1-β) of 80%. The study anticipated a 20% reduction in the incidence of primary endpoints. Considering a potential dropout rate of 10%, it was calculated that each group would require 62 participants. In our study, we plan to recruit 80 patients per group after allowing for a 30% lost follow up. The centers and estimated number of participants are as follows: Nanjing Drum Tower Hospital, Nanjing Central Hospital and Taikang Xianlin Drum Tower Hospital. This study will enroll patients consecutively over time and will conclude once the group with the smaller number of participants reaches 80 cases. As a multicenter study, no single center is allowed to recruit more than 50% of the total participants. Intervention The patients will be divided into one of two groups according to participant selection: Supraclavicular Oblique Incision Sternocleidomastoid Intermuscular Approach Group (SOI-SMIA Group) Once participants choose this group, they will undergo a thyroid lobectomy by SOI-SMIA within 24 h (Fig. 2 A): Position the patient supine with the head turned towards the healthy side. Mark the incision site preoperatively along the lowest skin crease above the clavicle (approximately 1 cm above the clavicle) corresponding to the thyroid lobe that will be excised. Identify the anterior and posterior edges of the sternocleidomastoid muscle’s sternal and clavicular heads. Make an arc-shaped incision of 4–5 cm in length along the skin crease between the muscles, ensuring that the incision does not extend beyond the posterior edge of the sternal head. Incise the skin, subcutaneous tissue, and platysma to free the upper and lower skin flaps. Locate the sternal and clavicular heads above the sternoclavicular joint and gradually dissect upwards to separate the intermuscular space of the sternocleidomastoid muscle. Continue to dissect medially to separate the intermuscular space between the omohyoid and sternothyroid muscles. Use retractors to pull back the dissected tissues, exposing the thyroid lobe to be excised, and proceed with the thyroid lobectomy. All surgeons from participating centers had over a decade of experience in thyroid surgery, with an annual caseload exceeding 500 thyroid surgery, and held senior academic titles (e.g., associate chief physician or higher). Traditional Low Collar Incision Linea Alba Cervicalis Approach Group (TLCI-LACA Group) Participants in this group will undergo a thyroid lobectomy using the TLCI-LACA approach within 24 h of allocation (Fig. 2 B). The procedure involves making a low, arc-shaped incision of 5–10 cm, located 1–2 fingerbreadths above the sternal notch. The skin flaps are then separated, and dissection is performed along the midline (white line) of the neck, passing through the anterior strap muscles to expose the trachea. The anterior neck muscles are retracted to fully expose the surgical area, allowing for the unilateral thyroid lobectomy to be performed. Endpoints Composite primary endpoint The composite index of surgical approach-related trauma (assessed at 1 month after surgery and 6 months after surgery) will be the primary endpoint. It includes both the superficial component and the deep component. The former includes the scar consciousness scale and appearance satisfaction scale from the Patient Scar Assessment Questionnaire (PSAQ) [ 11 ]. The latter refers to the neck impairment index scale [ 12 ]. (supplement 1). Secondary endpoints The secondary endpoints of the LATERAL study will be evaluated at three distinct time points: at the time of hospital discharge, one month after surgery, and six months after surgery. This structured timeline allows for a comprehensive assessment of both immediate and longer-term outcomes associated with the surgical interventions. The following endpoints will be collected at the time of hospital discharge: (1) Duration of surgery; (2) Intraoperative blood loss; (3) Postoperative drainage duration; At 1 month after surgery and 6 months after surgery: (1) Hoarseness; (2) Dysphagia; (3) hypocalcemia; (4) Subcutaneous hydrops; (5) Chylous fistula; (6) Tracheal injury; (7) Esophageal Injury; (8) Hypoparathyroidism; At 6 months after surgery: (1) Situations requiring emergency reoperation; (2) Thyroid cancer recurrence. Data collection and analysis All data will be collected by the primary investigator or nominated investigators (less than two for each participating center) approved by the primary investigator. The study consists of three periods: the screening period before subgrouping (day 0 to day X-1 from admission), the intervention period (day X to day X + 1) and the observational period (discharge, day X + 30, day X + 180). The data required to be collected during different phases are shown below (Table 1 ). We anticipate a loss-to-follow-up rate of 30%, informed by historical attrition trends in similar trials, and have implemented proactive retention strategies (e.g., reminders, telehealth check-ins) to minimize missing data. For intention-to-treat (ITT) analyses, all grouped participants will be included, with missing outcomes addressed via multiple imputation by chained equations (MICE), incorporating baseline covariates and sensitivity analyses to test assumptions. Per-protocol (PP) analyses will exclude major protocol deviations and employ complete-case analysis, with sensitivity checks to assess potential bias. Continuous data will be presented as a mean ± standard deviation or median with an interquartile range. Categorical data will be presented as numbers with percentages. The data will be analyzed by an independent samples t-test, the Wilcoxon rank sum test, a chi-square test, a continuity-corrected chi-square test or Fisher’s exact test. To reduce possible biases, we implemented a rigorous PSM protocol with the following safeguards: (1) Covariates including age, gender, tumor diameter, TNM stage and BMI were selected through causal pathway analysis using directed acyclic graphs (DAGs); (2) A hybrid matching algorithm combining radius matching (0.25 × SD caliper) and 1:2 nearest-neighbor matching was applied; (3) Post-matching balance was confirmed by standardized mean differences &lt; 0.1 across all covariates; (4) Sensitivity analyses including E-value assessment (E = 2.3) demonstrated robustness against potential unmeasured confounding. Patients with similar propensity scores were matched between the two groups, making the distribution of observed baseline covariates appear similar. The study is designed as a superiority trial, so all tests will be two-sided, and P-values &lt; 0.05 will be used to define statistical significance. The analysis will be done using SAS and/or Stata. The primary analyses will be conducted on the intention-to-treat (ITT) population, while secondary supportive analyses will be performed on the per-protocol (PP) population. The safety analysis will be carried out on the safety population. This approach ensures a comprehensive evaluation of the intervention’s efficacy and safety, providing robust and reliable results. Intention-to-Treat (ITT) Population: This population includes all grouped subjects. Per-Protocol (PP) Population: This is a subset of the ITT population, excluding subjects with major protocol deviations. The criteria for major protocol deviations will be detailed in the Statistical Analysis Plan (SAP). Safety Population: This population is identical to the ITT population, comprising all grouped subjects, and is used for assessing the safety profile of the intervention. Adverse events Adverse events (AEs) are defined according to the National Cancer Institute’s Common Terminology Criteria for Adverse Events. They encompass any unfavorable medical occurrence in a patient or clinical trial subject who has received an investigational intervention, regardless of whether a causal relationship with the treatment is established. Dissemination policy The scientific integrity of this project necessitates that data from all centers be analyzed and reported collectively. Therefore, individual centers are not expected to report data solely from their site. Proposed topics for publication will be shared with the principal investigators of all centers. The lead author of a study should be the individual who suggested the topic, and co-authors should be determined in accordance with the authorship guidelines. Discussion As surgical techniques and equipment continue to advance, contemporary surgical practice has evolved from an exclusive focus on tumor resection to an approach that equally values the preservation of function [ 13 ]. Thyroid lobectomy by supraclavicular oblique incision sternocleidomastoid intermuscular approach, while adhering to principles of surgical safety, efficacy, and feasibility, also prioritizes the preservation of tissue function and the aesthetic appearance of the incision. This approach is a potential ideal choice for suitable patients, offering functional preservation of the anterior cervical region during thyroid surgery. Compared to the TLCI-LACA, the SOI-SMIA surgery offers several distinct advantages [ 7 , 8 , 14 ]: 1. SOI-SMIA utilizes the natural intermuscular spaces of the neck to create a working cavity, thereby maximizing the preservation of the muscular and structural integrity of the anterior cervical region.2. SOI-SMIA obviates the necessity of incising the linea alba cervical, minimizing extensive dissection of the anterior cervical muscles and their fascial compartments. This reduction in tissue manipulation potentially decreases the incidence of postoperative adhesions within the anterior cervical region. 3. The supraclavicular region is characterized by minimal skin tension, a homogeneous distribution of subcutaneous tissue, and the lack of a substantial fat pad. These anatomical features facilitate more efficient postoperative wound healing and substantially lower the likelihood of prominent scar formation, making any resultant scars significantly less noticeable.4. SOI-SMIA initiates from the posterolateral capsule of the thyroid gland, prioritizing the early identification and preservation of critical neural structures. Specifically, the recurrent laryngeal nerve is located and safeguarded at the outset, followed by the preservation of the superior parathyroid gland in situ. Additionally, the external branch of the superior laryngeal nerve is identified prior to thyroid resection. This sequential approach enhances the accuracy of nerve identification and the conservation of parathyroid function. 5. SOI can be accessed by the natural anatomical gap between neck muscles without severing the anterior cervical band; Similarly, the SMIA technique has a relatively low dependence on specialized surgical instruments, providing a clear surgical field under direct vision and facilitating relatively straightforward operation [ 15 , 16 ]. It ensures the safe resection of tumors and comprehensive dissection of regional lymph nodes while meeting the requirements for functional preservation of the anterior cervical region. This makes it suitable for implementation and promotion across hospitals at all levels. As SOI-SMIA becomes more widespread and developed, it is necessary to study its role in comparison with TLCI-LACA in thyroid lobectomy. We have commenced a multicenter prospective cohort study (LATERAL study) for papillary thyroid carcinoma patients needing thyroid lobectomy in China that compares SOI-SMIA and TLCI-LACA and will be known as LATERAL. The results of this prospective investigation will confirm the role of SOI-SMIA in thyroid lobectomy. Supplementary Information Supplementary Material 1. Acknowledgements None.
