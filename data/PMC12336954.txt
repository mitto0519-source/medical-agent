ABSTRACT Aim Extremely low‐birthweight (ELBW) infants face an increased risk of physical, cognitive, and psychosocial challenges affecting health‐related quality of life (HRQoL). However, few studies have examined their long‐term prognosis in Japan. We aimed to assess the HRQoL of adolescents and young adults born with ELBW and identified associated factors. Methods A multicenter cross‐sectional study was conducted in Japan between January and March 2019. Adolescents and young adults born with ELBW completed the Japanese version of the Short Form Health Survey‐8 and provided sociodemographic and health‐related data. Results Among 289 eligible participants, 169 (58.5%) returned the HRQoL questionnaire (129 self‐respondents and 40 proxy respondents). Proxy respondents had lower birth weights and higher rates of motor and neurodevelopmental impairment. Among self‐respondents, moderate‐to‐severe attention deficit hyperactivity disorder (ADHD) was associated with low physical ( β = −0.36, 95% CI −0.54 to −0.18, p &lt; 0.001) and mental ( β = −0.29, 95% CI −0.48 to −0.10, p = 0.003) component summary scores, while motor impairment was associated with decreased physical component summary score ( β = −0.23, 95% CI −0.40 to −0.05, p = 0.012). Conclusions ADHD and motor impairment are associated with low HRQoL. Long‐term evaluations and tailored support strategies are essential for this population. Table 2 shows that participants with motor impairment exhibited significantly lower SF‐8 scores than those without. Specifically, the mean differences were −8.85 points for physical functioning (95% CI, −11.92 to −5.77; p &lt; 0.001), −5.65 points for role physical (95% CI, −9.28 to −2.01; p = 0.003), −6.93 points for Social Functioning (95% CI, −12.80 to −1.07; p = 0.021), and −5.65 points for the physical component summary (95% CI, −10.35 to −0.95; p = 0.019). Figure 2 and Table S10 show the results of multiple regression analyses between complications and sequelae, the SF‐8 domain, and summary scores. We evaluated the effects of motor impairment, moderate‐to‐severe ADHD, and neurodevelopmental disorder on SF‐8 scores at the time of the survey, adjusted for gestational age, birth weight, sex, and neonatal factors such as sepsis, PVL, IVH, and tracheostomy, during NICU hospitalisation. Motor impairment was associated with low physical functioning, role physical, bodily pain, and physical component summary scores. Moderate‐to‐severe ADHD significantly lowered all eight domains as well as both physical and mental component summary scores. 4 Discussion This study evaluated HRQoL in adolescents and young adults born with ELBW who were considered not to have severe neurodevelopmental impairment and investigated late sequelae and complications. The HRQoL scores of the 129 participants who completed the questionnaire were generally good. However, the HRQoL scores of individuals with moderate‐to‐severe ADHD were significantly lower in both physical and mental domains. Additionally, motor impairment significantly reduced physical HRQoL scores. There have been reports on the HRQoL of adolescents and young adults with EP/VP and ELBW/VLBW, and their HRQoL scores were comparable to those of full‐term controls [ 10 , 11 , 15 , 16 ]. Neurodevelopmental impairment thus increases the risk of poor HRQoL [ 17 , 18 , 19 ]. ELBW or VLBW infants have a high rate of cognitive dysfunction, anxiety, and attention deficits [ 15 , 16 ], and these psychological factors and cognitive dysfunction can affect HRQoL. The HRQoL scores of ELBW infants are lower than those of full‐term controls [ 18 , 19 ]. The HRQoL score may differ depending on the complications and sequelae of the surveyed participants [ 10 , 19 ]. There are few reports on the factors that affect HRQoL in ELBW infants with relatively good prognoses and few complications. This study indicated that moderate‐to‐severe ADHD significantly reduced both physical and mental QoL scores. A previous report indicated that ADHD reduced HRQoL at age 12 years in VPs born before 30 weeks [ 20 ]. The risk of ADHD in ELBW infants is approximately four times higher than in full‐term infants [ 21 , 22 ]. The ADHD symptoms observed in ELBW are predominantly inattentive [ 22 , 23 ], and there is little difference between the sexes compared to the general population. Some children with ADHD tendencies do not meet the diagnostic criteria [ 23 ]. The neurocognitive disadvantages of VLBW infants are likely to remain unchanged from childhood to adulthood [ 22 ], and the risk of ADHD remains high even in adulthood [ 22 , 23 , 24 ]. The severity of ADHD symptoms in adulthood was not significantly different from that in general ADHD cases [ 24 ]. ADHD in EPs is less comorbid with conduct disorders, which are frequently observed in the general population [ 23 ]. A decline in HRQoL in ADHD cases has been reported, which both lowers mental and physical HRQoL [ 25 , 26 ]. This study indicated that ADHD requiring medication in ELBW infants has a substantial impact on both physical and mental HRQoL. Risk factors for the persistence of ADHD symptoms into adulthood include family history, psychosocial adversity, and comorbidities. Environmental factors, such as family and school involvement, are closely related to the persistence of symptoms [ 27 ]. The inattention common in VLBW/ELBW infants may have a long‐term impact on their HRQoL, as the decline in symptoms with age is gradual [ 28 ]. Since ELBW infants are at high risk of developing ADHD, long‐term follow‐up is important to provide appropriate intervention at the appropriate time. Consistent with previous reports, patients with motor impairment had significantly lower physical HRQoL scores [ 17 , 18 , 19 ]. Motor impairment did not affect mental HRQoL scores. Because ELBWs with physical disadvantages can adapt well to society and their environment individually, they may not have complained about their mental burden [ 17 ]. In contrast, ADHD reduced both mental and physical HRQoL. Several studies have reported that ADHD is associated with poor physical health [ 25 , 26 , 27 ]. ADHD is associated with a high risk of sleep disorders, dental caries, being overweight or obese, accidents, and injuries. It is also associated with a high risk of complications associated with other diseases, such as lower urinary tract symptoms and asthma [ 24 ]. 5 Limitations This study had some limitations. First, the response rate was not high, at around 60%. Although there were no significant differences between the groups of respondents and non‐respondents when comparing background factors, it is possible that responses were not obtained from cases in which it was difficult to complete the questionnaire, such as individuals with a poor prognosis. Although it is conceivable that some respondents with motor impairment or neurodevelopmental disorders may have received assistance from parents or caregivers in completing the questionnaire, it is important to note that these individuals were either enrolled in high schools, including specialised schools for students with disabilities, in higher education, or were employed. Therefore, it is reasonable to assume that they possessed the necessary functional capacity to complete the questionnaire independently. Our findings suggest that ADHD significantly affects HRQoL in ELBW survivors. However, the limited number of ADHD cases in our study prevents us from investigating risk factors for ADHD and generalising these results to the broader population of VLBW/ELBW individuals with ADHD. More extensive longitudinal studies are needed to confirm these associations. Second, there was no comparison group of full‐term infants. Since ELBW infants are concentrated in tertiary facilities, the proportion of full‐term infants born in tertiary facilities without morbidities is low, and it was not possible to obtain a control group of full‐term infants with similar growth environments. The HRQoL scores were converted to values based on national standard values so that comparisons could be made for each domain of the SF‐8. As the average value for the national population was converted to 50, the results obtained were not significantly different from the Japanese national standard values [ 12 ], and it was thought that in many cases, the HRQoL scores were similar to those of full‐term infants. Third, this study collected participants who could answer the HRQoL scale themselves, which means that the long‐term prognosis was relatively good for this group. Therefore, the results cannot be generalised to all ELBW cases. Reporting bias could have occurred since this study was a patient‐reported survey. The prevalence of ADHD may have been underestimated because the ADHD group was limited to participants who received pharmacotherapy. No information on the association between maternal smoking and ADHD was obtained. 6 Conclusions The self‐reported HRQoL of adolescents and young adults born with ELBW in Japan was generally favourable. However, individuals with ADHD may experience significantly lower physical and mental HRQoL. It is essential to monitor physical health and neurodevelopmental disorders, especially ADHD, in adolescents or young adults born with ELBW over the long term and to provide timely and tailored support for their needs.
