A BSTRACT Background: Internet gaming disorder (IGD) or video gaming has been considered by World Health Organization (WHO) and American Psychiatric Association (APA) as a disease. WHO defines IGD as a disease with a pattern of uncontrollable gaming behavior, characterized by increasing priority given to gaming over other activities to the point that gaming takes precedence over most interests and activities of daily living. Objectives: This research aims to estimate the prevalence and examine the association of IGD scores with gender, age, sleeping hours, academic performance, and occurrence of arguing with others post-gaming among college students. Methods: A cross-sectional study was conducted in October 2022 at (IMSIU), Riyadh, Saudi Arabia. A total of 1005 college students filled out an online self-administered questionnaire developed based on an extensive literature review and pilot study. Results: More than two-thirds (76.1%) of students are active gamers. The majority (65.8%) reported no effects of gaming on daily activities. yet, one-third considered games as the most daily time-consuming activity. The majority are playing games to relieve stress. Interestingly, male and senior students had the highest IGD scores ( P = 0.017, P = 0.072, respectively). Unfortunately, there were associations between high IGD scores and having fewer sleeping hours as well as low academic performance ( P = 0.046, P = 0.047, respectively). Negative social effects include arguing after gaming was significantly linked with higher IGD scores ( P &lt; 0.001). Conclusion: IGD was prevalent among college students. There was no impact of games on students’ daily activities; however, gaming was the most time-consuming part of daily activity for the remaining students. Most college students were playing games to feel better. Higher IGD score was associated with male, senior students, less sleeping hours, poor academic performance, and post-gaming arguments with others. Introduction According to the World Health Organization (WHO), internet gaming disorder (IGD) is described in the 11 th Revision of the International Classification of Diseases (ICD-11), which was published in the middle of 2018, as a pattern of gaming behavior also known as digital or video gaming. It is characterized by uncontrollability over gaming, increasing priority given to gaming over other activities to the point that gaming takes precedence over most interests and activities of daily living, and continuation or escalation of gaming despite the occurrence of any negative effects.[ 1 ] In the most recent fifth iteration of the Diagnostic and Statistical Manual of Mental Disorders, the American Psychiatric Association (APA) recognized IGD (Internet Gaming Disorder) as a transient disease (DSM-5) (American Psychiatric Association, 2013).[ 2 ] Recent scientific studies have started to pay attention to how some people become fixated on particular elements of the Internet, particularly online gaming. To the exclusion of all other interests, the “gamers” engage in compulsive gaming, and their persistent online engagement leads to clinically substantial impairment or suffering. Because they spend so much time playing, individuals with this condition endanger their ability to do well in school or at work. When prevented from playing video games, they exhibit withdrawal symptoms.[ 3 ] A behavior pattern that would typically have been present for at least a year is required for the diagnosis of a gaming disorder. It must be severe enough to significantly affect personal, familial, social, educational, occupational, or other critical areas of functioning.[ 1 ] A study shows that the Philippines is the highest country with a percentage of 96.4% of internet users who play video games on any device, whereas Saudi Arabia comes as the 8 th country with a percentage of 91.4%.[ 4 ] There are multiple studies on IGD conducted in Saudi Arabia. In Riyadh, a study conducted at King Saud University in 2020, among medical students the sample was 228, and 8.8% of them had IGD.[ 5 ] Also, in southern Saudi Arabia published a study among adolescents, the sample was 450 students, and 29.3% had IGD.[ 6 ] In Eastern Saudi Arabia too, a study conducted in Dammam among high school students, the sample was 726 students, and the prevalence was 21.85%.[ 7 ] Our study aims to estimate the prevalence of IGD factors among college students at Riyadh’s Imam Mohammed Ibn Saud Islamic University. Furthermore, to determine the association of IGD score with such factors as gender, age, sleeping hours, academic performance, and occurrence of arguing with others post-gaming. Materials and Methods Study design and tasks A cross-sectional study in October 2022 at Imam Mohammad Ibn Saud Islamic University (IMSIU) including the humanities, sciences, and medicine colleges. Participant and sampling Data collection was initiated after securing ethical approval from the Institutional Review Board of Imam Mohammad ibn Saud Islamic University (registration number 0327/2022, dated March 2022). The population of this study is college students of IMSIU majoring in medicine, science, and humanitarian streams. Students studying in colleges other than IMSIU, students under 18, and students who have already graduated from IMSIU staff were excluded from this study. Written informed consent was obtained from all participants included in the study. The number of registered undergraduate students pursuing a bachelor’s degree in these majors was 7393 for both genders (3875 were males and 3518 were females). Using calculator.net, the minimal sample size of responses was calculated to be at least 366 participants to reach a confidence level of 95% and a margin of error of 5%. Study measurement Data were collected using an online self-administered questionnaire for all participants who met the criteria. However, before data collection began, a pilot study involving 30 adolescents who were not included in the survey was conducted to assess whether the questions were clear and could be understood by the students, as well as the time it took to complete the questionnaire. The questionnaire has been sent by the Information Technology deanship to all student’s university e-mails. To preserve the participants’ privacy and to reach both male and female students as well as those who might not have been in college when the data was being collected, we sent the questionnaire via electronic means using Google Forms. The questionnaire was divided into four sections, and an informed consent agreement was on the first page, Also, A yes or no question (Do you play video games?) was there on the first page, so those who do not play were requested to not complete the questionnaire, that’s because we want to reach the more reliable results conducted from real video games players. The first section included demographic questions, such as participant’s age, gender, social status, college, university year, GPA, and the average sleep hours in the last month. The second section had an Internet Gaming Disorder Behaviors scale with six items has been shortened from a validated Arabic translation of the IGD 9-Item Short scale,[ 5 ] It consists of six Yes-or-No questions conducting general information about gaming behaviors. The third section had an Internet Gaming Disorder questionnaire with seven items that were shorted from a validated Arabic translation of the IGD 20-Item Short scale.[ 8 ] The Internet Game Disorder scale was computed by adding up the respondents’ perceptions to the seven indicators yielding a score bound between 7 and 35 points. The shortening of these questionnaires was conducted via content validity analysis with a panel of experts. Finally, General information has been concocted from participants regarding their video games originally taken from a validated Arabic translation of IGDS9-SF.[ 7 ] Statistical data analysis Data analysis was performed via the Statistical Package for the Social Sciences (SPSS) software. Descriptive statistics were used to summarize the study findings, and frequencies and percentages were used to summarize binary and categorical data. Descriptive analysis with the mean and standard deviation was applied to continuous variables and the frequencies and percentages were used to describe the categorically measured variables. The statistical Normality assumption was tested using the Histogram and the Kolmogorov–Smirnov statistical test of normality. The Cronbach’s alpha test of internal consistency was used to assess the reliability of the measured questionnaire (seven-item IGD scale). The multiple response dichotomies analysis was used to describe the variables measured with more than option answers. The Internet Game Disorder scale was computed by adding up the respondents’ perceptions to the seven indicators yielding a score bound between 7 and 35 points; however, one negatively worded item (statement #5) required reverse coding before computing the IGD score. The Multivariable Binary Logistic Regression Analysis was applied to three students’ measured main outcomes (poor sleep &lt;6 hours/day, poor academic performance GPA &lt;4 points, and their odds of experiencing arguments with others about internet gaming), the association between predictor variables with these dichotomized outcomes was expressed as a multivariate-adjusted Odds Ratios (OR) with their associated 95% Confidence Intervals (CI’s). Also, the Multivariable Linear Regression Analysis was applied to the University student’s internet gaming disorder (IGD) score, the association between the predictor variables with the student’s perceived internet gaming disorder score was expressed as unstandardized beta coefficients (B) with their associated 95% confidence intervals. The SPSS IBM v21 Statistical Data analysis program was used, and the alpha significance level was considered at 0.50 Level. Reliability analysis The reliability analysis of the internet gaming disorder study questionnaire is shown in Table 1 . The study measured a tailored subset (seven indicators) of the original Internet Gaming Disorder IGD-20 questionnaire, these seven indicators were selected based on content validity discussion with a panel of experts. The Cronbach’s alpha test of internal consistency showed that the seven-indicator IGD subscale questionnaire was internally consistent indicating that the students had read these indicators nearly equally reliably, Cronbach’s alpha = 0.79. Also, Kuder–Richardson’s (KR-20) was applied to the shortened IGD-9 questionnaire measured with binary dichotomized questions (six out of the original nine indicators), and the resulting analysis findings showed low internal consistency between these dichotomized, KR-20 = 0.61. Predictors of Internet Gaming Disorder score To understand better what may explain why the students perceived less or more internet gaming disorder (IGD score), the multivariable linear regression analysis was applied to regress the students’ mean perceived IGD score against their sociodemographic characteristics, academic performance, and studying conditions plus their internet gaming practices and habits as well as preferences. The yielded analysis findings, Table 5 , showed that male students perceived significantly higher mean Internet gaming scores compared to female students, beta coefficient = 0.731, P value = 0.017, accounting for the other predictor variables in the analysis model. But the student’s college, academic performance, and daily sleep hours did not correlate significantly with their mean perceived Internet gaming disorder score, also the senior students perceived a slightly higher mean perceived Internet gaming disorder score compared to the other students; however, the difference between senior and other students on their IGD score was not statistically significant, P value = 0.072. However, the multivariate analysis findings showed that the students who think constantly of playing games perceived significantly higher mean perceived internet gaming disorder scores compared to the students who do not think constantly of the next time they can play games on average, beta coefficient = 1.572, P value &lt; 0.001. Too, the students who advised that they play games to avoid thinking of annoying matters measured significantly higher mean perceived Internet gaming disorder scores compared to those who did not advise that they avoid annoying matters by playing games on average, beta coefficient = 1.63, P value &lt; 0.001. Moreover, the students who argue with their peers regarding the consequence of playing games measured significantly higher mean perceived Internet gaming disorder scores compared to those who do not indulge in such arguments on average, beta coefficient = 1.935, P value &lt; 0.001. Also, the analysis findings showed that the students who tended to hide their time spent playing games from others measured significantly higher mean perceived Internet gaming disorder scores compared to those who did not hide their play hours, beta coefficient = 1.989, P value &lt; 0.001. The students who experienced the loss of interest in hobbies measured significantly higher mean perceived Internet gaming disorder scores compared to those who did not lose interest in hobbies, beta coefficient = 3.28, P value &lt; 0.001. Surprisingly, the students who used their laptops for their Internet games measured significantly lower mean perceived significantly lower mean Internet gaming disorder scores compared to those who did not use the laptops, beta coefficient= -1.186, P value = 0.021, and the students who preferred Open-World games also measured significantly lower mean perceived Internet gaming disorder score compared to those did not play this game, P value = 0.002. Moreover, the students who spent &gt;50 Saudi Riyals per month or more measured significantly higher mean perceived Internet gaming disorder scores compared to those who spend &lt;50 SAR per month on games or those who do not spend money on games at all, beta coefficient = 0.509, P value &lt; 0.001. Also, the students who spend ≥2 hours playing videos per day measured significantly higher mean perceived Internet gaming disorder scores compared to those who play games for less than two hours per day on average, beta coefficient = 1.076, P value &lt; 0.001. The students’ other measured factors and practices did not converge significantly on their mean perceived Internet gaming disorder score as such were dismissed from the above analysis model. Predictors of students’ having fewer sleeping hours Also, as a secondary analysis, the students’ odds of having slept less than six hours per day were considered as an outcome in the Multivariable Binary Logistic Regression Analysis to get a better insight on the impact of internet gaming disorder score along with other predictor variables on student’s odds of having fewer sleeping hours. The yielded findings, shown in Table 6 , suggested that the student’s sex and age as well as college did not correlate significantly with their odds of having had fewer sleeping hours, P value &gt;0.050 each respectively. However, the yielded findings showed that Junior students measured significantly higher odds (1.517 times more or 51.7% times higher odds) of having fewer sleeping hours compared to students in later study levels on average, P value = 0.045. Furthermore, the multivariable analysis findings showed that the students who played games to avoid thinking of annoying things were found to be significantly more predicted (1.75 times higher or 75% times more) to have fewer sleeping hours compared to the other students who do not play games to avoid annoying business/thoughts on average, P value = 0.004. Not only so but also the students’ mean perceived Internet gaming disorder score (IGD score) correlated significantly and positively with the student’s odds of having fewer sleeping hours, for each additional points rise in the students’ mean IGD score their odds of having less sleeping hours (i.e., having &lt; 6 hrs of sleep per day) tended rise by a factor equal to 3.2% times higher, P value = 0.046. Also, the analysis findings showed that the students who played videos alone (solo) were found to be significantly more predicted (1.662 times more) to have fewer sleeping hours compared to students who play with peers on the same device or those who play with their peers online on average, P value = 0.006. Predictors of students poorer academic performance (GPA &lt;4 points out of 5) In addition, the students’ odds of having poorer academic performance (a GPA below 4/5) were considered in the multivariate binary logistic regression analysis. The yielded findings, Table 7 , showed that male students were found to be significantly more predicted to have poorer academic performance (1.461 times more) compared to female students on average, P value = 0.020. Also, the findings from the analysis model showed that students’ age did not correlate significantly with their odds of poorer academic performance. However, the students’ mean perceived Internet gaming disorder (IGD) score had correlated significantly and positively with their odds of having poorer academic performance, as the students’ mean perceived Internet gaming disorder score tended to rise by one point on average their odds of having poorer academic performance tended to rise by a factor equal to 1.032 times more, or 3.2% times higher, on average, P value = 0.047. The students’ constant thinking of their next video game time did not correlate significantly with their odds of poor academic performance, P value = 0.069. However, the students who played games to avoid thinking of annoying things were found to be significantly more inclined (1.641 times more) to poor academic performance compared to those who did not play games to avoid annoying matters, P value = 0.002. The students who spent &gt;200 SAR/week on games were found to be significantly less predicted (12.9% times less) to have poor academic performance compared to those who spent &lt;200 SAR/week on games, P value = 0.033. The students’ age of starting video games did not correlate significantly with their odds of poorer academic performance, P value = 0.229. But the students who preferred open-world games were found to be significantly more predicted (1.397 times more) to have poorer academic performance compared to those who do Not prefer this game in general, P value = 0.037. The students’ other measured predictor-independent variables did not correlate significantly with their odds of having a &lt;4 GPA score at their university. Predictors of students post-gaming arguments In addition, the student’s tendency to argue with others post-gaming was analyzed as a step further to understand the students’ immersed behaviors in video/internet games. Table 8 displays the resulting findings from the Multivariable Logistic Binary Regression analysis of students’ odds of arguing with others post-gaming. The yielded findings showed that the student’s sex did not correlate significantly with their odds of arguing post-gaming. P value = 0.562. However, the analysis findings showed that students aged &gt;=23 years were found to be significantly less predicted (27.6% times less) for arguing post-game compared to those aged ≤22 years on average, P value = 0.046. Also, the analysis findings showed that science college students may not differ significantly from medical school students with respect to their inclination to argue post-gaming, but the students of the colleges of human sciences were found to be significantly more predicted (2.704 times more) to argue about games compared to medical students on average, P value = 0.003, also economy and business college students were approximately twice as more predicted to argue about game consequences with others compared to medical students, P value = 0.030. The students’ mean perceived Internet Gaming Disorder score had correlated significantly and positively with their odds of arguing with others post-gaming, as the students’ mean perceived IGD score tended to rise by one point on average their odds of augmenting tended to rise by a factor equal to 14.3% times higher on average too, P value &lt; 0.001, note the scatter plot in Figure 3 . Nevertheless, the students who hide their gaming time from others measured significantly higher odds (almost twofold: 1.941 times more) of augmenting game results with others compared to those who do not hide their gaming time on average, P value = 0.002. On another side, the multivariate analysis findings suggested that the students who spend money on games &gt;50 SAR/week were found to be significantly more predicted to argue about games with others (1.165 times more) compared to those who spend ≤50 Saudi Riyals per week on games on average, P value = 0.039. Moreover, the yielded analysis findings suggested that the students who preferred Fight and Wrestling games were found to be significantly more predicted (1.623 times more) to arguments post-gaming compared to students who preferred other games, P value = 0.015. However, the students who preferred horror games were found to be significantly less inclined to arguments compared to students who did not prefer this type of game, P value = 0.014. Although the students who played games online with their friends measured higher odds of argument about games with other persons compared to other students who played as solo players or with their friends using one device, the association between students’ online use of games with odds of argument was not statistically significant, P value = 0.052. Discussion We conducted this investigation, a cross-sectional study to estimate the prevalence of Internet Gaming disorder factors (IGD) among college students at Imam Mohammed Ibn Saud Islamic University, Riyadh, Saudi Arabia, and to determine the association of IGD with gender, age, academic achievement, amount of sleep, and arguing with other post gaming. A total of 765 out of 1005 of the University students played video games and completed and returned the online survey and subsequent analysis. This study has shown significant results that estimate the prevalence of IGD factors among students. A total of 65.8% reported no effects of gaming on daily activities. Yet, 30.1% consider games as the most daily time-consuming activity. A total of 66.8% are playing games to relieve stress. Similarly, a study of college students at Mansoura University in Egypt showed that 6% of the students were classified as having IGD.[ 9 ] Moreover, A study conducted on medical students at King Saud University in Riyadh, Saudi Arabia, showed that IGD prevalence was 8.8%.[ 5 ] Equally, A study in India on undergraduate medical and dentistry students estimated the prevalence of IGD to be 9% among their sample.[ 10 ] On the other hand, a 2015 study in Germany involving students aged 13–18 found the 12-month prevalence rate of IGD to be 1.16%, which is lower than estimated in the other studies.[ 11 ] A systemic review of 53 studies estimated the worldwide prevalence of IGD to be 3.05% and stated that the prevalence rates seemed to be elevated due to variations in the methodological characteristics and issues in measurement and sampling techniques.[ 12 ] In this study, males were associated with a higher IGD score ( P = 0.017). Likewise, this association also occurred in other results from other studies in China and Norway have a positive association between being male and having a higher risk of IGD.[ 13 , 14 ] Furthermore, A study in India found that the prevalence of IGD was significantly higher among males than females (15.3%, 3.5%, respectively).[ 15 ] A systematic review published in 2018 by Paulus et al .[ 16 ] also concluded that IGD may be observed up to five times more often in male children, except for only two studies that did not show any significant relationship. Moreover, A Mexican study observed five university students found that males had a higher IGD risk than females.[ 17 ] The study conducted at King Saud University in Riyadh also showed a significant association ( P = 0.0040) between IGD and gender.[ 5 ] This study didn’t find a significant correlation between age and higher IGD scores ( P = 0.072). However, this study found that most of the students who had IGD were senior students. Similar to our study, a study conducted in Slovenia did not identify age as a key predictor of IGD.[ 18 ] A survey was conducted among 524 high school students in Lebanon to explore the relationships between IGD, age, gender, sleep habits, and academic achievement in Lebanese adolescents. The results of the survey indicated that there was no significant correlation between age and IGD. This suggests that the likelihood of developing IGD does not increase or decrease with age among high school students in Lebanon.[ 19 ] The systematic review by Paulus et al .[ 16 ] also stated that the influence of age on IGD prevalence is not clear. It also concluded that older adolescents have the highest probability of becoming game addicts, which was a similar finding in our study. Interestingly, the study conducted on medical students at King Saud University also found no significant association between age and IGD ( P = 0.008), which is similar and very close to the findings of this study.[ 5 ] Interestingly, sleeping fewer hours is associated with higher IGD scores in our study ( P = 0.046). A similar association was found in the 2015 study held in Germany involving students aged 13–18 who reported more sleep-related disorders in comparison to their normal counterparts.[ 11 ] Contrary to our finding, the study conducted on medical students mentioned previously revealed no significant association between sleep and IGD ( P = 0.301). It is likely that, due to an increased mean age, there is a lack of significant association.[ 17 ] In a similar study conducted in India to determine the role of sleep quality on the relationship between IGD and perceived stress and suicidal behavior, it was found that those students who scored higher IGD scores had poor sleep quality, ultimately contributing to perceived stress and suicidal behavior.[ 20 ] Similar to our study, the study conducted on Lebanese students also concluded that less sleep duration was associated with developing IGD, with sleep disturbance being the biggest contributor to IGD.[ 19 ] Unfortunately, it was found that students with higher (IGD) scores were associated with having poor academic performance ( P = 0.047). In another study conducted in 2020 on Saudi adolescents, it was found that those participants who had IGD were far less likely to receive excellent academic grades than those who were not addicted.[ 21 ] Similar to our study, a positive correlation was found in the study conducted on Lebanese students and the systematic review by Paulus et al .[ 16 , 19 ] In contrast to our study, the study conducted on medical students at King Saud University in Riyadh found no significant association between IGD and academic performance.[ 5 ] Negative social effects after gaming Including arguing with others post-gaming are significantly linked with higher IGD scores ( P value &lt; 0.001). A systematic review, which included all publications included in PubMed and PsychINFO up until May 2016, reported that time spent on games increased levels of physical aggression regardless of whether the content was violent and found higher levels of depression, anxiety, and social phobia in patients with IGD.[ 22 ] In another interesting study published in 2021, conducted on 1032 gamers, it was evaluated that gamers with higher IGD risk present with lower social engagement signs.[ 23 ] In a 2018 study that involved clinical interviews with 31 male adolescents diagnosed with IGD, it was also found that participants had high scores on scales assessing depression and anxiety. However, there was the presence of several other personality traits as well, which were found to be highly associated with IGD, including introversion, inhibition, submissiveness, self-devaluation, interpersonal sensibility, obsessive-compulsive tendencies, phobic anxiety, hostility, as well as paranoid and borderline personality traits.[ 24 ] Time spent playing was positively correlated with a higher mean perceived IGD score ( P value &lt; 0.001). In line with our findings, a 2020 study conducted in southern Brazil also concluded that increased gaming time per day (in hours) was established as the strongest predictor for IGD.[ 25 ] Furthermore, many existing Indian studies have also established a significant link between IGD severity and time spent gaming.[ 26 , 27 , 28 , 29 ] Similar findings were reported in the systematic review, which included all publications up until May 2016.[ 22 ] This study is limited by its design, which was cross-sectional using an online self-administered questionnaire. Longitudinal studies may reveal different findings. Since this design is dependent on subjective participant recall and self-administering ability there was a possibility for recall bias. Furthermore, the sample size was (1005) participants which is considered somewhat small. Also, it was limited to Imam Mohammed Ibn Saud University students, Thus, findings cannot be generalized all over Riyadh City University students. Additionally, we were not able to find the exact prevalence of IGD due to the use of our questionnaire IGD-7 items which focused on specific IGD factors. Questionnaire factors and items have been built depending on three validated questionnaires that assessed the prevalence of IGD.[ 5 , 7 , 8 ] Our research findings emphasize that there are some IGD factors prevalent among Imam Mohammed Ibn Saud University students. We recommend further research on Riyadh college students to investigate IGD prevalence among them. We advise university students’ gamers to maintain a strict timetable that does not conflict with their responsibilities. Furthermore, Physical activities should be sought out, such as football, walks, and gym workouts. Conclusion This study concluded that students have a significant prevalence of IGD factors. There is no impact of games on daily activities in most of them, however, gaming is the most time-consuming part of daily activity for the remaining students. Furthermore, most of them are playing games to feel better. Higher IGD score is associated with male, senior students, less sleeping hours, poor academic performance, and post-gaming arguments with others. Researchers recommend further research on Riyadh college students to investigate IGD prevalence among them.
