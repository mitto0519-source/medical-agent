Abstract Background Sarcopenia, characterized by a progressive decline in muscle mass, strength, and function, is primarily attributable to aging. DNA methylation, influenced by both genetic predispositions and environmental exposures, plays a significant role in sarcopenia occurrence. This study employed machine learning (ML) methods to identify differentially methylated probes (DMPs) capable of diagnosing sarcopenia in middle-aged individuals. We also investigated the relationship between muscle strength, muscle mass, age, and sarcopenia risk as reflected in methylation profiles. Methods Data from 509 male participants in the urban cohort of the Korean Genome Epidemiology Study_Health Examinee study were categorized into quartile groups based on the sarcopenia criteria for appendicular skeletal muscle index (ASMI) and handgrip strength (HG). To identify diagnostic biomarkers for sarcopenia, we used recursive feature elimination with cross validation (RFECV), to pinpoint DMPs significantly associated with sarcopenia. An ensemble model, leveraging majority voting, was utilized for evaluation. Furthermore, a methylation risk score (MRS) was calculated, and its correlation with muscle strength, function, and age was assessed using likelihood ratio analysis and multinomial logistic regression. Results Participants were classified into two groups based on quartile thresholds: sarcopenia (n = 37) with ASMI and HG in the lowest quartile, and normal ranges (n = 48) in the highest. In total, 238 DMPs were identified and eight probes were selected using RFECV. These DMPs were used to build an ensemble model with robust diagnostic capabilities for sarcopenia, as evidenced by an area under the receiver operating characteristic curve of 0.94. Based on eight probes, the MRS was calculated and then validated by analyzing age, HG, and ASMI among the control group (n = 424). Age was positively correlated with high MRS (coefficient, 1.2494; odds ratio [OR], 3.4882), whereas ASMI and HG were negatively correlated with high MRS (ASMI coefficient, −0.4275; OR, 0.6521; HG coefficient, −0.3116; OR, 0.7323). Conclusion Overall, this study identified key epigenetic markers of sarcopenia in Korean males and developed a ML model with high diagnostic accuracy for sarcopenia. The MRS also revealed significant correlations between these markers and age, HG, and ASMI. These findings suggest that both diagnostic models and the MRS can play an important role in managing sarcopenia in middle-aged populations. INTRODUCTION Sarcopenia is a progressive condition marked by the loss of muscle mass and strength, predominantly associated with aging. This deterioration significantly impacts quality of life and increases mortality risk. 1 Although commonly perceived as an inevitable part of aging, recent findings suggest that sarcopenia is preventable and manageable through proactive lifestyle interventions even during middle age. 2 , 3 Research highlights a higher prevalence of sarcopenia among males 4 , 5 , 6 compared to that among females, which is attributed to factors such as the greater initial muscle mass in male, which declines more sharply because of hormonal changes such as testosterone levels. 7 Additionally, differences in lifestyle habits and genetic factors between sexes further exacerbate the risk, indicating the necessity of sex-specific strategies in the prevention and management of sarcopenia. Epigenetic modifications, such as DNA methylation, 8 which affect gene expression that is crucial for muscle function without altering the DNA sequence, play a significant role in sarcopenia progression. These modifications, especially abnormal DNA methylation patterns, have been identified as potential biomarkers for sarcopenia, 9 paving the way for early diagnosis and targeted treatment approaches. Machine learning (ML), a branch of artificial intelligence that uses data-driven algorithms to identify patterns and improve predictions, 10 has shown promise in diagnosing and forecasting sarcopenia. Although ML has been successfully applied in sarcopenia diagnosis using computed tomography image analysis 11 and clinical characteristics, 12 its use in detecting sarcopenia through DNA methylation markers is still an emerging field. Although ML has been applied to DNA methylation data from other diseases to build classifiers for prognosis prediction, 13 to our knowledge, no studies have yet explored its application for sarcopenia sing DNA methylation data based on Korean populations. Hence, the aim of this study was to identify potential biomarkers for the diagnosis of sarcopenia in middle-aged Koreans, using data from the Korean Genome Epidemiology Survey (KoGES)_Health Examinee (HEXA) study. 14 We sought to develop an ML-based model for the early diagnosis of male sarcopenia using the identified genomic information and establish a methylation risk score (MRS) for evaluating sarcopenia risk. METHODS Participants and datasets This research draws on epidemiological and epigenomic data from an urban cohort of the KoGES_HEXA study, 14 a comprehensive investigation into the genetic and environmental causes of prevalent diseases among Koreans. Conducted during 2004–2013, KoGES_HEXA data is a significant endeavor documented by the Korea Center for Disease Control and Prevention. It recruited participants aged 40–69 years from urban centers in South Korea through a hospital-based registry, ensuring that each participant provided written informed consent. The subset of interest for this study included individuals (n = 822) for whom DNA methylation data were available. Diagnostic criteria of sarcopenia Participants were categorized based on the appendicular skeletal muscle index (ASMI) and handgrip strength (HG), following the variables of the Asian working group for sarcopenia. 15 The appendicular skeletal muscle (ASM) was derived using the following formula 16 : ASM = 0.193 × Weight ( kg ) + 0.107 × Height ( cm ) – 4.157 × Sex (1 for male , 2 for female ) – 0.037 × Age ( years ) – 2.631 The ASMI was determined by dividing the square of the height by the ASM. HG was determined by averaging the measurements of the right and left hands. Before dividing into groups, we applied the interquartile range method to detect and remove outliers. These outliers were then excluded from further analysis. The cohort was divided into groups based on their ASMI and HG measurements. These measurements were classified into the lowest quartile (Q1) and the highest quartile (Q3). 17 , 18 , 19 , 20 , 21 Sarcopenia was defined as having both ASMI and HG measurements below these quartile thresholds. DNA methylation analysis Analysis of DNA methylation arrays Genomic DNA extracted from the peripheral blood of the subjects was used for evaluating DNA methylation levels. In total, 500 ng of genomic DNA from each sample was modified by treatment with sodium bisulfite provided in the EZ DNA methylation kit (Zymo Research, Irvine, CA, USA), following the manufacturer’s protocol. Genome-wide DNA methylation was profiled using the Illumina Infinium Human Methylation EPIC BeadChip (Illumina, San Diego, CA, USA), which contains more than 935,000 CpG probes. Each CpG probe has a β value ranging between 0 and 1; higher methylation of CpG gives a value closer to 1. Identification of differentially methylated probes (DMPs) We used the ChAMP Bioconductor package 22 to perform differential methylation analysis between sarcopenia and normal samples on EPIC datasets. Briefly, following data loading, samples were subjected to filtering for factors such as low quality, low bead count, presence of non-CpG probes, SNP-related probes, multi-hit probes, and probes located on the X or Y chromosomes. After quality control measures and normalization, each sample retained over 850,000 methylation sites for subsequent analysis. Probes with an adjusted P value &lt; 0.05 and absolute difference in beta values (|∆ β |) &gt; 0.15 were considered as DMPs. 23 , 24 , 25 Algorithm development Recursive feature selection The investigated sarcopenia methylation profiles involved many methylation sites. We performed a correlation analysis to remove probes with correlations higher than 0.7. This eliminated the possibility that highly correlated features could interfere with algorithm generalization and potentially lead to redundancy of biomarkers. Following this, recursive feature elimination was implemented on the remaining probes to pinpoint essential features for constructing the model. 26 The methodological approach for the dataset involved splitting it into a training set and test set with a ratio of 7:3. To ensure robust model validation and feature selection, the recursive feature elimination with cross validation (RFECV) technique was applied using the support vector classifier (SVC) within the Yellowbrick library. 27 Starting with all features, one feature with the lowest importance was systematically eliminated in each step, and RFECV-SVC was trained with a 10-fold cross-validation using accuracy scoring. This iterative process continued until the optimal number of features producing the best result was determined. Model training and evaluation Before training the model, we applied the SMOTETomek 28 method to address data imbalance, synthesizing new samples to ensure a more effective class balance and prevent overfitting. With the equilibrium restored, we selected candidate probes for constructing a diagnostic model using various algorithms, including logistic regression (LR), decision tree (DT), K-nearest neighbors (KNN), random forest (RF), naïve bayes (NB), and adaptive boosting (AdaBoost). To ensure robustness and mitigate overfitting, 70% of the data were allocated for training our models, and the remaining 30% were used for testing. We also implemented a 10-fold cross-validation strategy during the training phase. Each ML algorithm contributes unique predictive strengths; however, these algorithms also have potential limitations. To use the collective power of these diverse methodologies, we adopted an ensemble strategy, integrating multiple algorithms to benefit from their cumulative strengths while counterbalancing any individual weaknesses. In the subsequent evaluation phase, the comprehensive ensemble framework underwent a thorough assessment to fine-tune and enhance the predictive accuracy of the model. Calculation of the MRS To assess the risk of sarcopenia, the MRS was computed for each participant by identifying the CpG sites previously associated with the condition. The score was computed as follows 29 : M R S = ∑ i = 1 m W i C i where i is the number of candidate probes, W i is the weight based on the significance of the i th CpG site derived from LR effect sizes that quantify the relationship strength between methylation at these sites and sarcopenia, and the variable C i represents the observed methylation level at each site. Weights were assigned to CpG sites using LR models, establishing an MRS that reflects epigenetic risk factors for sarcopenia, independent of external variables. Participants were stratified into high, intermediate, and low risk categories according to the median risk score. Multinomial LR and odds ratio (OR) analyses were conducted to assess the correlation and odds between sarcopenia-defining variables (ASMI and HG) and age. Statistical analysis Calculations were performed using R version 4.2.2. The ML sections were implemented in Python version 3.9.13 on Jupyter Notebook 6.4.12. All P -values were adjusted using the Benjamini–Hochberg method. Ethics statement The Institutional Review Board (IRB) of Seoul National University ethically reviewed and approved the research (IRB No. E2304/003-003). The study complied with the Declaration of Helsinki. Owing to the anonymized nature of the dataset used, the IRB excused the present study from the necessity of securing additional informed consent from the participants. RESULTS General characteristics of the participants In this cohort study, we initially considered male participants (n = 622). We excluded 113 males because of missing HG data, resulting in a final cohort of 509 males for the analysis. For the DMP analysis, individuals were selected from the lowest and highest quartiles based on their sarcopenia status. This resulted in a group of 37 individuals with sarcopenia and another group of 48 individuals with normal group. This stratification facilitated a comparison of the methylation patterns. Demographic analysis revealed that the sarcopenia group was significantly older than the normal group ( P &lt; 0.001). The sarcopenia group also presented significantly lower means of height, weight, and body mass index than the normal group ( P &lt; 0.001). Muscle strength and mass, measured by HG and ASMI, were significantly lower in the sarcopenia group than in the normal group ( P &lt; 0.001). Waist and hip circumferences were smaller in the sarcopenia group than in the normal group ( P &lt; 0.001). Vital signs also differed, with the sarcopenia group showing lower pulse rates and blood pressures than those in the normal group ( P &lt; 0.001 for systolic blood pressure and diastolic blood pressure; P &lt; 0.05 for pulse). The incidence of comorbid conditions such as hypertension, diabetes, and hyperlipidemia was lower in the sarcopenia group, while arthritis was exclusively observed in this group. Drinking and smoking habits were more prevalent in the normal group. These demographic characteristics are summarized in Table 1 . Identification of DMPs for sarcopenia The procedures used to identify diagnostic biomarkers of sarcopenia are presented in Fig. 1 . By performing preprocessing steps on the group, 740,226 probes were identified in the primary data. Subsequently, probes containing missing values in any sample, those overlapping with SNPs, or those located on sex chromosomes were removed, and the final probes were retained for differential methylation analysis in the subsequent steps. Differential methylation analysis was performed on the sample, identifying 238 DMPs (|∆ β | &gt; 0.15 and adjusted P values &lt; 0.05), which consisted of 167 and 71 hyper- and hypo-methylated positions, respectively. These results are visually represented in a volcano plot in Supplementary Fig. 1 . Diagnostic biomarkers of sarcopenia using recursive feature elimination The process of selecting sarcopenia biomarkers began with an initial pool of 239 DMPs. To ensure feature independence and avoid multicollinearity, a correlation analysis was conducted, which led to the removal of 183 probes with a high Pearson correlation coefficient (≥ 0.7). With the remaining 56 probes, significant markers for the sarcopenia genome were identified using RFECV-SVC, achieving an accuracy of 0.95. This selection process resulted in the identification of an optimal set of eight independent probes for accurate sarcopenia classification ( Fig. 2 ). Model construction and evaluation The modeling results demonstrated varied efficacy among different classification algorithms in identifying sarcopenia using methylation markers. Notably, the ensemble voting classifier emerged as the most proficient model. Its superior performance is evidenced by the confusion matrix displayed in Fig. 3 . The model achieved an impressive area under the receiver operating characteristic curve (AUROC) of 0.94, alongside a high accuracy rate of 0.92. These metrics highlight the robustness of the model in accurately classifying sarcopenia instances. A detailed examination of Fig. 3 illustrates the performance metrics for each classifier used in this study. The DT classifier achieved an accuracy of 0.88, precision of 0.89, recall of 0.80, and an F1-score of 0.84. The RF classifier demonstrated superior performance, reaching an accuracy of 0.92, precision of 1.00, recall of 0.80, and an F1-score of 0.89. The LR, KNN, NB, AdaBoost, and majority voting classifiers all showed high performance, each achieving an accuracy of 0.92 or higher. Comprehensive details about the biomarkers analyzed can be found in Table 2 . Further, the ROC curve for these results is shown in Supplementary Fig. 2 . Correlation with MRS and variables for risk of sarcopenia MRS calculated using eight genomic markers served as a key component of our diagnostic model. The formula encapsulates various genomic contributions: MRS = ( cg 00299070 × -5.5781989 + cg 01372694 × 2.75937234 + cg 02000275 × 4.61396834 + cg 03041029 × 2.10698419 + cg 03655684 × 3.93925928 + cg 00647232 × 0.96366694 + cg 07089275 × 4.36766915 + cg 24699087 × 4.46198529) The MRS group was divided into the high, middle, and low score categories based on quartiles and analyzed using multinomial LR. For the high-MRS group, the age coefficient of 1.24939238 and an OR of 3.49 indicate a positive correlation; older age is associated with a higher likelihood of being in the high-MRS category. Conversely, the coefficients for HG and ASMI were −0.31162365 and −0.42752753, respectively, with corresponding ORs of 0.73 and 0.65. These negative values were consistent with ORs of 0.73 and 0.65, indicating an inverse relationship: the stronger the handgrip and the greater the muscle mass, the lower the probability of being in the high-MRS group. For the Low-MRS group, the coefficient for age was −1.18674218 and an OR of 0.31 suggested a negative correlation; older individuals are less likely to be in the low-MRS group. In contrast, positive coefficients for HG 0.27826224 and 0.56787078, with ORs of 1.32 and 1.76, respectively, indicate that better physical metrics correlate with a higher probability of being in the low-MRS group. Validation with a control group supported these relationships, showing that older age tends to correlate with higher MRS scores, while better physical indicators like HG and ASMI are associated with lower chances of being in the high-MRS group. To facilitate visualization of the correlation and OR analyses of the MRS with HG, ASMI, and age, these relationships are presented using box plots in Supplementary Fig. 3 . DISCUSSION Sarcopenia, characterized by the degeneration of skeletal muscle mass and strength, is a condition that primarily affects older adults and significantly impairs physical function and quality of life. 1 DNA methylation, an important epigenetic mechanism for regulating gene expression, is involved in the aging process and muscle function. This suggests that methylation plays a role in the development of sarcopenia. Therefore, based on previous studies showing that DNA methylation in muscle tissue is associated with aging and disease, 30 , 31 , 32 we hypothesized that sarcopenia in Korean males could be diagnosed by identifying specific methylation patterns using ML and implementing a MRS. We analyzed a cohort of male individuals aged 40–69 years from a rural hospital to identify key DMPs for diagnosing sarcopenia in Koreans. Using these key DMPs, we developed a diagnostic model utilizing ML techniques. To mitigate overfitting caused by the high dimensionality of the genome, we reduced redundancy by excluding values with Pearson correlation coefficients above 0.7. We then addressed data imbalance using the SMOTETomek model. Subsequently, we performed feature selection on the adjusted data using the RFECV-SVC model. The diagnostic model was evaluated using ensemble methods, achieving an accuracy of 0.92 and an AUROC of 0.94, confirming its high accuracy. Additionally, we calculated the MRS using the eight identified biomarkers to explore the association of methylation with sarcopenia. Apart from using the sarcopenia model data, MRS was implemented on a control dataset to validate the association between age and sarcopenia diagnostic variables. These findings indicate that the risk of a higher MRS increases with age and is negatively correlated with HG and ASMI. This association between MRS and age suggests that sarcopenia is likely to occur with aging. Additionally, the findings indicate that higher MRS levels correlate with an increase risk of sarcopenia, as muscle mass and function decline. This emphasizes the importance of enhancing muscle strength and function to mitigate the sarcopenia risk. The eight probes extracted between sarcopenia and normal samples were cg00299070 ( RYR2 ), cg01372694, cg02000275 ( EGLN3 ), cg03041029, cg03655684 (CLCN7), cg00647232 (SEC16B), cg07089275 (SSTR5), and cg24699087. Some of these genes were previously reported to be involved in the pathogenesis of muscle diseases. cg00299070, namely RYR2 , identified as a ryanodine receptor, is critical for calcium-induced release in excitable tissues. 33 Its overexpression has been implicated in a range of conditions including neurodegenerative diseases and heart failure, as well as in connection with sarcopenia, potentially through its influence on calcium ion release in skeletal muscle. cg02000275 ( EGLN3 ), known for its involvement in tumor progression, plays an unexpected role in muscle biology. It has been found to regulate skeletal muscle differentiation and gene expression, which are critical processes in maintaining muscle mass and function, the key areas affected in sarcopenia. 34 cg03655684 (CLCN7), vital for chloride channel production, is directly related to bone resorption activity. It causes osteopetrosis owing to defective osteoclast functioning, illustrating that the gene can intersect with sarcopenia-related pathways. 35 , 36 , 37 cg00647232 (SEC16B) is associated with cellular trafficking and has been linked to body weight regulation and metabolic disease. It may also influence the development of sarcopenia, particularly through pathways involving muscle protein synthesis or degradation. 38 Although the identity of the gene coded by cg01372694 remains unknown, its methylation status has been tied to rheumatoid arthritis, a condition that shares high comorbidity with sarcopenia. 39 This association underscores the potential for methylation patterns as indicators of sarcopenia risk, meriting further investigation. This study has some limitations. As it was conducted in a Korean population, these findings may not be directly applicable to other demographics. The study is based on data from 2004 to 2013, which might still be valid; however, it is important to consider the possibility that the results may differ with more recent data. Additionally, the use of blood samples for methylation analysis may not fully capture the methylation changes specific to sarcopenic muscle tissue. Blood provides a generalized view, potentially overlooking localized alterations in skeletal muscle that contribute to sarcopenia. Therefore, further in vivo and in vitro investigations are necessary to clarify the role of the identified methylation sites in sarcopenia. In summary, this study analyzed DNA methylation changes associated with sarcopenia in middle-aged Korean males and identified key biomarkers through ML. It further explored how MRS correlated with muscle function, strength, and age, highlighting their significant influence on sarcopenia. These findings emphasize the need for early risk assessment and intervention in sarcopenia management, paving the way for new advancements in diagnosis and treatment. Future validation and clinical trials are essential for integrating these biomarkers into clinical practice.
