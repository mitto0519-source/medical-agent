Abstract Abstract Objectives This study aimed to investigate whether the Chinese diagnosis-related group (C-DRG) payment system would reduce low-value coronary revascularisation services among coronary heart disease (CHD) inpatients without affecting high-value coronary revascularisation services. Design Retrospective observational study. Setting Routinely collected claims data from a health insurance database including all inpatients in 22 public hospitals in Sanming, Southern China. Participants All patients with CHD are admitted to public hospitals from 1 January 2017 through 31 December 2020. Intervention/exposure The implementation of the C-DRG-based payment system on 1 January 2018. Main outcome measures Using a health insurance database, we identified two cohorts: beneficiaries for whom the value of coronary revascularisation is lower (those with ischaemic heart disease without acute myocardial infarction, unstable angina and congestive heart failure during hospitalisation) and beneficiaries for whom its value is higher (those with acute coronary syndrome). Then, the rates of low-value or high-value coronary revascularisation were compared before and after the implementation of C-DRG policy, including the use of an interrupted time series analysis. Results An interrupted time series analysis demonstrated that the C-DRG policy was associated with a statistically significant immediate decrease in the rate of low-value coronary revascularisation of −9.78% (95% CI: −11.08% to −8.48%). Further, after introducing C-DRG, the rate of low-value coronary revascularisation decreased by −0.59% (95% CI: −0.88% to −0.30%) every quarter compared with before C-DRG. In addition, after C-DRG, the rate of high-value coronary revascularisation increased by 1.27% (95% CI: 0.14% to 2.41%) every quarter compared with before C-DRG. Conclusions This study suggested that C-DRG policy achieved at least short-term success in reducing use of low-value coronary revascularisation without evidence of decreasing high-value coronary revascularisation services. These results can support policymakers in reducing low-value care in China and other countries that use similar systems. STRENGTHS AND LIMITATIONS OF THIS STUDY This study is an interrupted time series analysis, a strong quasi-experimental design for estimating longitudinal impact following an intervention. We used routinely collected claims data from a health insurance database encompassing all patients admitted for coronary heart disease at all 22 public hospitals in Sanming, Southern China. Generalisability to other areas might be limited since only Sanming, the first pilot city of the Chinese diagnosis-related group reform, was examined in this study. This study has limitations of an observational study with the potential bias and unmeasured confounding associated with non-randomised allocation of participants. Introduction Coronary heart disease (CHD) ranks as one of the leading causes of death in China and globally. 1 2 According to the 2020 China Cardiovascular Disease Report, there were 11.4 million patients with CHD, and the estimated total inpatient expenditure associated with CHD was ¥112.0 billion in 2018. 3 CHD care has become one of the most expensive components of the healthcare system in China. It is crucial to focus on costly cardiovascular services, such as coronary revascularisation procedures, which make a tremendous contribution to the rising health expenditure. Low-value care provides little or no benefit, causes harm and incurs unnecessary costs. 4 Spending on low-value care diverts spending from high-value care which can improve the health of the population. 5 6 It was reported that low-value care, especially low-value coronary revascularisation procedure, is common in the care of patients with CHD. 7 Coronary revascularisation (coronary artery bypass grafting or percutaneous coronary intervention (PCI)) is an ideal procedure to investigate given that its value to patients varies substantially from scenarios where it can be lifesaving (high-value) for acute myocardial infarction (AMI) or of low-value for asymptomatic ischaemia. 8 Coronary revascularisation is one of the fastest-growing procedures in China, particularly PCI, which is one of the most commonly performed coronary revascularisation procedures. 9 Over the last decades, the number of PCIs has increased more than 20-fold in China. 9 Given the high prevalence and high cost of coronary revascularisation, reducing the use of low-value coronary revascularisation can yield substantial savings and is of particular importance for CHD health. Various approaches have been used to tackle the overuse of low-value care and have had varying success. 10 Among them, the prospective payment system is one of the most promising strategies, since it can reduce the financial incentives of providers to offer unnecessary procedures by shifting financial risks from insurers to providers. 10 Several studies have shown that insurers may target wasteful spending through prospective payment policies. 11 12 Diagnosis-related groups (DRGs) are a popularly adopted prospective payment system, which aims to reduce inefficiency and contain cost. A number of studies found that the DRG payment system significantly shortened patients’ length of stay and reduced the intensity of patient care during hospitalisation. 12 13 However, whether the reduction in intensity of care following the implementation of DRG policy mainly resulted from reducing low-value or high-value care has been unclear. A previous study in Taiwan assessed the impact of the DRG policy on the utilisation of three selected low-value preoperative testing services (chest X-ray, echocardiogram and stress testing) and found that the use of the three preoperative tests had only a minor temporary reduction in 2010, but later increased over time. 14 However, the study in Taiwan only examined three selected low-value diagnostic testing services. But the effect of DRG on the use of low-value therapeutic procedures, such as low-value coronary revascularisation procedures, was not examined and is unclear. On 1 January 2018, Sanming city was chosen as the first site in China to pilot a new payment system called ‘Chinese DRG (C-DRG)-based payment system’. In this system, hospitals were reimbursed prospectively based on a predetermined fixed rate of ‘bundled’ payments for inpatient care (for details of C-DRG policy, see online supplemental table 1 ). Our previous studies evaluated the association of C-DRG with total medical resource use for critically ill patients and patients with hip fractures. 15 16 However, whether the implementation of the C-DRG policy resulted in a reduced use of low-value or high-value care has not been studied. Given that coronary revascularisation accounted for a significant contribution to health expenditure, a better understanding of how financial incentives associated with a prospective payment system based on C-DRG may affect the provision of low-value or high-value coronary revascularisation for patients with CHD could better inform future interventions. Therefore, the primary objective of this study was to investigate whether the C-DRG policy reduced the use of low-value coronary revascularisation services among inpatients with CHD in a large health insurance claims database. This study also evaluated whether the C-DRG policy was associated with the use of high-value coronary revascularisation services. Methods Given the significant risk of bias inherent in retrospective observational studies, we used an interrupted time series (ITS) analysis. ITS analysis is a strong quasi-experimental methodology used to investigate the longitudinal effects of interventions 17 and has commonly been used to evaluate a wide range of policy interventions. 18 Data sources We examined the Sanming Basic Health Insurance Scheme claims database to identify all patients admitted for CHD at all 22 public hospitals from 1 January 2017 to 31 December 2020. The date of discharge was defined as the index date. The data for each admission obtained from the database routinely captured data elements in discharge abstracts including demographic characteristics, diagnoses, procedures, and medical expenses. CHD were identified by assessing primary diagnoses with the code-class I20–I25 of the International Classification of Diseases, 10th revision. These patients were grouped into two mutually exclusive categories: (1) acute coronary syndrome (ACS), and (2) stable ischaemic heart disease (SIHD). Patients with ACS included patients with AMI, unstable angina and congestive heart failure recorded in the claims data for whom coronary revascularisation procedures could be considered as high-value (necessary). 8 According to evidence-based clinical guidelines, coronary revascularisation is widely considered to be low-value for patients with SIHD without AMI, unstable angina and congestive heart failure during hospitalisation. 19 20 Therefore, the use of coronary revascularisation for patients with SIHD was defined as low-value coronary revascularisation, and the use of coronary revascularisation for patients with ACS was defined as high-value coronary revascularisation, both as dichotomous variables. We identified the occurrence of each low-value or high-value coronary revascularisation service using International Classification of Diseases, ninth revision, Clinical Modification procedure codes, recorded in the health insurance scheme claims database. Outcomes The primary outcome was low-value coronary revascularisation during hospitalisation (dummy variable=1, if underwent a low-value coronary revascularisation, and 0, otherwise). An additional outcome was high-value coronary revascularisation during hospitalisation (dummy variable=1, if underwent a high-value coronary revascularisation, and 0, otherwise). Other variables Other variables included the demographic characteristics, patient comorbidities and hospital characteristics. The demographic characteristics included age, sex and insurance types (Urban and Rural Resident Basic Medical Insurance (URRBMI) and Urban Employee Basic Medical Insurance (UEBMI)). In China, the URRBMI covers the urban non-employed and self-employed population and rural population, while the UEBMI covers the urban employed population and retired people. Patients’ comorbidities were estimated using the Charlson Comorbidity Index (CCI), 21 which was dichotomised to 0 and ≥1. The hospital characteristics included hospital level (tertiary hospital or secondary hospital) and hospital type (traditional Chinese medicine (TCM) hospitals or general hospitals). According to the hospital classification criteria in China, a tertiary hospital has more than 500 beds, and a secondary hospital has 100~499 beds. 22 TCM hospitals usually provided health services integrated with western medicine and TCM, and general hospitals mainly provided western medicine services. Patient and public involvement There was no patient or public involvement in the design, conduct or reporting of this study. Statistical analysis The Student’s t-tests and χ 2 tests were used to compare characteristics of patients before and after the implementation of C-DRG policy. Then, we used a quasi-experimental design to estimate the impact of C-DRG policy on the use of low-value or high-value coronary revascularisation services by employing ITS analysis with segmented linear regression. 23 24 Segments in the time series were defined as the sequence of measures divided into the pre-policy change and post-policy change periods. Segmented regression analysis allowed us to model the ITS data to estimate the immediate change in level, or change in the use of low-value or high-value coronary revascularisation services following the implementation of C-DRG policy change, and change in trend, or the change in slope of quarterly use of low-value or high-value coronary revascularisation services measures in the post-policy change segment compared with the pre-policy change segment. We also conducted logistic regression to examine the impact of C-DRG policy on using low-value and high-value coronary revascularisation services, adjusting for the covariates described above. Analyses were conducted using Stata (V. 15.0). For all tests, p&lt;0.05 was considered statistically significant, based on two-sided tests. Results The study included 21 805 patients with CHD, of whom 6173 were before the C-DRG reform, and 15 632 after the C-DRG reform. The characteristics of patients in the two groups were similar in age, sex, CCI and hospital type, but varied in terms of insurance type (p&lt;0.001) and hospital level (p&lt;0.001) ( table 1 ). The proportion of patients’ admissions covered by UEBMI rose from 30.5% before the C-DRG reform to 41.4% after the reform, while the proportion of admissions in tertiary hospitals decreased from 61.8% before C-DRG to 57.3% after the C-DRG reform. As shown in online supplemental table 2 , the rate of low-value coronary revascularisation utilisation dropped from 11% in the first quarter of 2017 to 6.3% in the fourth quarter of 2020. The scatter plots of low-value or high-value coronary revascularisation over the study period are displayed in figures1 2 , respectively. These figures plot the observed (actual data points) and fitted (model estimates) lines using segmented regressions. Following the implementation of the C-DRG policy, there was a downward step change seen in the rate of low-value coronary revascularisation. Table 2 quantifies these results. The results of ITS analysis showed that the C-DRG policy was associated with a significant reduction in low-value coronary revascularisation service ( table 2 ). Before the implementation of the C-DRG policy, the rate of low-value coronary revascularisation increased by 0.73% every quarter. The implementation of the C-DRG policy resulted in a statistically significant immediate (in the first quarter) decrease in the rate of low-value coronary revascularisation of −9.78% (95% CI: −11.08% to −8.48%), and a significant reduction in the quarterly trend of the rate of low-value coronary revascularisation of −0.59% (95% CI: −0.88% to −0.30%) per quarter relative to that before the reform ( table 2 ). As shown in table 2 , before the implementation of the C-DRG policy, the rate of high-value coronary revascularisation decreased at a rate of −0.06% every quarter. The immediate change in the rate of high-value coronary revascularisation was not significant (p=0.376), but there was a significant increase in the quarterly rate of high-value coronary revascularisation over time after introducing C-DRG. Compared with the preintervention trend, the C-DRG policy was associated with a significant increase in the quarterly rate of change of high-value coronary revascularisation by 1.27% per quarter (95% CI: 0.14% to 2.41%). We also included the covariates (age, sex, insurance types, CCI, hospital level and hospital types) in the ITS analysis. The estimated results of the ITS analysis adjusted for covariates are shown in online supplemental table 3 ). After the C-DRG policy, a statistically significant immediate decrease in the rate of low-value coronary revascularisation of −1.05% (95% CI: −1.37% to −0.73%) was noted, but no significant change was observed in the trend (p=0.518). In addition, the quarterly rate of high-value coronary revascularisation showed a little increase in the trend (0.03%) but was not statistically significant (p=0.435) ( online supplemental table 3 ). The results of multivariate logistic regression showed that C-DRG policy was significantly associated with reduction of low-value coronary revascularisation (OR: 0.27, 95% CI: 0.23 to 0.31) and increase of high-value coronary revascularisation (OR: 1.59, 95% CI: 1.42 to 1.78), adjusting for age, sex, insurance types, CCI, hospital level and hospital types ( table 3 ). Discussion To the best of our knowledge, this is the first study to evaluate the association of C-DRG policy with the use of low-value coronary revascularisation. This study showed that C-DRG policy was associated with improved outcomes, a significant reduction in the use of low-value coronary revascularisation services and an increase in the use of high-value coronary revascularisation services in our main ITS model. Freeing the resources from low-value care creates new opportunities for high-value care, because there is an opportunity cost (ie, loss of choosing other alternatives) for resources consumed in the delivery of low-value care, whereby resources could be better used in the delivery of high-value care. 25 Consistent with the hypothesis that prospective payment systems disincentivise unnecessary procedures, our findings demonstrated an immediate marked reduction in the use of low-value coronary revascularisation after the implementation of C-DRG policy. The evidence base characterising coronary revascularisation as low-value for the management of stable CHD has been well established in medical literature and widely acknowledged by international medical professional organisations since 2007. 19 It has been previously suggested that fee-for-service payment may provide inappropriate incentives for overuse of low-value health services and delay the abandonment of medical technologies proven to be ineffective. 26 In our study, with the implementation of the C-DRG policy, it is evident that the rate of low-value coronary revascularisation took a step downwards. This result verified that physician decision-making is determined, at least in part, by financial incentives, suggesting a link between the adoption of C-DRG policy and subsequent behavioural change in medical decision-making. In our study, the change in the rate of low-value coronary revascularisation trend relative to the preintervention trend was −0.59%. After covariate adjustment, no significant change in the rate of low-value coronary revascularisation trend was observed. This finding implied that it is not certain whether the reform had stable and prolonged effects on reducing the use of low-value coronary revascularisation services in the long term. However, there was no evidence of an increase in the rate of low-value coronary revascularisation. A previous study in Taiwan found that DRG policy was associated only with a temporary and minor decrease in the use of low-value preoperative testing, but later increased over time. 14 These results implied that physicians may have different reactions to low-value therapeutic procedures and low-value diagnostic tests after the DRG reform. Previous studies suggested that defensive medical practices of physicians might link to the use of low-value diagnostic tests. 27 28 Although some diagnostic tests, such as preoperative chest X-rays, may be widely regarded as low-value for patients undergoing low-risk surgeries, physicians may still prescribe low-value diagnostic tests as a safeguard of self-protection against risks of potential medical lawsuits. 14 These results implied that due to physicians’ defensive medicine, compared with low-value diagnostic tests, reducing low-value therapeutic procedures such as low-value coronary revascularisation might be more feasible, and has more potential to save avoidable cost under the DRG payment system. 29 These strategies of the C-DRG policy in China may have important implications for other countries that use similar systems to reduce low-value care, especially for low- and middle-income countries (LMICs). Many LMICs are also experiencing remarkable increases in the overuse of low-value health services and rapid increases in healthcare expenditures. 30 For example, according to one second-opinion centre in India, 55% of recommended cardiac stents or surgery were deemed inappropriate, and inappropriate PCI has been documented in Israel with a prevalence of 22%. 31 Elimination of low-value care practices has much economic promise because it would improve healthcare quality and spare the limited health resources for those unmet healthcare needs in these countries. 31 The DRG-based prospective payment system may be a feasible policy option to reduce low-value care in LMICs. This study showed that the implementation of the C-DRG policy was associated with the reduction of using low-value coronary revascularisation. However, the rate of low-value coronary revascularisation after the C-DRG reform was still 6.3% in the 4th quarter of 2020 ( online supplemental table 2 ). There was still room to reduce low-value coronary revascularisation, compared with some other countries, such as Australia, with a rate of 1.67–4.38%. 32 This suggests that besides the payment reform policy, detailed study and insights into the views and behaviours of physicians and patients, and barriers for the withdrawal of low-value care, are required in the future. For example, previous studies showed that patient pressure and the physician’s need to maintain a good patient–physician relationship could induce the use of low-value care. 33 34 Strengths in our study include our use of a representative sample with a large sample size, and our use of a robust ITS analytical design. Nevertheless, our study has several limitations. First, this was an observational study, and therefore, this study has all the limitations of an observational study with all the potential bias and unmeasured confounding associated with non-randomised analyses. However, differences in baseline and clinical characteristics were adjusted in a multivariate regression analysis, and the use of the recognised quasi-experimental ITS analytical design enabled us to control for pre-existing levels and trends to detect any discontinuities in the outcomes studied when the C-DRG policy was implemented. Adjusting for baseline trends in this way helps control most threats to internal validity. Second, we focused on low-value coronary revascularisation procedures in this study, because of the high prevalence and cost of coronary revascularisation. However, it is important to emphasise that our findings may not be generalisable to other low-value care services. Thus, further extensive evaluation for different low-value care services is needed. Third, only Sanming as the first pilot city of C-DRG reform was examined in this study; therefore, the results may not be generalised to other areas. In future, more comprehensive evaluations need to be conducted when the C-DRG policy is rolled out in more pilot sites in China; and the adoption of DRG-based payment systems for other countries requires adequate modifications to reflect specific country context. Conclusion Our study suggested that the C-DRG policy was associated with reduced use of low-value coronary revascularisation without decreasing high-value coronary revascularisation services, at least in its first 3 years of implementation.
