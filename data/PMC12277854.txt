Purpose Nutritional intervention is critical in managing pediatric non-alcoholic fatty liver disease (NAFLD). This study assessed eating habits and dietary quality of Korean children and adolescents with NAFLD using the Nutrition Quotient (NQ). Methods In this prospective multicenter study across seven pediatric centers in Korea, children diagnosed with NAFLD completed the NQ questionnaire. Participants were grouped as mild, moderate, or severe based on hepatic steatosis grade on ultrasonography. Total and subfactor NQ scores were compared among groups. Results Of 163 participants, 26.4% had mild, 52.8% moderate, and 20.8% severe steatosis. Total NQ scores decreased with steatosis severity (mild 59.3±16.9, moderate 54.2±12.9, severe 52.9±14.2; p =0.033). Likewise, moderation scores declined (mild 59.1±16.9, moderate 53.0±12.8, severe 52.4±14.0; p =0.024), as did practice scores (mild 67.6±37.8, moderate 57.1±19.0, severe 54.7±21.4; p =0.038). The severe group also reported more frequent fast food intake near schools or academies ( p =0.02). Conclusion Children with NAFLD had lower NQ scores than healthy peers reported in previous studies, reflecting poorer dietary quality. Lower moderation and practice scores, along with frequent fast food consumption, were associated with greater hepatic steatosis. These findings underscore the urgent need to improve eating habits through family-based nutritional education and sustained dietary management to mitigate NAFLD progression. The global incidence of NAFLD in children and adolescents is a growing concern worldwide, with significant implications for children’s long-term health [ 2 ]. Nutritional intervention plays a crucial role in the management of this condition for several reasons. Achieving and maintaining healthy weight are essential for managing NAFLD. Nutritional intervention focuses on creating a balanced calorie-controlled diet for weight management and obesity prevention [ 3 ]. NAFLD is closely associated with insulin resistance [ 4 ]. Nutritional strategies such as a low-glycemic diet can aid in improving insulin sensitivity and reduce the risk of disease progression. In addition, high sugar and certain dietary fats, particularly saturated and trans fats, contribute to the accumulation of liver fat. However, unlike adults, children undergo rapid growth and indiscriminately reduce calories or exclude essential nutrients, which can have severe consequences on both physical and mental development. Therefore, ensuring a balanced intake of carbohydrates, proteins, and fats is essential. Childhood and adolescence are critical periods in the formation of lifelong habits, including dietary habits. Introducing a balanced and varied diet early not only meets immediate nutritional needs but also fosters habits that contribute to long-term health. The complex interplay among dietary habits, metabolic factors, and liver health underscores the significance of understanding the nutritional status of individuals with NAFLD [ 5 ]. A thorough assessment and analysis of the nutritional baseline are essential prerequisites for designing effective and targeted interventions. In this study, we explored the association between dietary habits in children and adolescents, as assessed by the Korean Nutrition Quotient (NQ) score, and the metabolic consequences of NAFLD. Utilizing these indices allows for a culturally relevant examination of nutritional patterns and their potential impact on liver health in youth belonging to the Republic of Korea. The Korean NQ score provides a comprehensive framework for evaluating dietary intake, including macronutrient distribution, micronutrient status, and overall dietary quality. By analyzing these indices in relation to the state of NAFLD, we aimed to identify specific dietary factors that may contribute to or mitigate the risk of developing this liver condition in pediatric and adolescent age groups. This study addresses the need for tailored interventions by elucidating how specific dietary components, as reflected in Korean nutritional indices, may influence the incidence and severity of NAFLD in young individuals. The findings of this study can inform public health strategies aimed at promoting healthier dietary behaviors in children and adolescents, thereby mitigating the risk of NAFLD and its associated complications in this vulnerable population. This study investigated the importance of conducting a detailed baseline nutritional assessment in individuals diagnosed with NAFLD. MATERIALS AND METHODS Patients and study design This prospective multicenter study was conducted in the pediatric departments of seven centers in the Republic of Korea between July 2023 and January 2024: CHA Bundang Medical Center, Chungnam National University Sejong Hospital, Kosin University Gospel Hospital, Soonchunhyang University Bucheon Hospital, Seoul St. Mary’s Hospital, Kyung Hee University Hospital in Gangdong, and Inje University Ilsan Paik Hospital. Patients aged 6–18 years diagnosed with NAFLD were included in this study. NAFLD was diagnosed based on bright or hyperechoic lesions on liver imaging and alanine aminotransferase (ALT) levels of ≥22 IU/L [ 6 ]. Patients who were receiving hepatotoxic drugs, had underlying chronic liver disease (e.g., viral hepatitis, autoimmune hepatitis, Wilson disease, or other liver diseases), or were morbidly obese with endocrine, genetic, or central nervous system diseases were excluded. Body weight and height were measured by a trained technician, and body mass index was calculated by dividing weight in kilograms by the square of height in meters. Clinical data were obtained from electronic medical records, including sex, age at diagnosis, and laboratory results at diagnosis, including white ALT, aspartate aminotransferase, total cholesterol, triglyceride, low-density lipoprotein, high-density lipoprotein cholesterol, and fasting glucose levels. The degree of steatosis was classified as mild, moderate, or severe by a pediatric radiologist using ultrasonography [ 7 ]. To investigate dietary behavior and food quality, the Korean Nutrition Quotient-Children (NQ-C) questionnaire was used for those aged 6–12 years, whereas the Korean Nutrition Quotient-Adolescent (NQ-A) questionnaire was used for adolescents aged 12–18 years. Patients with NAFLD who participated in the survey were divided into three groups according to steatosis grade on liver ultrasonography, and the NQ score and each item were analyzed. Survey of dietary behavior and food quality using the Korean NQ score The NQ is an index scored by comprehensively evaluating the dietary behavior, food quality, and nutritional status in an individual or group [ 8 ]. It is used to comprehensively measure and evaluate the quantity and quality of children’s diets, and its validity has been proven. Therefore, in this study, we used a questionnaire to investigate children’s eating behaviors and food quality. The NQ, consisting of 19 questions, was printed and used on the website of the Korean Society of Nutrition. The survey was conducted using a written nutritional index questionnaire, and the NQ score and component areas were evaluated. The NQ score of children and adolescents evaluates their eating habits by comprehensively considering the level of nutritional intake, balance between diet and physical activity, food safety and hygiene, and correct food selection and eating behavior [ 9 10 ]. After filling out the questionnaire, the NQ score result sheet was printed and used for analysis. The evaluation items that constitute the NQ survey are classified into three factors: “balance,” “moderation,” and “practice.” Evaluation is divided into the balance factor, which determines whether one eats a variety of necessary foods; the moderation factor, which determines whether one consumes a small number of unhealthy foods; and the practice factor, which determines whether one practices healthy and safe eating behaviors. The total NQ score and scores for each sub-factor were classified into three grades based on the percentile distribution. The “high” grade corresponded to ≥75.0% of the national survey results in terms of the NQ score, indicating that the overall dietary habits are good and that the individual is practicing desirable dietary habits. The “medium” grade corresponded to 25.0–74.9% of the national survey results in terms of the percentile distribution, indicating that the dietary habits are average but require improvement in a more desirable direction. The “low” grade corresponded to 0.0–24.9% of the national survey results in terms of the percentile distribution, signifying that the overall dietary habits are poor and that the individual’s dietary habits require urgent improvement. Statistical analysis For comparative analysis between groups, the chi-square test or Fisher's exact test was used for categorical data, whereas the independent t -test or Mann-Whitney U-test and analysis of variance or Kruskal-Wallis test, as appropriate, were used for numeric data. Normal distribution was assessed using the Shapiro-Wilk test. The Jonckheere-Terpstra test was used to analyze the trends between the three groups that were independent but had rank relationships. The Mann-Whitney U-test was used as the post-hoc test. Continuous variables are reported as medians with interquartile ranges or means with standard deviations. Categorical variables are expressed as numbers and percentages (%). Statistical significance was set at p &lt;0.05. All analyses were performed using SPSS version 28.0 (IBM Corp.). Ethics statement This study was approved by the Institutional Review Board (IRB) of CHA Bundang Medical Center and all participating hospitals. Informed consent was obtained from the participating subjects and their guardians (IRB no. 2023-03-001-002). RESULTS Baseline characteristics A total of 163 participants were included in this study. Based on the severity of steatosis on liver ultrasonography, they were divided into mild (n=43, 26.4%), moderate (n=86, 52.8%), and severe (n=34, 20.8%) groups. All three groups had a higher proportion of male individuals than female individuals. No statistically significant differences in age, height, weight, or laboratory test results were observed among the three groups. The baseline demographic and clinical characteristics are summarized in Table 1 . NQ score and grade according to the severity of hepatic steatosis The total NQ score was 59.3±16.9 in the mild group, 54.2±12.9 in the moderate group, and 52.9±14.2 in the severe group, showing a significant tendency for the total NQ score to decrease as the degree of steatosis increased ( p =0.033). The mean NQ score by subcomponent was highest in practice at 59.8±26.1, followed by moderation at 54.8±14.6, and balance at 45.8±16.2, the lowest. The scores for the moderation factor were 59.1±16.9 for mild, 53.0±12.8 for moderate, and 52.4±14.0 for severe, showing a significant decrease as the severity increased ( p =0.024). The scores for the practice factor were also 67.6±37.8 for mild, 57.1±19.0 for moderate, and 54.7±21.4 for severe, showing a significant decrease as the severity increased ( p =0.038). There were no significant differences in balance scores among the three groups ( p =0.938) ( Table 2 ). Among the three NQ grades, the “medium” grade was the most common (36.8%), followed by “low” (35%) and “high” (28.2%) grades. In the balance, moderation, and practice sub-areas, the “medium” grade was the most common, followed by the “low” and “high” grades. With respect to the overall NQ grade, “high” grade was the most prevalent at 48.8% in the mild group, “medium” grade was the most prevalent at 45.4% in the moderate group, and “low” grade was the most prevalent at 44.1% in the severe group, showing a statistically significant difference ( Table 3 ). Analysis of NQ subcomponent items according to the degree of liver steatosis on liver ultrasonography When comparing items by NQ subfactor, the severe group among the moderating factors had a significantly higher frequency of eating out near school/academia (fast food consumption) ( p =0.02). In addition, there was no difference between the three groups in the items of sweet food and sweet drinks, fatty foods, ramen, and overeating, and in the balance item, there was no significant difference between the groups in the consumption of vegetables, fruits, dairy products, fish or shellfish, eggs, tofu or beans, and nuts. The practice items included breakfast habits, checking food labels, washing hands, and exercising, and there were no significant differences among the three groups ( Table 4 ). DISCUSSION This study is the first to evaluate diet quality and eating behavior patterns using the NQ score according to steatosis grade in children and adolescents with NAFLD in the Republic of Korea. A significant difference in the total NQ score according to the severity of hepatic steatosis was found. In particular, there was a significant difference according to severity in moderation and practice factors. The higher the severity of hepatic steatosis, the more likely that the patient would have difficulty in maintaining a diet that limits unhealthy sugars, saturated fats, and sodium, as well as undesirable eating behaviors. Additionally, there were fewer practices of safe eating habits and eating behaviors, such as hand washing and not walking around while eating. In previous studies investigating the total NQ score of healthy children and adolescents, the average NQ score was 61.2 in a study targeting middle school students in Chungnam, 58.3 in Daegu, 68.3 in Seoul, and 64.4 in fifth and sixth graders when the NQ was developed [ 8 11 12 13 14 ]. The average NQ score was 60 in Jeju, 66.8 in Gwangju, and 59.7 in Gyeongju [ 15 16 17 ]. Although the NQ score has been reported to vary by region and age group, the average total NQ score of children and adolescents with NAFLD in this study was 55.5, which is lower than that of healthy children and adolescents reported in previous studies. This suggests that children and adolescents with NAFLD have poorer overall eating habits and diet quality than healthy children and adolescents. Furthermore, the higher the severity of hepatic steatosis, the greater is the effort required to improve eating habits and dietary quality. In this study, the average NQ score by subfactor was the highest in practice, followed by moderate and balance. All three sub-factors required urgent improvements. Previous studies have reported that NQ and NQ subfactor scores vary depending on parental education level, family economic status, mother's employment status, family type, and subjective health awareness [ 8 12 13 ]. In order to improve NQ during the growth period, it is necessary to link with the family and provide dietary education to parents. In particular, family centered efforts are important for improving the eating behaviors of children and adolescents with NAFLD. Balanced eating behavior was identified by the intake of five key foods: whole-grain rice, fruits, white milk, soy products, and eggs. The frequency of fruit consumption showed no significant difference across the hepatic steatosis grades; however, it was lower than that reported in the national data [ 18 ]. This discrepancy could be due to the differing survey methodologies. Assessing the actual quantity of consumed fruit would be more meaningful. Fruits low in energy and rich in dietary fibers should be consumed daily to prevent chronic diseases. The current intake levels indicate the need for continuous management. White milk consumption was insufficient across all groups, with percentages below the national average recommended intake [ 19 ]. Increased milk consumption is necessary to improve the nutritional status and eating behaviors, as it correlates with better nutrient intake and reduced sugar consumption from sweetened beverages [ 20 ]. The vegetable intake was significantly below the recommended level, with adolescents consuming fewer side dishes than recommended [ 21 ]. Increasing vegetable consumption is crucial for health benefits, including the reduction of cardiovascular risk. Therefore, it is necessary to increase vegetable consumption among adolescents. In particular, children and adolescents with NAFLD need to make greater efforts to consume vegetables. Moderation-related behaviors such as consumption of sweet foods, fast foods, and late-night snacks can cause nutritional imbalances. Excessive sugar intake has been linked to essential nutrient deficiencies [ 22 ]. Therefore, it is necessary to make efforts to avoid excessive sugar intake during adolescence, as it can cause nutritional imbalances. Fast food consumption increases with the severity of hepatic steatosis, emphasizing the need to reduce food intake to improve nutritional status. A recent study conducted in the United States found that people who were obese or diabetic and consumed &gt;20% of their daily calories from fast food had significantly higher liver fat levels than those who rarely or never ate fast food [ 23 ]. As the frequency of fast-food consumption was higher in the severe group, it seems necessary to reduce the frequency of fast-food consumption to improve eating behavior and nutritional status. Practice-related behaviors, including breakfast consumption, food label checking, and hand washing, showed that breakfast skipping rates increased with the severity of hepatic steatosis, although this difference was not statistically significant. Skipping breakfast negatively affects nutrient intake, obesity risk, and academic performance, highlighting the need for intervention [ 24 25 ]. This study has some limitations. The broad age range (6-18 years) complicates distinguishing the behavioral differences between children and adolescents. However, the analysis was based on non-duplicate NQ questions with an average age of 12.2 years, allowing for a comprehensive assessment. The NQ questionnaire focuses on frequency rather than quantity, thus limiting detailed intake information. Additionally, this study did not analyze the gut microbiome, which may have provided insights into NAFLD pathogenesis. Moreover, the absence of a control group limited the ability to fully contextualize the findings. Although the previous K-NQ results discussed in this manuscript partially compensate for this limitation, it is important to acknowledge this constraint explicitly. Creating an age- and sex-matched pooled control group using data from previous studies could be a practical approach to addressing this issue in future research. In conclusion, the results of this study showed that the NQ score of pediatric patients with NAFLD was lower than that of their healthy counterparts in previous studies. There is a need to improve eating habits, especially those related to moderation and practice. In particular, the severity of hepatic steatosis worsens as the frequency of fast-food consumption increases. Overall, improvements in eating habits are urgently needed, and family-based nutritional education and continuous nutritional management are essential.
