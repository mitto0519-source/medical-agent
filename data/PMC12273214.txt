Background Metabolic syndrome (MS) is a well-established risk factor for chronic kidney disease (CKD), especially in hypertensive patients. These patients are more likely to exhibit additional components of MS, further elevating their risk of developing CKD. This study aimed to investigate the association between the components of MS and the risk of CKD in hypertensive patients. Methods This retrospective cohort study included 51,470 hypertensive patients who participated in the national basic public health services for hypertension management in Minhang District, Shanghai, China. Cases of CKD were identified through linkage with elderly physical examination statistics. Multivariable-adjusted Cox proportional hazard models were used to assess the relationship between the number of MS components and the risk of CKD. Additionally, the study explored the associations between different combinations of MS components and CKD. Results During the 3-year follow-up period, the incidence of CKD was significantly higher in hypertensive patients with MS compared to those without MS (33.77 vs. 25.67 per 1000 person-years). Hypertensive patients with MS had a 15% increased risk of developing CKD compared to those without MS. Furthermore, the risk of CKD increased progressively with the number of MS components ( P for trend &lt; 0.01; HR 1.17, 1.27, and 1.37 for 3, 4, and 5 components, respectively, versus 1 component). Additionally, elevated waist circumference (WC) was significantly associated with CKD in hypertensive patients with only one additional MS component ( P &lt; 0.05). Conclusion In hypertensive patients, the risk of CKD significantly increases with the accumulation of MS components. Notably, elevated WC in hypertensive patients is particularly associated with a higher risk of CKD. According to the Global Burden of Disease study, the global prevalence of CKD was 9.1% in 2017, affecting approximately 700 million patients [ 2 ]. With the aging population, the rising prevalence of CKD has become a critical health challenge, imposing substantial healthcare costs [ 3 ]. Therefore, it is essential to identify and investigate the characteristics of individuals at higher risk for developing CKD at an earlier stage of life. Metabolic syndrome (MS) is a cluster of medical conditions characterized by abnormal metabolic components, including abdominal obesity, dyslipidemia, elevated fasting glucose, and hypertension [ 4 ]. Evidence suggests that MS is associated with an increased risk of CKD, with the risk of CKD rising progressively as the number of MS components increases [ 5 , 6 ]. Hypertension, a key component of MS, is highly prevalent in the adult population, affecting approximately 32% (95% CI: 30–34) of women and 34% (95% CI: 32–37) of men [ 7 ]. With the aging population and increased life expectancy, the prevalence of comorbid chronic diseases has risen significantly [ 8 ]. Additionally, after exploring the multimorbidity patterns of Chinese elderly through multiple methods, the consistent multimorbidity patterns were identified, one of which was hypertension, diabetes and dyslipidemia. Hypertensive patients are more likely to exhibit additional MS components and are at a higher risk of developing CKD. However, while most studies have focused on the association between the number of MS components and CKD in the general population, few have investigated the relationship between specific combinations of MS components and the risk of incident CKD in hypertensive patients. Therefore, to strengthen the prevention and control of components of MS in the management of hypertensive patients, further research is needed to clarify the association between specific MS components coexisting with hypertension and the risk of CKD. In the study, we conducted a large- sample, community-based, longitudinal cohort study. This study not only examined the association between the number of MS components and incident CKD in elderly hypertensive individuals but also evaluated the relationships between different MS component patterns and the risk of CKD. Methods Study population and design The study population consisted of hypertensive patients who received national basic public health services for hypertension management and underwent elderly physical examinations at all community health service centers in Minhang District, Shanghai, China, between 2020 and 2023. Initially, 67,525 hypertensive patients were included at baseline. Exclusion criteria were applied as follows: individuals aged outside the range of 60–79 years ( n = 6,770), those with missing baseline data ( n = 691), and those with only one physical examination record ( n = 5,350). Additionally, 3,244 subjects were excluded due to a baseline glomerular filtration rate (GFR) of &lt; 60 mL/min/1.73 m². After applying these exclusion criteria, 51,470 subjects were included in the final analysis (Fig. 1 ). Data collection At baseline, participants underwent hypertension management assessments, which included the collection of demographic characteristics, medical history, and lifestyle factors such as smoking, drinking, and physical activity status. The first physical examination attended by the study population during 2020–2023, which consisted of measurement of anthropometric indices and collection of biological specimens, was used as the baseline. Waist circumference was measured in a horizontal plane midway between the inferior margin of the ribs and the superior border of the iliac crest. Blood pressure was measured by trained technicians using electronic sphygmomanometers. After a 12-hour overnight fast, plasma concentrations of glucose, triglycerides, and high-density lipoprotein (HDL) cholesterol were measured. After that, the occurrence of CKD was tracked through annual physical examinations for each participant. For individuals who developed CKD, the date of the first physical examination indicating CKD was used as the cutoff date; for those who did not develop CKD, the date of their last physical examination was used as the cutoff date. Definition of metabolic syndrome The components of MS included hypertension, elevated fasting blood glucose, elevated waist circumference, and dyslipidemia (defined as either elevated triglycerides or reduced HDL-C). According to the 2022 Chinese guidelines for the prevention and treatment of type 2 diabetes mellitus [ 4 ], MS was defined as the presence of three or more of the following components: (1) Abdominal obesity, defined as a waist circumference ≥ 90 cm for males or ≥ 85 cm for females; (2) Elevated fasting glucose, defined as a fasting serum glucose ≥ 6.1mmol/L or previous diagnosis of diabetes mellitus (DM); (3) Hypertension, defined as a systolic blood pressure (SBP) ≥ 130 mmHg, a diastolic blood pressure (DBP) ≥ 85 mmHg, or current use of antihypertensive medications; (4) Hypertriglyceridemia, defined as a serum triglyceride concentration ≥ 1.70mmol/L; and (5) Low HDL cholesterol (HDL-C), defined as a serum HDL cholesterol concentration &lt; 1.04mmol/L. Definition of incident CKD and hypertension grades CKD was defined as estimated glomerular filtration rate (eGFR) &lt; 60 ml/min/1.73m 2 , calculated using the 2009 Chronic Kidney Disease Epidemiology Collaboration (CKD-EPI) equation [ 9 ]. Hypertension was classified according to the Chinese Guidelines for the Prevention and Treatment of Hypertension (2024 revision) with the following grades: Controlled BP was defined as SBP &lt; 140 mmHg and/or DBP &lt; 90 mmHg; Grade 1 was defined as SBP 140–159 mmHg and/or DBP 90–99 mmHg; Grade 2 was defined as SBP 160–179 mmHg and/or DBP 100–109 mmHg; Grade 3 was defined as SBP &gt; = 180 mmHg and/or DBP &gt; = 110 mmHg. Statistical analyses Data were analyzed according to variable characteristics. Continuous variables were expressed as medians with interquartile ranges (IQRs), while categorical variables were presented as frequencies and percentages. Group comparisons between participants with and without CKD were performed using Wilcoxon rank-sum tests for continuous variables and chi-square tests for categorical variables, as appropriate. For MS component classification, hypertriglyceridemia and low HDL-C were collectively categorized as dyslipidemia. Kaplan-Meier survival curves were generated to compare CKD development among MS groups, with statistical significance evaluated using log-rank tests. Cox proportional hazards models were employed to assess the association between MS components and CKD risk. Two models were constructed: Model 1 was unadjusted, while Model 2 adjusted for age, sex, baseline eGFR, smoking status, alcohol consumption, physical activity, and systolic blood pressure. Results were expressed as hazard ratios (HRs) with 95% confidence intervals (CIs). Additional analyses examined associations between specific MS component patterns and CKD risk using similar Cox models. The P value for trend was obtained by directly including the number of components as an ordinal categorical variable in the regression model. All analyses were performed using SAS version 9.4 (SAS Institute), with statistical significance set at P &lt; 0.05 (two-tailed). Result Baseline characteristics Table 1 showed the baseline clinical and biochemical characteristics of the participants stratified by CKD development status. Among 51,470 hypertensive patients followed for an average of 1.92 years, 2,901 (5.64%) developed CKD during the 3-year follow-up period. Compared to non-CKD patients, those who developed CKD were significantly older and had higher body mass index (BMI), systolic BP, FBG, TG levels, and WC, but lower HDL levels (all P &lt; 0.05). The prevalence of metabolic abnormalities was significantly lower in the CKD group versus the non-CKD group: elevated FBG (42.99% vs. 45.85%), elevated TG (37.76% vs. 43.61%), reduced HDL (12.59% vs. 17.20%), elevated WC (47.80% vs. 52.91%), and metabolic syndrome (44.29% vs. 51.36%) (all P &lt; 0.01). The difference in the distribution of hypertension grades between the two groups was statistically significant ( P &lt; 0.01). Among CKD patients, the prevalence of MS components was: 30.09% with three components, 17.10% with four components, and 4.17% with all five components at baseline, showing a significantly different distribution pattern compared to non-CKD patients ( P &lt; 0.01). Associations between MS and incident CKD Table 2 showed the incidence rates and HRs of CKD associated with MS. In the study population, the CKD incidence rate was significantly higher in patients with MS (33.77 per 1000 person-years) compared to those without MS (25.67 per 1000 person-years). After multivariate adjustment, MS was associated with a significantly increased risk of CKD (HR = 1.15; 95% CI: 1.07–1.24), using MS-free participants as the reference group. A dose-response relationship was observed between the number of MS components and CKD risk. Compared to hypertensive patients without additional MS components, those with 3, 4, and 5 MS components had progressively higher risks of developing CKD, with HRs of 1.17 (95% CI: 1.05–1.31), 1.27 (95% CI: 1.12–1.44), and 1.37 (95% CI: 1.13–1.68), respectively ( P for trend &lt; 0.01). Figures 2 and 3 displayed the Kaplan-Meier cumulative incidence curves for CKD stratified by the number of MS components. The log-rank test revealed significant differences in CKD incidence among groups with varying numbers of MS components ( P &lt; 0.01). Notably, groups with more MS components demonstrated steeper cumulative incidence curves, indicating faster progression to CKD. Associations between MS components patterns and incident CKD Table 3 presented the associations between each MS component and incident CKD. In multivariate-adjusted models, except reduced HDL-C, hypertensive individuals exhibiting any single additional metabolic abnormality including elevated FBG, increased TG and elevated WC were associations with CKD ( P &lt; 0.05). The strongest association was observed for elevated WC (HR = 1.17; 95% CI: 1.09–1.26; P &lt; 0.01). Table 4 presented the results of the accumulation of each component of the MS contributes to the development of CKD. Among hypertensive patients stratified by the number of MS components, we observed a gradual increase in CKD incidence density corresponding with the number of components. Patients with all five MS components showed the highest CKD incidence density (38.69 per 1000 person-years). In multivariate-adjusted models, three MS component patterns were significantly associated with increased CKD risk. The strongest association was observed for the pattern that included all MS components (HR = 1.37; 95% CI: 1.20–1.54; P &lt; 0.01), followed by the combination of elevated WC and dyslipidemia (HR = 1.20; 95% CI: 1.05–1.38; P &lt; 0.01). Among patients with just one additional MS component, only elevated WC demonstrated a consistently significant association with CKD risk in both unadjusted and adjusted analyses (HR = 1.17; 95% CI: 1.03–1.34; P &lt; 0.05), although the criteria for the determination of metabolic syndrome were not yet met. Discussion This large community-based cohort study investigated the association between MS components and CKD risk in elderly Chinese hypertensive patients. The study showed that the risk of CKD incidence among hypertensive patients was strongly associated with MS, and CKD incidence rates increased with a higher number of MS components. In addition, MS components, including elevated TG, elevated FBG and elevated WC were associated with CKD in patients with hypertension. Furthermore, in the study of the accumulation of components of MS and CKD, hypertensive patients showed significantly increased CKD risk even when only exhibiting elevated WC as an additional component, although the 2 components did not yet meet the criteria for determining MS. In recent years, with the expanding role of primary healthcare institutions in hypertension management, identifying CKD risk factors in this population has become crucial for comprehensive disease control. However, research specifically examining the association between MS and CKD in hypertensive patients remains limited. A Chinese cross-sectional study of 2,484 adults reported independent associations between CKD prevalence and elevated TG, elevated FBG, and hypertension severity in hypertensive patients, though it found no significant associations with WC or HDL-C [ 10 ]. In general population, the associations between MS and its individual component with CKD have been reported in several studies. A Chinese national cohort study of middle-aged and elderly adults [ 11 ] and a 10-year prospective Korean Ansan-Ansung cohort study [ 6 ] both reported significantly higher CKD risk in individuals with MS compared to those without, consistent with our findings. In addition, individual MS components have also been independently linked to CKD development. The Korean Ansan-Ansung study identified elevated TG, low HDL-C, hypertension, and hyperglycemia as independent CKD risk factors [ 6 ]. Similarly, a Taiwanese retrospective cohort found significant associations between CKD and central obesity, elevated TG, and low HDL-C [ 5 ]. A meta-analysis of Chinese populations confirmed that all five MS components independently predicted CKD risk, with cumulative effects observed with increasing numbers of components [ 11 ]. However, few studies have described the relationship between the different component patterns of MS and CKD. Therefore, this study was analyzed based on different MS component patterns, and the results showed that most MS component combinations were associated with increased CKD risk, except for the pattern of hypertension, elevated FBG and elevated WC. WC is a simple method to assess abdominal obesity that is easy to standardize and clinically apply [ 12 , 13 ]. Consistent with our finding, a recent review of obesity and CKD suggested that obesity was a strong risk factor for kidney disease [ 14 ], and another prospective study from Korea with a 6-year follow-up showed that WC was a risk factor for the development of renal function decline [ 15 ]. The study showed that hypertensive patients were at increased risk for CKD accompanied only by elevated WC, although the 2 components did not yet meet the criteria for determining MS. Elevated WC, as a component of MS, was found in our study to be associated with CKD in patients with hypertension. Previous studies have shown that the underlying mechanisms of obesity and CKD were complex and involve several biological pathways, including hemodynamic changes [ 16 ], abnormal lipid metabolism [ 17 ], inappropriate activation of the renin-angiotensin-aldosterone system [ 18 ], and dysregulations of the hormone response [ 19 ]. The activation of RAAS in obese patients leads to hemodynamic changes, which causes glomerular hyperfiltration, compensated glomerulomegaly, subsequent focal glomerulosclerosis, and promoting the progression of MS related renal damage [ 18 , 20 ]. Hormone regulation disorder and ectopic lipid deposition in obese patients can directly or indirectly affect the structure and function of renal intrinsic cells [ 19 ]. Also, Obesity and metabolic abnormalities often coexist. Plasma concentrations of some proinflammatory adipokines are elevated in individuals with the MS, which contributes to the progression of CKD [ 21 , 22 ]. Our study has several strengths. First, it was a community-based cohort study of the elderly population conducted as a national project that was managed systematically. The use of standardized medical examination data enabled comprehensive adjustment for potential confounders in our analyses. Second, our longitudinal design provides stronger evidence for causal relationships between metabolic syndrome components and CKD development compared to cross-sectional studies. To the best of our knowledge, this is the first cohort study to investigate the risk of CKD in patients with hypertension and other metabolic syndrome components. Lastly, we focused on the analysis of the association of different combinations of components with CKD based on the distribution of MS components. These efforts show that MS management in patients with hypertension should be emphasized, and more attention should be focused on obesity during risk management in order to prevent CKD. The present study of community-based health management of hypertension is informative for the prevention and control of CKD in this population, but several limitations should be acknowledged. First, eGFR was estimated using the CKD-EPI equation rather than measured directly, although this method has been widely validated in Chinese populations. In addition, the definition of CKD used only eGFR without other CKD diagnostic markers (e.g., albuminuria), potentially underestimating CKD incidence. Future studies should examine MS associations with broader renal damage indicators. Second, our findings from elderly Chinese participants may not be generalizable to other ethnic groups or younger populations. Third, participants in this cohort who underwent regular health exams were relatively healthy, which might lead to a selection bias and subsequent underestimation of risks. Finally, while we adjusted for major confounders, residual confounding may persist due to unmeasured medication use (e.g., hypoglycemics, antihypertensives including ACE inhibitors, ARB and ARNI, and lipid-lowering agents), which could affect CKD progression [ 23 ]. Conclusions This cohort study of hypertensive patients demonstrated that a clear dose-response relationship between accumulating MS components and increasing CKD risk. Particularly, elevated WC in hypertension patients was significantly associated with a higher risk of CKD. Thus, among all the MS components, obesity should be concerned during the risk management of hypertension.
