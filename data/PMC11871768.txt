To view a copy of this licence, visit http://creativecommons.org/licenses/by-nc-nd/4.0/ . Background Potentially nephrotoxic drugs play an important role in acute kidney injury (AKI) in children. In the previous pharmacovigilance study, we found a statistical correlation between diazepam and AKI in children, but the causality needs to be further verified. Methods An observational cohort study was conducted to investigate the association between diazepam exposure and the risk of AKI in hospitalized children. The exposed group received diazepam while the control group did not. The outcome was AKI in accordance with the Kidney Disease Improving Global Outcomes (KDIGO) guidelines. Logistic regression was used to explore the association between diazepam exposure and the risk of AKI in children after adjusting for covariates. Results A total of 3018 hospitalized children were included, 148 in the diazepam group and 2870 in the control group. After adjusting for covariates, diazepam exposure was associated with an increased risk of AKI in children (OR = 1.85, 95%CI: 1.11–3.09, P = 0.019). Multivariate logistic regression showed that age ( P &lt; 0.001), low baseline serum creatinine ( P &lt; 0.001), chronic kidney disease ( P = 0.039), diuretics ( P = 0.009), Non-steroidal anti-inflammatory drugs (NSAIDs, P = 0.046), proton pump inhibitors ( P = 0.007) and vasoactive drugs ( P = 0.007) were risk factors for diazepam-associated AKI in children. Conclusions The use of diazepam in children is associated with an increased risk of AKI. The risk factors for diazepam-associated AKI in children included age, low baseline serum creatinine, chronic kidney disease, diuretics, NSAIDs, proton pump inhibitors, and vasoactive drugs. Studies have found that 20-25% of annual hospitalizations for AKI are caused by medications [ 5 , 6 ]. Children are more prone to drug-induced kidney injury (DIKI) than adults due to physiological differences, such as glomerular filtration rate, clearance and expression of cytochrome P450 enzyme in liver and kidney, lacking of clinical trials for children and dose reduction for adults, etc [ 7 , 8 ]. A systematic review revealed that the incidence of DIKI in children was 18.2% [ 9 ]. Importantly, nephrotoxic drugs are an increasingly common cause of hospitalized AKI, especially in pediatric patients [ 10 , 11 ]. DIKI presents several challenges for clinicians, mainly related to the difficulty of detecting potentially nephrotoxic drugs promptly [ 12 ]. In our previous study based on signal mining in the FDA Adverse Event Reporting System database, we found several potentially nephrotoxic drugs, including diazepam [ 13 ]. Meanwile, However, since the results of signal mining only indicate that the drug has a statistical association with adverse reactions and the causal relationship remains unknown [ 14 ]. Meanwhile, there have been case reports of kidney injury caused by diazepam [ 15 , 16 ]. Therefore, it is particularly important to conduct a cohort study based on electronic medical records to verify the association between diazepam and AKI in children. This will provide evidence for the prevention of DIKI in children, and promote rational drug use in children. Methods Study design, population, and data source The retrospective cohort study was conducted among hospitalized children in West China Second Hospital, Sichuan University which is the largest women’s and children’s hospital located in western China. The study population was children aged 1 month to 18 years who were hospitalized for more than 2 days from January 1, 2020 to December 31, 2022. We excluded patients: (1) serum creatinine was tested less than twice during hospitalization; (2) with no baseline serum creatinine; (3) with underlying kidney disease (congenital renal insufficiency, kidney transplantation history, end-stage renal disease, cystic fibrosis, membrane oxygenation, dialysis); (4) with no serum creatinine within 48 h or 7 days. Data collection The relevant information of patients was collected through electronic medical records, including (1) general information, such as age, gender, treatment days, ICU admission, and serum creatinine; (2) medical information, such as disease diagnosis, ICD-10 code; (3) prescription information such as medication name, prescribed time, and prescribed status. Outcome definition, drug exposure, and comorbidities AKI was defined as an increase in serum creatinine more than 1.5 times baseline serum creatinine within 7 days or an increase in serum creatinine greater than 26.5 µmol/L within 48 h, according to the Kidney Disease Improving Global Outcomes (KDIGO) guidelines. Based to the severity of AKI, it was divided into Stage 1, Stage 2 and Stage 3 [ 17 ]. Baseline serum creatinine was defined as the mean of serum creatinine in the 30 days before admission. Diazepam exposure was defined as the use of diazepam during hospitalization. Diazepam is commonly used as an antiepileptic and anticonvulsive drug in children. All drugs prescribed during hospitalization were classified based on the anatomical therapeutic chemical classification system. The classification and specific names of nephrotoxic drugs used in combination are shown in Table S1 . The comorbidities were defined by dichotomous covariates according to the ICD-10 diagnostic codes (Table S2 ). The comorbidities analyzed included tumor, infection, sepsis, respiratory dysfunction, and chronic kidney disease. Sample size Based on the sample size calculation for the cohort study, the required sample size for this study was determined. According to existing literature, the incidence of drug-induced kidney injury is 18.2%, while the incidence in the control group is 7.0% [ 9 , 18 ]. The significance level (Type I error) was set at 0.05, and the statistical power was set at 80%. Since this study was conducted during patients’ hospitalization, the issue of loss to follow-up was not considered. As a result, the sample size calculated for each group was 133 patients. In the results of main analysis, the incidence of AKI in the diazepam exposure group was 20.3%, while the incidence in the control group was 6.2%. Considering the significance level (Type I error) of 0.05, the calculated statistical power of the study is approximately 98.8%, which is well-equipped to identify significant findings. In the results of propensity score matching analysis, the incidence of AKI in the diazepam exposure group was 17.8%, while the incidence in the control group was 7.4%. Considering the significance level (Type I error) of 0.05, the calculated statistical power of the study is approximately 84.8%, which is also well-equipped to identify significant findings. Statistical analysis Continuous variables were presented as a mean (standard deviation [SD]) or a median (interquartile range [IQR]), and categorical variables were presented as a number (percentage). The group differences for continuous variables were analyzed by t-test or Wilcoxon test, whereas categorical variables were analyzed by χ 2 test. Based on risk factors for drug-induced kidney injury and consultation with clinical and statistics experts [ 9 , 19 ], confounding factors included in this study included gender, age, baseline serum creatinine, comorbidities (tumor, infection, sepsis, respiratory dysfunction, epilepsy, chronic kidney disease), and concomitant nephrotoxic drugs (antibacterial drugs, antineoplastic drugs, diuretics, NSAIDs, antifungal agents, intravenous contrast agents, vasoactive drugs, ACEI/ARB, proton pump inhibitors, antiviral agents, immunosuppressive agents). Multivariate logistic regression was used to verify the association between diazepam exposure and the risk of AKI in children after adjusting for pre-specified covariates. Then, Subgroup analyses of the association between diazepam exposure and AKI in children were conducted by gender, age, ICU admission, comorbidities, and concomitant nephrotoxic drugs to examine whether grouping factors might be modifying the effect. We conducted sensitivity analysis to verify the robustness of the results of the primary analyses through propensity score matching. We used nearest neighbor matching, matching exposed patients to comparison patients 1:4 with a 0.05 caliper. The matching factors were confounding factors included in this study. Multivariate logistic regression analysis was used to investigate the risk factors of diazepam-associated AKI in children. Statistical analyses were performed using Stata 15.1 software, and a P -value of less than 0.05 was considered to be statistically significant. Ethics approval The Medical Ethics Committee of West China Second University Hospital, Sichuan University approved the study protocol and waived patient informed consent. Results Study population A total of 3018 patients among the 29,433 hospitalized children were eligible for inclusion in the study (Fig. 1 ). Of these patients, 1744 (57.8%) were male, with a median (interquartile range, IQR) age of 5 (2–8) years and a median (IQR) treatment duration of 10 (6–21) days. Among the included patients, 148 patients (4.9%) were prescribed diazepam during hospitalization and 208 patients (6.9%) developed AKI. The demographic and clinical characteristics of the children in the study stratified by diazepam exposure are summarized in Table 1 . The incidence of AKI in the diazepam group was higher than that in the control group (20.3% vs. 6.2%), Moreover, most AKI patients were stage 1. In comparison to the control group, the diazepam group exhibited longer treatment days (25 days vs. 10 days), lower rates of tumor (20.9% vs. 53.9%), higher rates of infection (46.6% vs. 27.0%), sepsis (40.5% vs. 15.6%), respiratory dysfunction (33.8% vs. 12.4%), and CKD (4.7% vs. 2.0%). The most frequently prescribed concomitant drugs were antibacterial agents, antineoplastic drugs, and diuretics. Association between diazepam exposure and risk of AKI in children Without adjustment for confounders, the results showed that diazepam exposure was associated with a significantly increased risk of AKI (OR = 3.85, 95%CI: 2.51–5.90, P &lt; 0.001). After adjusting for confounders, the results showed that diazepam exposure was associated with a significantly increased risk of AKI in children (adjusted OR = 1.85, 95%CI: 1.11–3.09, P = 0.019) (Table 2 ). The results of the subgroup analysis are summarized in Table 3 . Notably, a significantly higher odds ratio (OR) for diazepam exposure was observed in children aged 2–12 years, with a significant interaction effect ( P for interaction = 0.019). Propensity score matching methods generated well-balanced pairs of diazepam group and control group (Table S3 ). There were 135 patients in the matched diazepam group and 540 patients in the control group. Propensity score matching analysis demonstrated that diazepam exposure was associated with a significantly increased risk of acute kidney injury (OR = 2.70, 95% CI: 1.57–4.67, P &lt; 0.001), indicating that the results of the main results were robust. Risk factors for diazepam-associated AKI in children Multivariate logistic regression results showed that age (OR = 1.12, 95% CI: 1.07–1.17, P &lt; 0.001), low baseline serum creatinine (OR = 0.95, 95% CI: 0.94–0.97, P &lt; 0.001), chronic kidney disease (OR = 2.59, 95% CI: 1.05–6.39, P = 0.039), diuretics (OR = 1.72, 95%CI: 1.15–2.58, P = 0.009), Non-steroidal anti-inflammatory drugs (NSAIDs; OR = 1.45, 95% CI: 1.01–2.09, P = 0.046), proton pump inhibitors (OR = 1.78 95%CI: 1.17–2.71, P = 0.007) and vasoactive drugs (OR = 1.88, 95%CI: 1.19–2.98, P = 0.007) were risk factors for diazepam-associated AKI in children (Table 4 ). Discussion Nephrotoxic drugs are an increasingly common cause of AKI in hospitalized patients, especially in pediatrics. The timely identification of potentially nephrotoxic drugs presents a significant challenge for clinicians on prevention of DIKI [ 12 ]. Based on the potentially nephrotoxic drugs identified through signal mining in our previous study [ 13 ], this study used electronic medical records to conduct a cohort study to verify the association between diazepam (a potentially nephrotoxic drug) and AKI in children. To the best of our knowledge, this is the first cohort study to focus on the association between diazepam and AKI in children. Diazepam, one of the benzodiazepines, is frequently used clinically to treat anxiety, insomnia, epilepsy, febrile convulsions, and muscle spasms [ 20 ]. Common adverse effects of diazepam include drowsiness, dizziness, fatigue, and long-term continuous use of diazepam can lead to dependence or addiction, excluding kidney injury [ 21 , 22 ]. However, our cohort study based on three years of electronic medical records found that diazepam exposure was significantly associated with an increased risk of AKI in children (OR = 1.85, 95% CI: 1.11–3.09), confirming the results of signal mining in our previous study. Although our study confirmed an association between diazepam exposure and the risk of AKI in children, research discussing this relationship is limited. Currently, there are few studies on the association between diazepam and kidney injury. Based on a new quantitative algorithm for DIKI signal detection, Yu et al. [ 23 ] found that diazepam was a potential new signaling drug for DIKI in children (OR = 1.61, 95% CI: 1.43–1.47). Moreover, a nationwide population-based retrospective cohort study showed that diazepam was significantly associated with an increased risk of chronic kidney disease in adults (HR = 1.63, 95CI%: 1.53–1.74) [ 24 ]. The mechanism of diazepam-related AKI is unclear. One possible mechanism is that propylene glycol is included in the parenteral formulations of diazepam, which may lead to AKI and proximal renal tubule injury [ 25 ]. Meanwhile, animal research found that diazepam has a direct effect on the renal tubule transport mechanism [ 26 ]. In addition, our study found that age, low baseline serum creatinine, chronic kidney disease, diuretics, NSAIDs, proton pump inhibitors, and vasoactive drugs were risk factors for diazepam-associated AKI in children, which was consistent with the findings of several previous studies [ 27 – 33 ]. Based on the findings of this study, clinicians should consider the risk of kidney injury when treating children with diazepam. For high-risk populations, such as age, low baseline serum creatinine, and combination of nephrotoxic drugs (diuretics, proton pump inhibitors, etc.), adjustment of medication regimen should be considered, and renal function should be monitored regularly during treatment. Our study has some strengths. First, based on our previous study, this study conducted a cohort study through electronic medical records to verify the association between diazepam and AKI in children, which can provide reference for the prevention of DIKI and decision-making basis for clinical rational drug use in children. Second, this study used multivariate logistic regression analysis and propensity score matching to control confounding factors, ensuring the authenticity and reliability of the results. Finally, AKI was diagnosed based on a change in serum creatinine levels rather than the ICD-10 code, as a diagnosis based on the ICD-10 code tends to produce more false negative diagnoses [ 34 ]. Meanwhile, several limitations of our study should be considered when interpreting the findings. For one thing, we did not use urine output levels for this cohort to identify AKI, which may have led to some patients not being identified. For another, we did not have long-term follow-up data for children, which prevented us from exploring the effects of long-term diazepam use on the kidneys. Meanwhile, future studies may consider the potential effects of diazepam dosage and different administration modes on AKI risk. Moreover, this study is a single-center retrospective study, and the extrapolation of the findings is limited. Therefore, it is necessary to conduct further multi-center, large-sample, and prospective studies to verify the causal relationships. Conclusion The use of diazepam is associated with an increased risk of AKI in children. The risk factors for diazepam-associated AKI in children included age, low baseline serum creatinine, diuretics, proton pump inhibitors, and vasoactive drugs.
