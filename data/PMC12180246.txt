Abstract Background This study investigated and compared the abilities of the triglyceride glucose index (TyG) and its correlated factors involving TyG-body mass index (TyG-BMI), TyG-waist-to-height ratio (TyG-WHtR), and TyG-waist circumference (TyG-WC) to predict cardiovascular disease (CVD) among Chinese postmenopausal women. This topic has not been adequately explored in the existing literature. Methods This prospective study included 1110 Chinese postmenopausal women, stratified into the CVD group and the non-CVD group. The TyG index and its correlated components (TyG, TyG-BMI, TyG-WHtR, and TyG-WC) were calculated. The primary endpoint was CVD. Results Across a 12-year follow-up period, 76 (6.84%) CVDs were documented. The TyG index, in collaboration with various indicators of obesity, demonstrated a robust positive correlation with the risk of CVD. In comparison to other TyG indices, TyG-WC was the strongest predictor for CVD (HR: 2.61, 95%CI:1.64–4.14; P &lt; 0.001), and the TyG-WHtR index exhibited the strongest diagnostic value in identifying CVD (AUC: 0.632, 95%CI: 0.603–0.660; P &lt; 0.001). Incorporating TyG-WHtR into the base model significantly enhanced incremental risk stratification for CVD (NRI: 0.115, 95%CI: 0.040–0.190, P &lt; 0.05; AIC: 996; BIC: 1041; Harrell’s C-index: 0.73). Decision curve analysis (DCA) suggested that TyG, TyG-WHtR, and TyG-WC can provide significant clinical benefits for Chinese postmenopausal women. The sensitivity analyses have demonstrated the robustness of these results. Conclusions The TyG index and its correlated factors can effectively predict CVD in Chinese postmenopausal women. TyG-WHtR has the most potent ability to predict CVD among postmenopausal women. Supplementary Information The online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1186/s12944-025-02643-1. Introduction CVD represents a primary contributor to morbidity and mortality among women globally. Following menopause, there is a notable decline in estrogen levels, which substantially elevates the risk of developing CVD [ 1 ]. Insulin resistance (IR) is a crucial pathological factor in numerous metabolic diseases, affecting glucose and lipid metabolism and triggering systemic inflammation and oxidative stress. It has become a significant surrogate marker for judging insulin resistance and the related risk of CVD [ 2 ]. Alongside the hyperinsulin clamp, the gold standard for figuring out IR, this alternative method offers a more cost-effective and straightforward approach. The latest research has suggested that the TyG index, along with obesity markers such as waist circumference (WC), waist-to-height ratio (WHtR), and body mass index (BMI), proposed as newfangled biomarkers for IR [ 3 , 4 ], showed greater predictive value for CVD mortality and risk in the American population [ 5 ]. Nonetheless, there remain critical gaps in translating these findings to Chinese menopausal women. Abdominal obesity is a key risk factor for cardiometabolic diseases, with BMI, WC, WHtR, and body fat percentages serving as simple markers for screening. Asians are at higher risk of adverse events at lower BMI cutoffs compared to international standards [ 6 ]. Optimal cut-off values for WC differ among ethnicities, which are generally smaller in Chinese women than those in non-Asian populations [ 7 ]. What’s more, postmenopausal women are significantly affected by obesity, exhibiting higher rates of severe obesity in comparison to their male counterparts. Lowered estrogen levels increase the vulnerability of postmenopausal women to dysregulated lipid metabolism, fat redistribution, and visceral fat accumulation [ 8 ]. CVD risk showed a stronger correlation with body adiposity index in Chinese men, while it was more consistently correlated with WHtR and WC in Chinese women [ 9 ]. Current models fail to adequately consider the physiological changes related to menopause that are specific to Chinese women. Liu Qian and colleagues discovered that a high baseline TyG index in Chinese postmenopausal women is associated with an increased risk of future CVD [ 10 ]. There is no systematic comparison of various TyG-derived indicators to confirm their stability and predictive capability in Chinese postmenopausal women. These limitations leave physicians without a TyG and TyG-derived indicators prediction tool appropriate for use in clinical practice for Chinese menopausal women. This study is committed to collecting 12-year follow-up data to verify the stability and predictive power of TyG-derived indicators in Chinese menopausal women and establish risk stratification standards and clinical cut-off values suitable for the Chinese female population, addressing a critical gap in existing literature. Methods Study population The population was recruited from the prospective APAC Study, which aims to research the incidence and correlations of asymptomatic polyvascular abnormalities (APA) among the general public. The study included 1143 postmenopausal women randomly selected from the Kailuan population in Tangshan, Hebei, China, from 2010 to 2011. The exclusion criteria consisted of a history of coronary heart disease, cerebral stroke, and transient ischemic attacks. Participants were monitored through face-to-face interviews at routine medical examinations every two years until death or December 31, 2022. In instances where face-to-face meetings were not feasible, the participants were monitored for two years utilizing available medical insurance records. Furthermore, follow-up procedures were extended as supplementary clinical data became accessible [ 11 ]. This research was carried out following the strict fundamentals of the Declaration of Helsinki. All participants submitted their written informed consent. The flowchart of this study is shown in Fig. 1 . After removing 33 patients, 4 of whom were missing the date of baseline information, 3 of whom used hormone replacement therapy (HRT), and 26 of whom experienced menopause caused by surgery, summing up to 1110 individuals were selected for this study. Data collection and definition Healthcare professionals, including medical doctors and nurses, collected and documented all demographic and clinical information. All participants were asked to complete a set of combined interview questionnaires related to demographics, lifestyle (smoker or drinker), data on age at menopause, educational background (primary school, middle school, and high school or above), and medical history. The individual reported having reached natural menopause, characterized by a complete absence of menstrual periods for at least 12 consecutive months. In this study, the identification of CVD was a combination of myocardial infarction (MI) and stroke, collected through biennial questionnaires and discharge records from 11 hospitals. ICD-10 codes I21 and I63, I60, or I61 were used to identify myocardial infarction and stroke, respectively. All participants in the APAC trial were enrolled in the Hospital Discharge Register to track CVD and the Municipal Social Insurance Institution. Three doctors who were not aware of the study’s concept evaluated medical records of probable CVD occurrences. Height, body mass index (BMI), waist circumference (WC), diastolic blood pressure (DBP), and systolic blood pressure (SBP) were obtained via a physical check-up. Laboratory findings comprised triglyceride (TG), fasting plasma glucose (FPG), levels of total cholesterol (TC), low-density lipoprotein-cholesterol (LDL-C), high-density lipoprotein-cholesterol (HDL-C), the high-sensitivity C-reactive protein (hs-CRP), and creatinine. Calculation of TyG index and related factors The TyG index serves as a quantifiable gauge of insulin resistance by integrating FPG and triglyceride concentrations. Fasting blood glucose (FBG) levels and triglyceride concentrations were evaluated at the initial assessment. Weight and height were quantified to figure out BMI (kg/m²). The TyG index was quantified as ln [triglycerides (mg/dl) × glucose (mg/dl)/2]. The TyG-BMI is calculated by multiplying the TyG index by the BMI. The TyG-WC was gauged utilizing the following formula: TyG × waist circumference. The value of the WHtR was calculated as waist circumference/height, while the TyG-WHtR was calculated by multiplying the TyG value by the WHtR [ 12 ]. Statistical analysis Continuous variables were defined as mean ± standard deviation (SD) and assessed utilizing the independent sample t-test for normally distributed data. In contrast, skewed distributed variables were reported as medians with 25th and 75th percentiles and examined for Mann-Whitney U tests. Categorical variables were reported as frequencies and percentages (%), and comparisons were conducted employing the chi-squared test. A Receiver Operating Characteristic (ROC) curve assessment to disclose the effectiveness of TyG and TyG-related factors in predicting CVD among postmenopausal women was fitted. Participants were classified based on their TyG values and corresponding metrics. The groups included: high TyG, high TyG-WC, high TyG-WHtR, high TyG-BMI, low TyG, low TyG-WC, low TyG-WHtR, and low TyG-BMI. These classifications were finalized by means of specific cutoff values. The Kaplan-Meier curves were implemented to evaluate the relationship between incident CVD and TyG index, TyG-BMI, TyG-WHtR, and TyG-WC among the Chinese postmenopausal population, with the log-rank test employed for statistical evaluation. To probe the link between CVD and TyG as well as TyG-related factors, Cox proportional hazard models and restricted cubic splines (RCS) were employed for the exploratory study. A univariate Cox regression analysis to detect possible risk elements linked to CVD was conducted. Subsequently, factors significantly associated ( P &lt; 0.05) were included in multivariate Cox regression, adjusting for age, education level, smoking status, hypertension, hyperlipidemia, diabetes, creatinine levels, and hs-CRP. All the adjusted variables were evaluated for collinearity, and no clear evidence of multicollinearity was detected (the all-variance inflation factor (VIF) of the included variables was &lt; 2) (Supplementary Fig. 1 ). The net reclassification improvement (NRI) and integrated discrimination improvement (IDI) were applied to estimate the potential enhancement in predictive capability attained through incorporating the TyG index, TyG-WC, TyG-WHtR, and TyG-BMI into the original model. DCA was utilized to evaluate clinical utility. Akaike’s Information Criterion (AIC), Bayesian Information Criterion (BIC), and Harrell’s concordance index (C-index) were calculated to evaluate model fit and predictive performance (Supplementary Table 1 ). The base model was adjusted for age, education level, smoking status, hypertension, hyperlipidemia, diabetes, creatinine levels, and hs-CRP. This study implemented a dual-strategy approach to control potential confounding biases: Firstly, to address survivorship bias, we re-conducted cohort analyses by excluding participants who experienced CVD within the first two years of follow-up ( n = 10), thereby assessing the impact of early events on causal inference. Secondly, to control for time-varying confounding factors, we performed landmark analysis by resetting the follow-up starting point to the end of the second year, retaining only those participants who remained event-free at this time point, and subsequently reconstructed Cox proportional hazards models. Statistical analyses were executed with the aid of SPSS version 26.0 (IBM Corporation, Armonk, NY, USA) and R version 4.1.2 (R Foundation for Statistical Computing, Vienna, Austria). Statistical significance was defined as a P -value below 0.05. Results Baseline characteristics of participants The characteristics of CVD patients and non-CVD patients were contrasted in Table 1 . Compared to non-CVD individuals, women with CVD were prone to be older, self-reported diabetes, hypertension, dyslipidemia, thicker WC, higher BMI, WHtR, and elevated SBP and DBP, featured greater values of TG, FPG ( P &lt; 0.05). Meanwhile, CVD patients’ TyG index and TyG-related factors were meaningfully increased ( P &lt; 0.05). No statistically notable differences in the prevalence of smokers, drinkers, educational statements, or degrees of TC, LDL-C, HDL-C, Hs-CRP, and creatinine were observed across the groups. Diagnostic performance of TyG and its relevant factors for CVD among the postmenopausal population The Receiver Operating Characteristic (ROC) curve analysis revealed that both TyG-WHtR and TyG-WC demonstrated superior diagnostic efficacy for CVD in postmenopausal women, with Area Under the Curve (AUC) statistical values of 0.632(95%CI: 0.603–0.660) and 0.619(95%CI: 0.590–0.648), trailed by TyG (AUC: 0.609, 95% CI: 0.580–0.637) and TyG-BMI (AUC: 0.599, 95% CI: 0.569–0.627) (Table 2 ). The established cutoff values for TyG-WHtR, TyG, TyG-WC, and TyG-BMI are 5.26 (sensitivity 42.11%; specificity 79.62%), 8.91 (sensitivity 55.26%; specificity 62.65%), 841.51 (sensitivity 42.11%; specificity 80.09%), and 223.69 (sensitivity 60.53%; specificity 58.77%), respectively. Postmenopausal women may be classified into distinct risk categories using these cutoff values, allowing for more targeted assessments and interventions (Table 2 ). The incidence of CVD rose in the groups with high cutoff values for TyG, TyG-WC, TyG-WHtR, and TyG-BMI (Fig. 2 ). Figure 3 demonstrates the Kaplan-Meier curves depicting the cumulative incidence rates of CVD about the TyG index and its associated factors. Variables demonstrating a significant association with CVD were integrated into multivariate Cox regression, which was adjusted for age, education level, smoking, hypertension, diabetes, dyslipidemia, creatinine, and hs-CRP. Throughout the observation period, the cumulative incidence rate of CVD exhibited a consistent pattern with elevated TyG-WC, as determined by the log-rank test ( P &lt; 0.001). This trend was also observed with TyG-WHtR ( P &lt; 0.001), TyG ( P &lt; 0.05), and TyG-BMI ( P &lt; 0.05). Associations of TyG and its relevant factors with CVD among the postmenopausal population Multivariable-adjusted restricted cubic spline analyses indicated a significant linear relationship between TyG, TyG-WC, TyG-WHtR, and TyG-BMI with total CVD (Fig. 4 ). Table 3 outlines the connections between TyG-related indexes and CVD among postmenopausal women. The findings proved a marked positive relationship between the four indices (categorical or continuous) and CVD in postmenopausal women ( P &lt; 0.05). After adjustments for age, education level, smoking, hypertension, diabetes, dyslipidemia, creatinine, and hs-CRP, all the indices clearly showed strong positive correlations with CVD ( P &lt; 0.05). TyG-WC-H (&gt; 841.51) and TyG-WHtR-H (&gt; 5.26) exhibited a superior predictive power (HR:2.30, 95%CI: 1.34–3.95; HR:2.12, 95%CI: 1.34–3.95) after adjusting for multiple covariates ( P &lt; 0.05). In considering TyG and TyG-WHtR as continuous variables, it appeared that a 1 standard deviation increase in the aggregated average is significantly correlated with the incidence of CVD in postmenopausal women (HR: 1.57, 95%CI: 1.35–1.82; HR:1.65, 95%CI: 1.14–2.37) ( P &lt; 0.05) (Table 3 ). Incremental value of TyG and its relevant factors over the original model for CVD among the postmenopausal population Table 4 demonstrated minor changes in the NRI and the IDI after incorporating data on TyG, TyG-BMI, TyG-WHtR, and TyG-WC into base model for CVD that included age, education level, smoking, hypertension, diabetes, dyslipidemia, creatinine, and hs-CRP levels in postmenopausal women. TyG, TyG-WHtR, and TyG-WC demonstrated statistically significant improvements in IDI and NRI in predicting CVD (NRI:0.105, IDI:0.013; NRI:0.064, IDI:0.008; NRI:0.115, IDI:0.008) ( P &lt; 0.05). Upon assessing the model’s goodness of fit, we found that the TyG-WHtR index showed the most significant improvement, with an AIC value of 996, a BIC value of 1041, and Harrell’s C-index value of 0.73 (Supplementary Table 1 ). Furthermore, the DCA curves indicated that TyG, TyG-WHtR, and TyG-WC offered greater clinical benefits for postmenopausal women in China (Fig. 5 ). Sensitivity analyses To assess potential bias from residual confounding due to survivorship and time, we repeated the analyses separately, excluding participants who experienced CVD events within two years from the start of follow-up. Additionally, a landmark analysis was conducted. These analyses evaluated the relationship between CVD risk and the TyG index, along with its related factors (TyG-WC, TyG-WHtR, and TyG-BMI), and confirmed similar findings (Supplementary Tables 2 and Supplementary Table 3 ). These sensitivity analyses confirmed that our results are reliable and consistent. Discussion Postmenopausal women face an increased risk of CVD due to changes in body fat, sex hormones, vascular function, and lipid levels. This risk can be influenced by HRT, mainly if initiated early [ 13 ]. However, the efficacy and advantages of HRT for preventing primary CVD in postmenopausal women are still debated [ 14 ]. Future research should concentrate on identifying predictive indicators of CVD in menopausal women and developing sex-specific therapeutic strategies to address their increased susceptibility to CVD during this stage of life. The TyG index is a reliable sign of insulin resistance and has been consistently connected to an increased risk of CVD across various studies. Research has indicated that both baseline and cumulative TyG index levels can predict future CVD events, such as stroke and myocardial infarction [ 15 , 16 ]. Sex differences were observed, with TyG showing stronger connections with CVD risk variables in women [ 17 ]. The index has also been handy in identifying CVD risk among lean postmenopausal women [ 18 ]. This study confirmed prior research that the TyG index has a marked linear relationship with CVD and may act as a predictive marker for CVD in postmenopausal women. An increase of one standard deviation in the TyG index was linked to a 57% greater risk of CVD among postmenopausal women after adjusting for age, smoking, education level, diabetes, hypertension, hyperlipidemia, hs-CRP, and creatinine, surpassing the general population rates in Iran (16%), America (23.2%), China (43%), and at Kailuan General Hospital (6%) [ 19 ]. The decline in estrogen levels during menopause can impact various factors such as blood lipid metabolism and endothelial function, leading to an increased risk of CVD. The TyG index may interact with these physiological changes, providing a more sensitive assessment of CVD risk in menopausal women than in the general population. However, prior research categorized participants into four groups (Q1, Q2, Q3, Q4) based on the quartiles of the TyG index in postmenopausal women [ 20 ]; in contrast, participants were classified into two groups according to cutoff values determined by the ROC curve. The established cutoff value for TyG in this study was 8.91. Interestingly, the ideal TyG index threshold for predicting CVD was also found to be 8.91, with a sensitivity of 58% and specificity of 58% in the Isfahan Cohort Study (ICS) [ 21 ]. A total of 5,432 individuals aged ≥ 35 years were included in the study. The results indicated that the risk of CVD predicted by TyG may not be influenced by menopause when classified according to a cutoff value. Integrating TyG-related parameters with anthropometric measures often demonstrates improved predictive capability for various metabolic and cardiovascular conditions compared to using TyG alone. These markers can potentially serve as cost-effective, non-insulin methods for assessing risk across clinical and epidemiological contexts [ 22 , 23 ]. BMI is a highly variable measurement that does not consider body fat distribution and is influenced by age, sex, and race. WC is a key indicator of abdominal fat and is essential for assessing central obesity. It also serves as a better predictor of tumors in the digestive tract, cardiovascular diseases, and metabolic disorders [ 24 – 26 ]. WHtR is a more effective measure of abdominal obesity than BMI and WC, as it integrates both waist circumference and height. Asian women are inherently prone to central obesity (accumulation of fat around the waist and abdomen) [ 27 ]. A high WHtR is associated with metabolic disorders in Chinese postmenopausal women, even in those with a normal BMI [ 28 ]. A study of 11,937 adults from the National Health and Nutrition Examination Survey showed a meaningful positive association between TyG, TyG-WC, TyG-WHtR, and TyG-BMI and CVD. The analysis of ROC curves demonstrated that TyG-WHtR and TyG-WC exhibited superior diagnostic efficacy compared to TyG alone [ 5 ]. The study utilized a cross-sectional design, which limits the capacity to ascertain causal connections between the variables. A survey by Hye-Min Park and colleagues with 7,808 participants aged 40 to 69 from the Ansung-Ansan cohort found that in non-diabetic individuals, the C-index for TyG-WHtR and TyG-WC was notably higher than for TyG and TyG-BMI [ 29 ]. However, the diabetes group had 918 participants, significantly smaller than the 6,890 in the non-diabetes group, which may have limited statistical power. This prospective study revealed analogous findings among postmenopausal participants, indicating that the associations between TyG-related elements and CVD exhibit a linear pattern. The menopausal women who eventually developed new-onset CVD had greater values of TyG-WHtR, TyG-WC, and TyG-BMI. TyG-WHtR and TyG-WC showed better diagnostic efficacy for CVD in postmenopausal women than TyG. In this study, the established cutoff values for TyG-WHtR, TyG-WC, and TyG-BMI are 5.26, 841.51, and 223.69, respectively. The incidence of CVD demonstrated an increase among groups exhibiting high cutoff values for TyG-WC, TyG-WHtR, and TyG-BMI among postmenopausal women. Indicators in this study had a lower AUC and a linear increase in HR values, especially TyG-BMI. The fat distribution in postmenopausal women shifts from the buttocks and thighs to the abdomen and internal organs, manifested as an increase in visceral fat area and an increase in waist-hip ratio [ 30 ], which may weaken the association strength between BMI (a general obesity index) and metabolic risk. This makes the predictive efficacy of TyG-BMI for CVD lower than expected. This study did not support a nonlinear increase in HR, suggesting that the TyG-related index may directly reflect the cumulative impact of metabolic load. Each increase of one unit corresponds to pathophysiological progression, resulting in no plateau period for HR and a lack of a clear threshold. Our findings align with those of previous studies on this matter. Tyg-WC, TyG-WHTR, and TyG-BMI showed a linear correlation with total CVD, myocardial infarction, and coronary heart disease. However, TyG, Tyg-WC, Tyg-WHTR, and Tyg-BMI demonstrated nonlinear associations with CVD mortality [ 12 ]. Furthermore, among the adult population in the United States and the middle-aged and elderly population in China, the AUC value range of TyG and its related indicators for CVD is 0.5–0.7 [ 12 , 31 ]. TyG-WC exhibits high sensitivity for metabolic-associated fatty liver disease (77.6%) in a pediatric population with obesity [ 32 ], but low sensitivity for CVD (42.11%) in Chinese postmenopausal women. This notable difference may stem from the TyG-WC index being affected by variations in fat distribution and metabolic status [ 33 ]. CVD in postmenopausal women results from multiple factors [ 34 ], including vascular dysfunction, plaque formation, and inflammation. However, the TyG-WC index has limited ability to capture these complex changes. TyG-WC and TyG-WHtR in Chinese postmenopausal women have low sensitivity but high specificity for CVD, enabling them to identify non-patients with a low false-positive rate accurately. They are suitable for secondary screening in high-risk populations, but may miss some early or atypical cases. The lack of studies comparing their results with other established risk prediction models in previous research limited the capacity to evaluate these indices’ clinical usefulness for CVD fully. The original model was modified for age, education level, smoking, hypertension, diabetes, dyslipidemia, creatinine, and hs-CRP, focusing on the variables with a P value below 0.05 in the final multivariate model when TyG was treated as a continuous variable. After that, the IDI and NRI metrics were used to evaluate the enhancement of the original model’s performance by incorporating the TyG index and its associated factors, thereby improving their contribution to predicting CVD. Contrasted with the base model, the + TyG, +TyG-WC, and + TyG-WHtR models were better calibrated and demonstrated improved accuracy and predictive power, as seen in the enhanced NRI and IDI. In evaluating the model’s goodness-of-fit, we found that the TyG-WHtR index demonstrated the greatest improvement in this regard. The DCA curve indicated that TyG, TyG-WHtR, and TyG-WC offered considerable clinical advantages. These outcomes manifested that the TyG index and the associated factors can identify postmenopausal women at a greater risk of developing CVD. Study strengths and limitations This prospective cohort research offers valuable insights into the predictive capabilities of the TyG index and its associated measures (TyG-WC, TyG-WHtR, and TyG-BMI) in the context of CVD risk stratification among postmenopausal women, addressing a critical gap in the existing literature. An important strength of this study is its longitudinal design, which effectively minimizes recall bias and establishes a clear temporal relationship between metabolic dysregulation and cardiovascular disease outcomes over a follow-up period of 12 years. The study enhances its approach by employing obesity-adjusted TyG indices, moving beyond the limitations of traditional metrics such as isolated lipid or glucose measurements. However, several limitations warrant consideration. First, the sample population was exclusively sourced from the Kailuan community cohort, which may restrict the generalizability of the findings to ethnically diverse populations or those with varying socioeconomic and lifestyle profiles. Furthermore, the absence of direct insulin measurements hindered a comparative analysis of the TyG indices and the homeostasis model assessment (HOMA) parameters, which are regarded as a gold-standard surrogate for insulin resistance. This oversight limits evaluating the incremental predictive value of TyG indices compared to established biomarkers in clinical practice. although the model accounted for key confounders, unmeasured variables, such as dietary patterns, physical activity levels, and psychosocial stressors, may still influence the association between TyG and CVD. These factors are recognized to impact both metabolic health and CVD risk, and their omission could weaken the strength of the risk estimates. Finally, observational designs limit the ability to infer causation; randomized trials are essential to determine if interventions aimed at reducing the TyG index directly lower cardiovascular disease events. Conclusion The results of this study have important implications for clinical practice in managing CVD risk among Chinese postmenopausal women. The TyG-WC and TyG-WHtR indices have a higher level of diagnostic accuracy than TyG alone among postmenopausal women, allowing clinicians to identify high-risk individuals earlier and more precisely. Incorporating these indices into routine clinical evaluations could enhance risk assessment, especially in resource-limited settings where advanced diagnostic tools may not be accessible. Combining TyG-WHtR with established biomarkers or imaging techniques may improve the early detection of subclinical CVD, enabling timely interventions such as lifestyle modifications, targeted lipid-lowering therapies, or personalized HRT regimens.
