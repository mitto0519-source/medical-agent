Background Allergic rhinitis is a prevalent immune disorder with a significant impact on quality of life and socioeconomic burden. Korean Medicine treatments such as herbal medicine and acupuncture are widely utilized for allergic rhinitis due to clinical effectiveness and patient preferences. In particular, herbal decoctions, a key component of Korean Medicine treatment for allergic rhinitis, offer personalized treatment but pose challenges for randomized controlled trials due to the difficulty of placebo development. This study aims to establish a registry utilizing real-world data (RWD) on Korean Medicine treatment for allergic rhinitis, focusing on the effectiveness, safety, and cost-effectiveness of herbal decoctions in clinical practice. Methods The KOrean medicine REgistry for Allergic rhinitis in Real-world settings (KOREA-R) aims to enroll 400 patients with allergic rhinitis receiving Korean Medicine treatment at two Korean Medicine hospitals from February 2025 to June 2028. Patients will be examined in three visits at 2-week intervals, followed by two additional online follow-ups at 6 and 12 months after week 4. RWD on symptom severity, quality of life, treatment administration and compliance, adverse events, and laboratory blood tests will be collected. Participation is voluntary, with no restrictions on treatment types or dosages. Statistical analyses, including propensity score matching and difference-in-difference, will be used to evaluate the effectiveness, safety, and cost-effectiveness of Korean Medicine treatment, particularly herbal decoctions. Data management ensures confidentiality and quality through a validated electronic data capture system and regular monitoring. Conclusion The findings of KOREA-R will provide insights into the effectiveness and safety of Korean Medicine treatment for allergic rhinitis, with a focus on herbal decoctions tailored to individual patient symptoms and pattern identifications. Additionally, by systematically collecting RWD for multidimensional analysis, KOREA-R will offer valuable information on the cost-utility and cost-effectiveness of herbal decoctions for both patients and healthcare policymakers. Study registration Clinical Research Information Service, KCT0010133 (Registration date: January 13, 2025). The prevalence of allergic rhinitis is increasing, with the overall prevalence reported to be approximately 18% worldwide [ 3 , 4 ]. It causes various symptoms, such as runny nose, sneezing, nasal congestion, itching, and pain, and can lead to sleep disturbance, decreased learning ability, decreased productivity, and decreased quality of life [ 5 ]. In particular, allergic rhinitis has a severe impact on quality of life and direct and indirect costs, resulting in a significant socioeconomic burden [ 6 ]. The first-line treatment for allergic rhinitis is avoidance of relevant allergens and irritants, and conventional treatments for allergic rhinitis include intranasal corticosteroids, nasal saline irrigation, antihistamines, leukotriene receptor antagonists, and allergen immunotherapy [ 7 ]. However, due to adverse effects of conventional treatments (e.g., nasal irritation, stinging, and systemic allergic reactions) and patient preferences, Korean Medicine treatments including herbal medicine and acupuncture, which are complementary and integrative therapies, have been widely used to manage allergic rhinitis [ 7 , 8 ]. The clinical effectiveness and safety of herbal medicine and acupuncture for allergic rhinitis, have been reported by various clinical studies; however, most of the studies were conducted in China [ 9 , 10 ]. Allergic rhinitis is one of the diseases frequently treated in Korean Medicine institutions and ranked 22nd in terms of the number of patients in the Korean Medicine field as of 2023 [ 11 ]. In addition, according to a survey targeting Korean Medicine clinics, herbal decoctions have been established as the first-line treatment for allergic rhinitis [ 12 ]. However, although patient satisfaction with the use of Korean Medicine treatment has been reported to be high, a significant number of patients have highlighted that the cost of Korean Medicine treatment is high and that health insurance coverage for herbal decoctions should be expanded [ 13 ]. In response to the public’s demand for expanded insurance coverage, the Korean government is conducting a pilot project to include herbal decoctions for allergic rhinitis in health insurance from April 2024 to December 2026 to further expand the use of herbal decoctions and provide patients with more economical treatment options. The use of herbal decoctions has the advantage of allowing customized treatments by mixing herbs according to the symptoms and pattern identification of individual patients. However, it is difficult to conduct a randomized controlled clinical trial (RCT), which provides a higher level of evidence, using herbal decoctions. Additionally, as herbal decoction treatment is a type of personalized treatment, it is difficult to develop a placebo drug, making it impossible to evaluate its efficacy through RCTs. Therefore, to evaluate the clinical effectiveness and safety of herbal decoctions, an observational study in the form of a registry that collects real-world data (RWD) from clinical settings may be an effective method [ 14 ]. Therefore, the objective of this study is to prospectively collect medical information on patients who visit Korean Medicine hospitals for allergic rhinitis and receive Korean Medicine treatment by building a KOrean medicine REgistry for Allergic rhinitis in Real-world settings (KOREA-R), as well as to analyze trends in the collected data to understand actual clinical settings. Overall, we aim to systematically collect data on the administration of Korean Medicine treatment, such as herbal decoction prescriptions, obtain basic data on allergic rhinitis-related symptoms and patient conditions before and after Korean Medicine treatment, and collect and analyze data on the effectiveness, safety, and cost-effectiveness of Korean Medicine treatment, specifically focusing on herbal decoctions for patients with allergic rhinitis. Methods and design Study design and registration This is a prospective observational registry study involving patients with allergic rhinitis, which collects RWD derived from clinical treatment at Korean Medicine hospitals. The researcher will first explain to the patients the study title, purpose, study period, study method, expected effects and risks, and possibility of withdrawal of consent at any time in an easily understandable manner. The patients will voluntarily decide whether to participate in the KOREA-R study, and if they agree, they will sign the written informed consent form. For minor patients, written consent from their legal guardian is also obtained. Patients will receive typical Korean Medicine treatment for allergic rhinitis according to the medical judgment of Korean Medicine doctors, and no additional interventions for research purposes will be conducted. There are no restrictions on the type, cycle, or dosage of the Korean Medicine treatment applied to each patient, and medications for treating allergic rhinitis that were taken before participating in the study can be used in conjunction with Korean Medicine treatment. However, if the dosage and administration of medications have changed or if a new treatment other than Korean Medicine treatment is initiated to treat allergic rhinitis, detailed information on concomitant medications and treatment should be collected. Data will be collected at 2-week intervals from the enrollment date until week 4, and online follow-ups will be conducted 6 months and 12 months after week 4 (Table 1 ). After collecting data at the hospital on the enrollment date, data collection through telephone or online surveys without visiting the hospital is permitted on week 2 (Visit 2) or week 4 (Visit 3) depending on the treatment plan and patient circumstances. The study will be carried out from February 2025 to June 2028 at Kyung Hee University Korean Medicine Hospital and Daejeon Korean Medicine Hospital of Daejeon University in Korea. The registry study protocol was registered with the Clinical Research Information Service (registration number: KCT0010133) before enrolling the first patient (Registration date: January 13, 2025). Eligibility criteria The inclusion criteria for the KOREA-R study are (1) those with two or more of the four major symptoms of allergic rhinitis (runny nose, nasal congestion, sneezing, and itching), (2) those with a total score of 4 or higher based on the Total Nasal Symptom Score (TNSS), (3) those with a positive result in an allergy test conducted within 12 months of the enrollment date, and (4) those who have received a sufficient explanation of this study, agreed to participate in this study of their own free will or following the will of their legal representatives, and signed a written consent form approved by the institutional review boards (IRBs). The exclusion criteria are (1) those who have received herbal decoction treatment within the past month to treat allergic rhinitis and (2) those whom the researcher has judged to be unsuitable for study enrollment. Recruitment During the study period, patients receiving Korean Medicine treatment for allergic rhinitis in Korean Medicine hospitals participating in the study will be recruited as participants. The researcher will register patients who visit participating Korean Medicine hospitals for allergic rhinitis treatment and meet the eligibility criteria of this study. In addition, recruitment advertisements will be posted on the research institution’s bulletin boards and website to successfully achieve the target number of research participants. Data collection (outcome measure) The following data will be collected from participants (Table 1 ). Demographic and socioeconomic information: Demographic information (e.g., sex and age) and socioeconomic information (e.g., education level, marital status, occupation, religion, and income) are collected. Medical history and treatment history: The first onset date of allergic rhinitis, clinical symptoms (runny nose, nasal congestion, sneezing, itching, etc.), allergic rhinitis disease code diagnosed by a medical professional, presence of other allergic diseases (e.g., atopic dermatitis and asthma), and family history of allergic diseases are investigated. In addition, treatment history and medications taken within the past 3 months or currently taken are investigated. Anthropometric data: Height, weight, and body mass index (BMI) are measured. For minors, the height percentile, weight percentile, and BMI percentile according to sex and age are also investigated based on the 2017 Korean National Growth Charts for children and adolescents [ 15 ]. Anthropometric data are collected for adults only at Visit 1, whereas the data are collected for minors at Visits 1 and 3, as well as online follow-ups. At follow-ups, patients are asked to provide their most recent anthropometric data and the date of measurement. Questionnaire evaluation: The severity of four symptoms of allergic rhinitis (runny nose, nasal congestion, itching, and sneezing) is assessed using the TNSS questionnaire [ 16 ]. Allergic rhinitis-related quality of life is assessed using the Quality of Life questionnaire in Korean Children with Allergic Rhinitis (QoL-KCAR) for those under 12 years of age at enrollment date and the Mini Rhinoconjunctivitis Quality of Life Questionnaire (Mini-RQLQ) for those 12 years of age or older [ 17 , 18 ]. The patients’ digestion (discomfort felt while eating and digesting) and sleep (severity of sleep disturbance), which are factors associated with allergic rhinitis symptoms and investigated in Korean Medicine clinical practice, are self-evaluated using a numeric rating scale (NRS) ranging from 0 (no symptoms) to 10 (the worst intensity imaginable). To evaluate health-related quality of life, five areas (mobility, self-care, usual activities, pain/discomfort, and anxiety/depression) are evaluated on a 3-point scale using EuroQol 5-Dimensions Youth, 3-Level (EQ-5D-Y-3L) for those under 12 years of age at enrollment date and using EuroQoL 5-Dimensions, 3-Level (EQ-5D-3L) for those 12 years of age or older [ 19 ]. In addition, the EuroQol Visual Analogue Scale is used to evaluate the patients’ subjective health status, with a value ranging from 0 (worst imaginable health status) to 100 (best imaginable health status). The TNSS and NRS for digestion and sleep are measured at all visits and follow-ups, and allergic rhinitis-related quality of life and health-related quality of life are investigated from Visit 1 to 3. Patients are asked to respond to five questions regarding their overall satisfaction with Korean Medicine treatment on a scale ranging from 1 to 5 at Visit 3. In addition, at Visit 1, all registered participants are surveyed about the reasons for choosing or not choosing herbal decoction treatment, and at Visit 3, only participants who have received herbal decoction treatment are surveyed about their willingness to continue taking herbal decoctions. Direct medical costs (e.g., medicine costs and diagnosis and examination costs) attributed to allergic rhinitis are investigated from Visit 1 to 3. In all surveys, if a minor participant does not understand the content, a legal representative will answer the questions of the participant. Korean Medicine treatment information: From Visit 1 to 3, the administration of Korean Medicine treatment for allergic rhinitis is investigated. For herbal decoctions, the prescription name, herb composition and dosage, and prescription date, as well as participation in the pilot project of health insurance are investigated. For herbal medicine preparations, whether health insurance is applied and the prescription name, dosage, and prescription date are investigated. The implementation of non-pharmacological Korean Medicine interventions such as acupuncture, moxibustion, bee venom acupuncture, cupping therapy, and chuna manual therapy is also investigated. Compliance with herbal medicine: At Visits 2 and 3, if participants have been prescribed herbal medicines such as herbal decoctions or herbal medicine preparations at the previous visit, compliance with herbal medicine will be determined by quantifying the actual amount taken by participants compared to the amount they were supposed to take. Laboratory blood tests: To determine whether the eligibility criteria are met, a multiple allergen simultaneous test (MAST) is performed at the enrollment date to detect a positive response to one or more specific allergens. If participants show a positive result for one or more allergens in an allergy test (e.g., MAST, allergy skin test, and ImmunoCAP) conducted within 12 months before the enrollment date, the MAST can be omitted. For safety assessment, liver and kidney function test indices such as aspartate aminotransferase (AST), alanine aminotransferase (ALT), blood urea nitrogen (BUN), and creatinine are measured at Visits 1 and 3. Additional laboratory tests may be performed if necessary based on the judgment of the researcher. Adverse events: For safety assessment, all adverse events occurring during the study period are investigated. Adverse events are any harmful and unintended signs, symptoms, or diseases occurring in participants. Based on the reports of participants, medical interviews, and observations of researchers, onset and end dates, previous history, severity of adverse events, whether herbal decoctions have been taken at the time of the adverse events, measures taken for herbal decoctions, reactions after re-administration of herbal decoctions, and other measures taken are investigated and recorded in detail. Concomitant medications: The use of concomitant medications during the study period is examined in detail. Data management and quality control The electronic case report form (eCRF) system for data collection is based on myTrial Electronic Data Capture (National Institute for Korean Medicine Development, Gyeongsan, Republic of Korea), validated by the National Agency for Korean Medicine Innovative Technologies Development. By setting and managing access rights to data according to roles such as data input, data quality management, and data analysis, unauthorized researchers cannot access the data. Information that can identify participants is not entered, and the entered information is kept confidential. Data collected for this study are stored securely on a dedicated collection server with strictly regulated data access permissions. To ensure the quality of research, monitoring is conducted to protect the rights and welfare of participants, to confirm whether the entered research data are accurate, complete, and verifiable through a comparison with the source documents, and to confirm whether the research is conducted following the IRB-approved research protocol and related regulations. The scope of monitoring includes checking compliance with the study protocol, appropriate and accurate data collection, review of the participants’ consent and re-consent forms, and collection and reporting of adverse events. Sample size calculation Considering that this is a registry study targeting patients receiving Korean Medicine treatment at Korean Medicine hospitals for allergic rhinitis, we plan to register all patients who meet the eligibility criteria. Based on research results showing an average of 161 patients with rhinitis and sinusitis visiting a Korean Medicine hospital for the first time each year [ 20 ], it is estimated that the number of patients with allergic rhinitis visiting a Korean Medicine hospital for the first time is approximately 80 per year. Considering the need for agreement to participate in the registry study, it is expected that approximately 200 participants may be registered per hospital during the study period of approximately 3.5 years. Therefore, the goal is to recruit a total of 400 participants from two institutions, Kyung Hee University Hospital of Korean Medicine and Daejeon University Daejeon Hospital of Korean Medicine. Criteria for discontinuation and dropping out Participants can discontinue participation in the study at any time of their own free will or following the will of their legal representatives. Participants who fall under the following categories will be dropped out of the study: (1) if it is confirmed that the eligibility criteria have been violated after study registration, (2) if the participants or their legal representatives withdraw consent to participate in the study, (3) if the participants cannot be tracked (only treatment information from Visit 1 is collected, and data are not collected from subsequent visits, making it impossible to compare the results before and after treatment), and (4) if the participants are judged by the researcher as not suitable to proceed with the clinical research. Statistical analysis All statistical analyses will be performed using SAS ® Version 9.4 (SAS Institute Inc., Cary, NC), with two-tailed test as the primary test, and the significance level is set at 5%. Continuous variables will be presented as the mean (95% confidence interval) or median (interquartile range), and categorical variables will be presented as the frequency and percentage. Demographic and baseline characteristics between the herbal decoction treatment and non-treatment groups will be compared by independent t-test or Wilcoxon rank-sum test for continuous variables, depending on normality, and Chi-square test or Fisher’s exact test for categorical variables. Baseline differences between the two groups will be adjusted as confounders using analysis of covariance, with baseline values included as covariates when comparing mean changes in outcome measures between the two groups. The effect outcomes will be assessed by comparing changes in outcome measures such as the TNSS at week 2, week 4, and 6 and 12 months after week 4 between the herbal decoction treatment and non-treatment groups using the propensity score matching-difference in difference (PSM-DID) model. Propensity scores will be estimated via logistic regression, adjusting for covariates such as sex and age, with balance between matched groups verified using standardized mean differences. The difference in allergic rhinitis symptom improvement before and after treatment and according to herbal decoction use and duration will be compared between the groups. Additionally, changes in outcome measures will be evaluated within each group by paired t-test or Wilcoxon signed-rank test, depending on data normality. When analyzing safety outcomes, the number of adverse events will be compared between the groups by Chi-square test or Fisher’s exact test, and the difference in AST, ALT, BUN, and creatinine levels before and after treatment will be analyzed by paired t-test or Wilcoxon signed-rank test. Missing values will be handled based on identified patterns by applying either the last observation carried forward method for data classified as missing completely at random or the multiple imputation method for data categorized as missing at random. Economic evaluation To evaluate the cost-utility of the herbal decoction treatment and non-treatment groups, the incremental cost-utility ratio of the two groups will be calculated at each visit using EQ-5D-Y-3L or EQ-5D-3L as utility outcomes. In addition, to compare the cost-effectiveness of the herbal decoction treatment and non-treatment groups, the incremental cost-effectiveness ratio between the two groups will be calculated using effect outcome measures such as the TNSS. Direct medical costs will be determined based on cost questionnaires, institutional data such as medical records and administrative departments, and medical expense receipts collected from participants, and secondary data sources such as Statistics Korea data may be used if necessary. For economic analysis, a healthcare system perspective will be adopted. Discussion Concerns about the adverse effects of conventional treatment, and patient preferences have led to the widespread use of Korean Medicine treatments like herbal medicine and acupuncture for the treatment of allergic rhinitis [ 7 , 8 ]. However, most studies on their effectiveness and safety have been conducted in China [ 9 , 10 ]. In Korea, herbal decoctions are the preferred first-line treatment for allergic rhinitis in Korean Medicine institutions [ 12 ]. Despite high patient satisfaction, many patients have been demanding expanded insurance coverage for herbal decoctions due to the cost [ 13 ]. In response, the Korean government launched a pilot project (2024–2026) to include herbal decoctions for allergic rhinitis under health insurance. Since conducting RCTs on herbal decoctions is challenging due to their personalized nature, an observational registry study collecting RWD is a practical alternative for evaluating their clinical effectiveness and safety [ 14 ]. Especially, the use of RWD in healthcare research has been increasing, particularly for evaluating clinical outcomes. RWD can provide valuable insights into treatment effectiveness and safety in routine clinical practice, complementing evidence from RCTs [ 21 ]. As a result, regulatory agencies and researchers are increasingly utilizing RWD to support decision-making in medical and health policy [ 22 ]. Although the clinical effectiveness of herbal decoctions for allergic rhinitis patients was confirmed in a previous study conducted at Korean medical clinics, it was limited as a retrospective analysis [ 14 ]. In addition, a prospective observational study on combined Korean Medicine therapy for allergic rhinitis was previously conducted; however, it only targeted children with allergic rhinitis under the age of 12 years and did not elicit an effect similar to that of herbal decoctions alone [ 23 ]. This KOREA-R study distinguishes itself from previous studies in several key aspects. First, it includes a cost-effectiveness evaluation to assess the appropriateness of health insurance coverage for herbal decoction treatment. Second, it examines the long-term effects of Korean Medicine treatment through follow-ups at 6 and 12 months after week 4, offering valuable insights into sustained outcomes. Third, this research focuses exclusively on patients with allergic rhinitis confirmed through an allergy test, ensuring greater diagnostic accuracy. Fourth, it further validates the safety of herbal decoctions through comprehensive blood test analyses. This registry observes the actual form of clinical treatment conducted at Korean Medicine hospitals and the RWD generated during the process. Accordingly, no separate research-purpose intervention is conducted on the participants, and the participants receive general Korean Medicine treatment for allergic rhinitis under the medical judgment of the Korean Medicine doctors participating in the study. In other words, since this study does not involve intervention beyond routine medical practice, there is no additional burden, risk exposure, or possibility of adverse effects for the participants due to their participation in the study. Additionally, the safety of Korean Medicine treatment will be explored in detail through monitoring of adverse events and blood tests for liver and kidney function before and after treatment during the study period. The limitation of the KOREA-R study is that participants are recruited from two Korean Medicine hospitals, which may not fully represent the broader population or diverse treatment practices across other institutions. Nevertheless, the KOREA-R has great strengths as follows. First, by leveraging a prospective registry of 400 patients receiving Korean Medicine treatment for allergic rhinitis, the study evaluates the effectiveness and safety of Korean Medicine treatment with a focus on herbal decoctions in actual clinical settings, providing insights that controlled studies may not capture. Second, the study accommodates the individualized nature of herbal decoction treatment, reflecting real-world practices where prescriptions vary based on patient symptoms and pattern identifications. Third, the study systematically collects various data, including symptom severity, quality of life, treatment satisfaction, safety, and economic burden, allowing multidimensional analysis. Finally, by assessing cost-utility and cost-effectiveness, the study provides valuable information on the economic viability of herbal decoctions for patients and healthcare policymakers.
