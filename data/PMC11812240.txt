Background Anthropometric indicators, such as the Lipid Accumulation Product (LAP), the Visceral Adiposity Index (VAI), and the Body Roundness Index (BRI), could be valuable tools for identifying metabolic syndrome (MS) in rural populations because of their simplicity, low cost, and ability to identify central obesity and related metabolic risks where access to healthcare is limited. This study aimed to evaluate the predictive capacity of three anthropometric indices for identifying MS in a representative sample of rural workers in Espírito Santo, Brazil. Methods A cross-sectional analysis was conducted, and the diagnostic performance of these indices was assessed via ROC curves under the NCEP and IDF criteria for MS. The study involved 806 participants, aged 18–59 years, and the data collection included anthropometric measurements, biochemical tests, and blood pressure assessments. Results The results showed that LAP had the highest discriminatory capacity for MS under both diagnostic criteria, with AUCs of 0.803 (NCEP) and 0.885 (IDF), followed by BRI and VAI. The cutoff points identified for LAP were 33.84 (NCEP) and 26.80 (IDF), with high sensitivity and specificity. The BRI also demonstrated strong predictive power with AUC values above 0.7, whereas the VAI had the lowest AUC among the indices but remained a useful predictor. The correlations between the indices and MS components highlighted significant relationships, especially between LAP and triglycerides ( r = 0.830). Conclusions This is the first study to determine cutoff points for these indices in a rural Brazilian population, suggesting that LAP and BRI are reliable tools for MS screening. The low cost of these methods could aid in improving healthcare accessibility and prevention strategies in rural areas with limited resources. Future research is recommended to validate these findings in other populations. MS is becoming a serious public health issue and a significant economic burden due to its high global prevalence, which continues to rise in both developed and developing countries [ 2 ]. According to a recent meta-analysis, the global prevalence ranged from 12.5% (according to the Adult Treatment Panel III of the National Cholesterol Education Program - NCEP-ATP III criteria) to 31.4% (according to the Joint Interim Statement - JIS criteria) [ 3 ]. In Brazil, the prevalence was estimated at 33% [ 4 ], with variations from 12.3% [ 5 ] to 30% [ 6 ] in rural areas. In the context of rural populations, the identification and prevention of MS face particular challenges, as these populations often experience limited access to healthcare services, lower levels of education, and income, which are factors that may exacerbate the prevalence and severity of health problems [ 7 ]. Furthermore, the predominant economic activities, such as agriculture and livestock farming, may increase exposure to occupational risks and promote a more sedentary lifestyle, which negatively impacts health indicators [ 8 ]. Thus, identifying effective tools to detect individuals at elevated risk for metabolic abnormalities, particularly MS, is essential. Predictive indices offer a straightforward and rapid approach for screening, bypassing the requirement for invasive and complex testing, yet delivering reliable and sensitive differentiation of the presence or absence of risk factors [ 9 ]. Three reliable indicators of visceral adiposity have been developed on the basis of anthropometric and metabolic parameters. The first is the lipid accumulation product (LAP), which integrates waist circumference (WC) and triglyceride (TG) levels, serving as a proxy for lipid overaccumulation and insulin resistance, which are pivotal in the development of MS [ 10 ]. The second is the visceral adiposity index (VAI), which combines anthropometric (waist circumference and BMI) and biochemical parameters (triglycerides and HDL cholesterol), offering insights into visceral fat dysfunction and its contribution to metabolic disturbances [ 11 ]. The third is the body roundness index (BRI), derived from height and waist circumference, quantifies body shape and fat distribution, reflecting the degree of central obesity, a core component of MS [ 12 ]. The comparison of LAP, VAI, and BRI is essential due to their distinct yet complementary roles in assessing adiposity and cardiometabolic risk, which are central to the pathophysiology of MS. Each indicator reflects different aspects of lipid accumulation, visceral fat distribution, and overall body shape, providing a multifaceted approach to risk stratification. Therefore, this study aims to evaluate the utility of different anthropometric indicators in predicting metabolic syndrome in rural adult population in Brazil. Specifically, it seeks to establish cutoff points for LAP, VAI, and BRI, adapted to this population, as well as to compare the discriminatory capacity of these indices in identifying MS. This research was conducted in a rural population in Santa Maria de Jetibá, Espírito Santo, with the aim of providing relevant evidence to improve screening and prevention strategies in areas with limited access to healthcare services. Methods Study design and population This cross-sectional epidemiological study is part of a broader research project conducted in the municipality of Santa Maria de Jetibá, in the state of Espírito Santo, southeastern Brazil, entitled “Health conditions and associated factors: a study with farmers from Espírito Santo—AgroSaúdES”. The project was funded by the Espírito Santo Research Support Foundation (FAPES) through the Call FAPES/CNPq/Decit-SCTIE-MS/SESA—PPSUS—No. 05/2015. The study was approved by the Research Ethics Committee of the Health Sciences Center of the Federal University of Espírito Santo (UFES), under number 1,856,331. All participants signed the Free and Informed Consent Form (ICF). The research was conducted in the municipality of Santa Maria de Jetibá, located in Espírito Santo, southeastern Brazil, with a representative sample of farmers who met the following inclusion criteria: aged between 18 and 59 years, not pregnant, having farming as the main source of livelihood, and being continuously employed for at least six months. The sample size was calculated via Epidat software, version 3.0. To identify eligible farmers, data from individual and family records, collected by the Family Health Strategy teams, were utilized to ensure coverage across all 11 health regions of the municipality. This process led to the identification of 7,287 farmers from a total of 4,018 families. The estimate considered a 50% prevalence of outcomes (to maximize the sample) [ 13 ], a sampling error of 3.5%, and a significance level of 95%, resulting in a minimum sample of 708 farmers. However, to improve the representativeness of the sample, data from 789 farmers who were investigated in the original project and had all the necessary data for outcome calculation were included in the analysis. Data collection Data collection was carried out between December 2016 and April 2017 at the local healthcare units in the municipality, and was conducted by qualified researchers. Detailed information about the data collection process and research development can be found in the article by Petarli et al. [ 13 ]. Sociodemographic, occupational, and lifestyle data were collected through a semistructured questionnaire, followed by anthropometric measurements and blood pressure assessment. The participants’ body weight and height were measured while standing, barefoot, and wearing light clothing. Weight was measured using an Omron-514Cs digital scale, with a maximum capacity of 150 kg and an accuracy of 0.1 kg. Height was measured with a Sanny portable stadiometer, model ES-2060, with an accuracy of 0.1 mm. WC was measured using a tape measure (Sanny, Mod. TR-4010, São Bernardo do Campo, SP-Brazil) at the midpoint between the iliac crest and the lower rib. Three non-consecutive measurements were taken, the first being discarded and the average of the last two considered as the final measurement. Blood pressure was measured following the procedures recommended by the VII Brazilian Hypertension Guidelines [ 14 ]. Three measurements were taken on the right arm, with the participant seated after a rest period of at least 5 min, using a digital device (Omron HEM-7200, Shimogyo-ku, Kyoto-Japan) calibrated and validated by the National Institute of Metrology, Quality and Technology (INMETRO) to obtain systolic blood pressure (SBP) and diastolic blood pressure (DBP). To minimize potential interference with the results, participants were instructed to sit and rest for approximately five minutes, empty their bladder, and refrain from consuming food, alcohol, coffee, or smoking for 30 min before the assessment. The first measurement was discarded, and the final value was obtained by averaging the second and third readings. If the difference between these two measurements was greater than 5 mmHg, a fourth measurement was taken and used as the final value. Farmers underwent blood sampling for biochemical tests, and to ensure accuracy of the results, all analyses were conducted in a single laboratory. A 12-hour fasting period was required for the tests. HDL cholesterol was measured using the colorimetric enzymatic method with the Cholesterol Liquicolor Kit and Cholesterol HDL Precipitation Kit (In Vitro Diagnostica Ltda, Belo Horizonte, MG, Brazil). TG levels were evaluated using the colorimetric enzymatic method with the Liquicolor mono ® Triglycerides Kit (In Vitro Diagnostica Ltda, Belo Horizonte, MG, Brazil). Index calculation LAP was calculated using TG and WC values. The formula is as follows: for men, LAP = [WC (cm) − 65] × TG (mmol/l), and for women, LAP = [WC (cm) − 58] × TG (mmol/l) [ 10 ]. VAI was calculated using specific equations for men and women. For men, the formula was as follows: VAI = (WC / [39.68 + 1.88 × BMI]) × (TG / 1.03) × (1.31 / HDL). For women, the equation is as follows: VAI = [WC / (36.58 + 1.89 × BMI)] × (TG / 0.81) × (1.52 / HDL), where BMI is body mass index in kg/m², WC represents waist circumference in cm, TG refers to triglycerides in mmol/L, and HDL is high-density lipoprotein in mmol/L [ 11 ]. BRI was calculated using the formula developed by Thomas et al. [ 12 ]: BRI = 364.2 − 365.5 × √(1 − [WC in centimeters / 2π]² / [0.5 × height in centimeters]²). Diagnostic criteria To define the cutoff points for the indices, the MS criteria established by the IDF [ 15 ] and NCEP-ATP III [ 16 ] were applied. According to the IDF criteria, an individual with abdominal obesity, defined by a WC ≥ 84 cm for women or ≥ 94 cm for men, is considered to have MS when this condition is associated with at least two of the following factors: fasting glucose ≥ 100 mg/dL, SBP ≥ 130 mmHg or DBP ≥ 85 mmHg, TG ≥ 150 mg/dL, HDL-c &lt; 40 mg/dL for men and &lt; 50 mg/dL for women. According to the NCEP-ATP III criteria, MS is diagnosed when at least three of the following criteria are present: WC &gt; 102 cm for men or &gt; 88 cm for women; HDL-c &lt; 40 mg/dL for men and &lt; 50 mg/dL for women; TG ≥ 150 mg/dL; SBP ≥ 130 mmHg and DBP ≥ 85 mmHg; and fasting glucose ≥ 100 mg/dL. The use of antihypertensive, hypoglycemic, or lipid-lowering medications is also considered, classifying the individual as having hypertension, diabetes, or dyslipidemia, respectively. Statistical analysis Statistical analyses were performed using IBM SPSS Statistics 23 (Armonk, NY: IBM Corp). The normality of the variables was verified using the Kolmogorov-Smirnov test. To describe the study variables, measures of central tendency (median) and measures of dispersion (interquartile range) were used for continuous variables, and absolute and percentage values were used for categorical variables. The chi-square test was used to compare two qualitative variables. The Mann-Whitney U test (for nonparametric variables) was used to analyze the associations between quantitative and qualitative variables. Spearman’s correlation test was employed to identify correlations between the two dependent variables. The significance level adopted was α &lt; 5%. The Receiver Operating Characteristic (ROC) curve was generated to identify the cutoff points for LAP, VAI and BRI, with ideal areas under the curve (AUCs), sensitivity, specificity, and Youden’s index for diagnosing MS. The nonparametric method of DeLong et al. [ 17 ] was applied to compare the AUCs of the indices. Results Table 1 describes the characteristics related to the components of MS and the studied indicators, according to the presence or absence of MS, based on the NCEP and IDF criteria. According to the NCEP criteria, 45.7% of individuals with MS have a greater mean age (44.3 ± 9.8 years; p &lt; 0.001). Additionally, participants with MS had a greater WC (102.3 ± 11.5 cm vs. 86.5 ± 11.3 cm; p &lt; 0.001), lower HDL-c levels (45.3 ± 12.0 mg/dL vs. 58.3 ± 12.6 mg/dL; p &lt; 0.001), and elevated TG levels (198.3 ± 130.6 mg/dL vs. 90.4 ± 41.7 mg/dL; p &lt; 0.001). Blood glucose was also higher in the MS group (91.0 ± 28.8 mg/dL; p &lt; 0.001), as were SBP and DBP (137.7 ± 16.7 mmHg and 82.1 ± 12.0 mmHg, respectively; all p &lt; 0.001), compared to individuals without MS. BMI is higher in participants with MS (30.5 ± 4.3 kg/m² vs. 24.9 ± 4.0 kg/m²; p &lt; 0.001). When the IDF criteria is used, the results are similar. Individuals with MS were older (44.8 ± 9.4 years vs. 37.9 ± 10.7 years), had greater WCs (103.3 ± 10.9 cm vs. 86.4 ± 11.2 cm), had lower HDL-c levels (45.9 ± 12.0 mg/dL vs. 58.0 ± 12.8 mg/dL), and had higher TG levels (194.0 ± 125.3 mg/dL vs. 92.4 ± 49.8 mg/dL). Blood glucose, blood pressure, and BMI are also higher in individuals with MS (all p &lt; 0.001). The sociodemographic characteristics of the sample can be found in the article by Cremonini et al. [ 5 ]. Table 2 presents the median values of LAP, VAI, and BRI in family farmers with and without MS according to the NCEP and IDF criteria. The results show that individuals with MS have significantly higher values for all indicators (all p &lt; 0.001). Table 3 presents the correlations between the LAP, VAI, and BRI indicators and the components of MS in farmers. All indicators showed statistically significant correlations ( p &lt; 0.001) with most MS components. Specifically, LAP presented positive correlations with WC ( r = 0.624), SBP ( r = 0.164), DBP ( r = 0.241), fasting plasma glucose ( r = 0.220), and TG ( r = 0.830), and a negative correlation with HDL-c ( r = -0.376). VAI also showed significant positive correlations with WC ( r = 0.336), SBP ( r = 0.098), DBP ( r = 0.136), fasting plasma glucose ( r = 0.124), and TG ( r = 0.928), as well as a negative correlation with HDL-c ( r = -0.487). BRI, in turn, presented positive correlations with WC ( r = 0.446), DBP ( r = 0.125), fasting plasma glucose ( r = 0.146), and TG ( r = 0.163), and a negative correlation with HDL-c ( r = -0.135), but did not show a significant correlation with SBP ( r = 0.032, p = 0.370). The results of Table 4 show the AUC, cutoff points, sensitivity, specificity, and Youden’s index for the LAP, VAI, and BRI indicators in relation to MS, using both the NCEP and IDF criteria. For the NCEP criterion, the LAP index showed the highest AUC (0.803), followed by BRI (0.776) and VAI (0.719), indicating that LAP has the highest discriminatory capacity among the three indices for diagnosing MS based on this criterion. The cutoff point for LAP was 33.84, with a sensitivity of 69.52% and a specificity of 82.91%. These values resulted in a Youden index of 0.52, reflecting a reasonable balance between sensitivity and specificity. BRI, with an AUC of 0.776, showed higher sensitivity than LAP (72.38%) but lower specificity (73.00%), resulting in a slightly lower Youden’s index (0.45). VAI had the lowest AUC (0.719) among the three indices, with a sensitivity of 51.11% and a specificity of 86.71%, leading to a Youden index of 0.37, suggesting that despite its high specificity, its low sensitivity limits its effectiveness in diagnosing MS by the NCEP criterion. For the IDF criterion, BRI had the highest AUC (0.892), followed by LAP (0.885) and VAI (0.771). BRI showed the highest sensitivity (81.11%), although with a specificity of 72.82%, leading to a Youden index of 0.53, the highest among the three indices under this criterion. LAP, with a sensitivity of 77.20% and a specificity of 69.92%, had a Youden index of 0.47, reflecting adequate discrimination between the presence and absence of MS. VAI, once again, had the lowest AUC (0.771) and the lowest sensitivity (48.53%), although it maintained the highest specificity (84.44%), resulting in a Youden’s index of 0.32, confirming its limitation in terms of sensitivity for diagnosing MS. The results presented in Table 5 show a comparison of the AUC values for different diagnostic indices of MS. Under the NCEP criterion, the difference between the AUCs of the LAP and VAI indices was 0.083 (95% CI: 0.062–0.105; p &lt; 0.001). The LAP index also showed a significant difference when compared to the BRI, with a difference of 0.027 (95% CI: 0.006–0.050; p = 0.016). Additionally, the comparison between the BRI and VAI indices revealed a difference of 0.057 (95% CI: 0.021–0.095; p = 0.0025), demonstrating a statistically significant difference between these two indices. Under the IDF criterion, the difference between the AUCs for the LAP and VAI indices was 0.116 (95% CI: 0.096–0.136; p &lt; 0.001). However, the comparison between the LAP and BRI indices did not reveal a statistically significant difference, with a difference of -0.005 (95% CI: -0.025–0.014; p = 0.6197). On the other hand, the comparison between BRI and VAI revealed a difference of 0.120 (95% CI: 0.090–0.154; p &lt; 0.001), indicating that under this criterion, BRI and VAI also significantly differ. Discussion The present study evaluated the predictive capacity of three noninvasive and low-cost anthropometric indicators in discriminating MS in a representative sample of farmers from Espírito Santo. ROC curve analysis revealed that LAP, VAI, and BRI were effective in distinguishing MS, with AUCs greater than 0.7, and LAP and BRI had greater diagnostic accuracy. To date, this is the first study to evaluate, compare, and determine cutoff points for these parameters in discriminating MS in a rural population in Brazil. The criteria established by the NCEP and IDF for diagnosing MS differ in their emphasis on central obesity, with the IDF requiring WC as a mandatory component, while the NCEP considers it as one of the five equally weighted factors. These differences can lead to variations in prevalence estimates and may influence the predictive performance of adiposity indices such as LAP, VAI, and BRI. The stricter WC threshold used by the IDF may result in higher sensitivity for detecting central obesity-related metabolic disturbances, whereas the NCEP criteria might capture a broader spectrum of metabolic alterations. In this study, the indices demonstrated better performance when the IDF criteria were applied. This can be attributed to the direct incorporation of WC, TG, and HDL-c —variables that are integral to the calculation of LAP, VAI, and BRI — into the IDF definition of MS. As a result, there is greater alignment between the components of the indices and the diagnostic framework, leading to improved discriminatory capacity. Additionally, the IDF’s focus on central obesity aligns more closely with the physiological basis of these indices, which predominantly reflect visceral adiposity [ 10 , 11 ] and lipid accumulation [ 12 ]. Among the indices studied, LAP stands out for its simplicity, low cost, and applicability in different populations [ 18 ], and has emerged as the best indicator for MS. Similar results have been reported in other studies comparing various anthropometric indices in different populations [ 18 – 20 ]. In the present study, the cutoff point found for LAP was 33.84 according to the NCEP criterion and 26.80 for the IDF criterion. In Brazil, Nascimento-Ferreira et al. [ 21 ] reported a similar value (34.2) in adults without cardiovascular diseases (CVD) or diabetes mellitus 2. On the other hand, most authors suggest higher cutoff points for the general population. Bilgin-Göçer et al. [ 22 ] proposed a cutoff value of 44.5 for Turkish adults; for the Indian and Romanian populations, the values found were 38.05 [ 23 ] and 37.1 [ 24 ], respectively. This difference in LAP thresholds for MS may be due to ethnic variations in insulin resistance and abdominal fat distribution, the age of the study participants, and the diagnostic criteria for MS used [ 18 ]. The VAI index is an important indicator of insulin resistance and visceral obesity and is associated with the risk of CVD [ 11 , 18 ]. Considerable evidence suggests that VAI plays a significant role in predicting MS [18; 25; 26]. In the present study, the AUC values for predicting MS were 0.719 for the NCEP criterion and 0.771 for the IDF criterion, with a cutoff point of 1.60. This value was similar to that reported for the adult population in Turkey (1.61) [ 22 ] and Venezuela (1.70) [ 27 ]. The results of the present study also revealed the utility of BRI in identifying farmers with MS. BRI is an indicator of body fat percentage and visceral adipose tissue and can be used to assess diseases such as obesity, CVD, and diabetes [ 12 , 28 ]. In the present study, BRI demonstrated good discriminatory capacity for MS, with AUCs above 0.7 for both diagnostic criteria, confirming the utility of this indicator for this purpose, as discussed by Rico-Martín et al. [ 28 ] in a recent meta-analysis. The proposed cutoff points were 3.97 and 3.87 according to the NCEP and IDF, respectively. Similar values have been suggested for the Chinese [ 29 – 33 ], Vietnamese [ 34 ], and Taiwanese populations [ 35 ]. The differences observed in the cutoff values for LAP, VAI, and BRI compared to international studies likely reflect the unique sociodemographic, genetic, and environmental characteristics of the population under study. Variations in lifestyle factors, dietary patterns, and levels of physical activity may also contribute to these discrepancies [ 36 ]. Such differences underscore the importance of developing population-specific thresholds to enhance the accuracy and relevance of predictive models for MS. From a public health perspective, the establishment of locally derived cutoff points allows for more precise identification of at-risk individuals, facilitating early interventions and the allocation of resources in a targeted manner [ 37 ]. Incorporating these findings into public health policies can aid in the development of screening protocols tailored to rural populations, where access to healthcare services may be limited. By integrating LAP, VAI, and BRI into routine assessments, health professionals can leverage simple, cost-effective tools to mitigate the burden of metabolic syndrome and associated cardiovascular diseases. The median values of each indicator were consistently greater in individuals with MS compared to those without the syndrome, indicating greater visceral adiposity in MS patients than in controls. Similarly, Ray et al. [ 23 ] and Macut et al. [ 38 ] reported significantly higher LAP values in patients with MS. Regarding VAI, Bilgin-Göçer et al. [ 22 ], Pekgor et al. [ 39 ], and Qing et al. [ 40 ] also reported the same relationship. Finally, Bilgin-Göçer et al. [ 22 ] and Endukuru et al. [ 41 ] reported higher BRI values in patients with MS compared to controls. In the present study, LAP was strongly correlated with TG and moderately correlated with WC. There were weak correlations between LAP and SBP, DBP, and fasting plasma glucose. Additionally, there was a moderate negative correlation between LAP and HDL-c. Similar correlations were reported by Fahmy et al. [ 19 ], Rotter et al. [ 42 ], and İlhan &amp; Yıldızhan [ 43 ]. VAI was strongly correlated with TG and moderately correlated with HDL-c. Correlations with fasting plasma glucose, WC, SBP, and DBP were weak but significant. Researchers evaluating the sensitivity of five anthropometric indices in predicting MS in Chinese adult patients with growth hormone deficiency reported that VAI was correlated with most components of MS, except for SBP [ 40 ]. In Brazil, VAI was significantly associated with several components of MS, including elevated blood glucose, HDL-c, and TG [ 44 ]. Finally, BRI was moderately correlated with WC and weakly correlated with fasting plasma glucose, DBP, and TG. BRI also had a weak negative correlation with HDL-c and a very weak and nonsignificant correlation with SBP. In Turkey, researchers have reported a moderate positive correlation with fasting plasma glucose and SBP, as well as a negative correlation with HDL-c levels [ 23 ]. Notably, this is the first study to compare and determine cutoff points for different anthropometric indicators to discriminate MS in a rural population in Brazil. Owing to their simplicity and low cost, the use of such indicators in clinical practice could represent an advance in screening individuals at risk of developing a range of pathologies, especially in areas with limited access. This study has some limitations. First, its cross-sectional design requires caution in interpreting the results because of the possibility of reverse causality. Furthermore, owing to the scarcity of studies in the literature using the same methodology in similar populations, the results could not be compared with greater precision. The sociodemographic, occupational, and lifestyle data were collected through interviews and questionnaires, which may be subject to recall bias. Although efforts were made to minimize this by using structured instruments and trained interviewers, some degree of inaccuracy in self-reported information may persist. Also, the LAP, VAI, and BRI indices incorporate variables that are also part of the diagnostic criteria for MS, such as WC, TG, and HDL-c. This overlap may contribute to the high predictive performance observed for these indices, potentially inflating their discriminatory power. However, despite this limitation, these indices are widely recognized and utilized in clinical and epidemiological settings due to their practicality and strong association with cardiometabolic risk. Future studies are encouraged to explore alternative indices that may provide additional discriminatory power without relying on the same components used in the definition of metabolic syndrome. Conclusions On the basis of the data and information presented in the present study, it is concluded that the LAP, VAI, and BRI indicators demonstrated good discriminatory capacity for identifying MS in a rural population in Brazil. LAP stood out as the most effective indicator, especially due to its simplicity, low cost, and applicability in different populations. BRI also showed high accuracy, especially with the IDF criteria, whereas VAI, although useful, showed limitations in terms of sensitivity. The findings of this study highlight the significant predictive capacity of LAP, VAI, and BRI in identifying metabolic syndrome, reinforcing their potential as valuable tools for early screening and risk stratification, particularly in rural populations. Incorporating these indices into routine clinical practice may facilitate the identification of individuals at higher risk, enabling earlier interventions aimed at preventing the progression to more severe cardiometabolic conditions. From a public health perspective, the use of simple, cost-effective indicators such as LAP, VAI, and BRI can enhance large-scale screening programs in resource-limited settings, supporting targeted health promotion and preventive strategies. Future policies could benefit from integrating these indices into national guidelines for metabolic syndrome surveillance, contributing to a reduction in the burden of cardiovascular diseases and type 2 diabetes.
