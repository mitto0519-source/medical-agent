Abstract Background Despite extensive research efforts aimed at unraveling the neural mechanisms underlying Internet gaming disorder (IGD), reproducibility remains a challenge, largely due to overlooking the clinical and biological diversity within individuals affected by IGD. Therefore, investigating the altered brain features associated with IGD within both individual-shared and individual-specific subspaces is crucial for understanding this complex and heterogeneous disorder. Methods This study included 555 participants, comprising 326 individuals with IGD and 229 recreational game users (RGUs). Firstly, we computed altered functional connectivity (AFC) matrices for individuals with IGD and compared them with those of RGUs. Subsequently, we applied the common orthogonal basis extraction algorithm to partition the AFC of individuals with IGD into individual-shared and individual-specific subspaces. Finally, we examined brain regions exhibiting generally abnormal patterns in the individual-shared subspace and employed multiple linear regression analysis to assess the predictive influence of AFC within the individual-specific subspace on clinical symptoms. Results Our findings revealed individual-shared altered patterns in the visual network, medial frontal network (MFN), and frontoparietal network (FPN) among individuals with IGD, which are associated with executive control and visual processing. Within the individual-specific subspace, we observed that AFC within the default mode network could predict scores related to fun-seeking behavior in the behavioral activation system (BAS), while AFC within the MFN correlated with reward responsiveness and drive scores in the BAS. Additionally, AFC within the FPN was predictive of scores in the behavioral inhibition system. Conclusions This study successfully decomposed the AFC of IGD into individual-shared and individual-specific subspaces. The AFC within individual-specific subspaces holds promise as potential biomarkers for elucidating clinical symptoms in IGD, thereby offering an analytical framework for investigating heterogeneity in other addictive behaviors. Introduction Internet gaming disorder (IGD) presents as a heterogeneous and multifaced condition, characterized by diverse clinical symptoms ( Hyun et al., 2015 ; Z. L. Wang, Potenza, et al., 2023 ). It is frequently associated with comorbidities such as depression, panic disorder, anxiety, and social phobia ( Carlisle, 2021 ). The varied and intricate clinical symptoms of IGD pose a significant challenge to its diagnosis and treatment ( G.-H. Dong, Dai, &amp; Potenza, 2024 ). Delving into the heterogeneity of IGD could prove crucial in unraveling its neurobiology, ultimately facilitating more accurate diagnosis and tailored behavioral interventions. Recent studies have confirmed that IGD can be defined as a pathological condition characterized by abnormal functional connections among brain networks (G.-H. Dong, H. Dong, et al., 2021 ; Park et al., 2017 ). The core clinical symptoms of IGD entail a specific deficit in affective and cognitive functions ( Yan, Li, Yu, &amp; Zhao, 2021 ). For instance, the default mode network (DMN) has been implicated in cognitive processes such as self-related cognition ( D. Lee, Lee, Lee, &amp; Jung, 2017 ; Ma et al., 2011 ) and emotional regulation ( G. Dong, Li, Wang, &amp; Potenza, 2017 ). Several resting-state functional connectivity (rsFC) studies have observed diminished interaction within the DMN in individuals with IGD ( G. Dong, Li, Wang, Potenza, et al., 2017 ; Liu et al., 2021 ; L. Wang et al., 2016 ). This reduction is negatively correlated with the severity of IGD symptoms ( Bae, Han, Jung, Nam, &amp; Renshaw, 2017 ). Furthermore, deficits in emotional regulation can predict future IGD symptoms ( Estévez, Jáuregui, Sánchez-Marcos, López-González, &amp; Griffiths, 2017 ; Wichstrøm, Stenseng, Belsky, von Soest, &amp; Hygen, 2019 ). Additionally, the functional connectivity between the frontoparietal network (FPN), implicated in planning and executive functions ( Noël, Van Der Linden, &amp; Bechara, 2006 ), and the limbic network, associated with emotional processing and reward ( Strack &amp; Deutsch, 2004 ), could represent a crucial neural mechanism underlying behavioral inflexibility and deficits in craving regulation in individuals with IGD. For instance, our previous studies have demonstrated abnormal connections among functional networks implicated in executive control and reward processing in individuals with IGD ( G.-H. Dong, M. Wang, et al., 2021 ; G. Dong, Lin, &amp; Potenza, 2015 ; W. R. Zhou, Wang, Zheng, Wang, &amp; Dong, 2021 ; Wei-ran Zhou et al., 2022 ). Furthermore, we found that the rsFC between these networks was negatively correlated with addiction severity and craving scores ( W. R. Zhou et al., 2021 ). These case-control studies primarily emphasize group-level functional connectivity, disregarding the biological distinctions among individuals. However, research has demonstrated that differences in brain structure and functions among individuals offer a plethora of scientifically and clinically valuable information ( Kanai &amp; Rees, 2011 ; Kashyap et al., 2019 ). Moreover, rsFC has been extensively employed in predicting behavioral outcomes, spanning from cognitive functions to personality traits ( Bertolero, Yeo, Bassett, &amp; D’Esposito, 2018 ). Therefore, it is crucial to broaden our investigation beyond group-level distinctions and delve into the predictive capacity of rsFC within these individual-specific subspaces. Recreational game users (RGU) are individuals who engage in frequent gaming but do so exclusively for recreational purposes, maintaining a controlled approach ( Kuss &amp; Griffiths, 2012 ). RGUs do not develop gaming addiction in the absence of experiencing the adverse effects of pathological gameplay ( S. Chen, Li, Wang, Du, &amp; Dong, 2020 ). Thus, including RGU as a control group could mitigate the potential influence of gaming experience and familiarity with gaming-related cues on the neural characteristics of individuals with IGD ( S. Chen et al., 2020 ; G. Dong, Li, Wang, &amp; Potenza, 2018 ). The connectivity patterns among brain functional networks in individuals with IGD differ from those observed among RGUs ( W. R. Zhou et al., 2021 ). Therefore, we first analyzed the altered functional connectivity (AFC) in IGD compared to that in RGU. Subsequently, we applied common orthogonal basis extraction (COBE) to decompose the AFC matrix in IGD. The COBE algorithm was initially designed for projecting “multi-block” data, which consists of a collection of matrices, into a block-shared subspace that is shared by all blocks, along with block-specific subspace ( G. Zhou, Cichocki, Zhang, &amp; Mandic, 2015 ; G. Zhou et al., 2016 ). In this study, the AFC matrix of each participant could be considered a block. Applying COBE to the AFC matrices of all participants allowed for the decomposition of each participant's AFC matrix into an individual-shared subspace and an individual-specific subspace. AFC in the individual-shared subspace reflects the common abnormal neural pattern. Hence, its elimination may enhance prediction performance ( Kashyap et al., 2019 ). Moreover, the functional connectivity within individual-specific subspaces reflects unique individual differences. Using COBE, we identified shared components across individuals and explored abnormal brain regions specific to each individual with IGD. We also investigated the relationship between the Frobenius norm of functional connectivity matrix in individual-specific subspaces (FAFC) and clinical symptoms. The Frobenius norm serves as an overall measure that reflects the global features of the network. Finally, we employed regression analysis to assess the predictive capability of functional connectivity within individual-specific subspaces on clinical symptomatology. Methods and materials Participants This study initially included fMRI data from 601 participants. However, 46 participants were excluded from the analysis due to head motion exceeding 3 mm, leaving a total of 555 participants. Among these, 326 had IGD (men: 183; femen: 143), and 229 had RGU (men: 139; femen: 90). Participants were recruited continuously over a five-year period from 2019 to 2024 to ensure a sufficiently large and diverse sample. All participants were right-handed college students with normal or corrected-to-normal vision (self-reported visual acuity of 0.8 or better). They did not have a history of illegal drug use or psychiatric disorders. Demographic details are presented in Table 1 . Refer to the Supplementary Information (Methods S1) for details on the inclusion and exclusion criteria for participants. Clinical measures All participants filled out an 18-item short version of the Cognitive Emotion Regulation Questionnaire (CERQ) ( Garnefsk &amp; Kraaij, 2006 ) and a 20-item Behavioral Inhibition System/Behavioral Activation System scale (BIS/BAS) ( Carver &amp; White, 1994 ). Further assessment details are available in Supplementary Information (Methods S2) . Image acquisition and fMRI data pre-processing Resting-state fMRI data were acquired at Hangzhou Normal University using a 3T Siemens Trio MRI system, with a scan duration of approximately 7 minutes. To mitigate the impact of machine noise and head movement, participants wore earplugs and were positioned with a foam-padded head coil. Imaging parameters consisted of a repetition time (TR) of 2,000 ms, 33 interleaved axial slices with 3.0 mm thickness, echo time (TE) of 30 ms, flip angle of 90°, field of view (FOV) of 220 × 220 mm, and matrix size of 64 × 64. Preprocessing was performed using DPABI version 5.3, involving several steps: (i) discarding the initial ten volumes; (ii) correcting for slice timing; (iii) correcting head motion and scrubbing; (iv) registering the images to the standard Montreal Neurological Institute (MNI) space; (v) applying spatial smoothing with a full width at half maximum of 4 mm; (vi) removing linear trends; (vii) regressing out nuisance covariates; and (viii) band-pass filtering (0.01–0.1 Hz). Participants with translation or rotational head motion exceeding 3 mm were excluded from further analysis. Network construction This study utilized Shen's brain atlas, which comprises 268 regions of interest (ROIs) distributed across ten functional networks: the medial frontal network (MFN), frontoparietal network (FPN), default mode network (DMN), limbic network (LN), motor network (MN), visual networks I and II (VN-I and VN-II), visual association network (VAN), basal ganglia network (BGN), and cerebellum (Ce). This atlas groups together voxels with similar BOLD signal timecourses, resulting in functionally homogeneous regions. For each ROI, we extracted the voxel-wise BOLD timecourses and computed the mean time series by averaging across all voxels within the ROI. This average time series was then used to represent the functional activity of the ROI. Resting-state functional connectivity (FC) was computed by averaging the time series of the 268 ROIs, resulting in a 268 × 268 FC matrix for each participant. The correlation coefficients were then subjected to Fisher's Z transformation. Assess the AFC of IGD individuals compared to RGU Based on previous studies ( H. Chen et al., 2022 ), we defined AFC as the difference in FC levels between participants with IGD and RGUs. Each IGD participant yielded a 268 × 268 AFC matrix. The formula is as follows: F C a l t e r e d = F C I G D − m e a n ( F C R G U ) S D ( F C R G U ) Common orthogonal basis extraction (COBE) The analysis process is depicted in Fig. 1 . Drawing from previously published studies ( G. Zhou et al., 2015 ; G. Zhou et al., 2016 ), we used the COBE algorithm to project AFC in IGD into individual-shared and individual-specific subspaces. The COBE algorithm code can be found in the Supplementary Information (Methods S3) . Here, we provide a concise overview of its application to AFC matrices in this study. We acquired a 268 × 268 AFC matrix for each IGD subject, with an individual's AFC matrix considered as a block ( Kashyap et al., 2019 ). Let S n denote the 268 × 268 AFC matrix of the n -th subject. COBE aims to decompose S n into S n = A ¯ Y ¯ n T + A ˆ n Y ˆ n T = individual-shared subspace + individual-specific subspace, where A ¯ is a 268 × C matrix and C represents the number of components spanning the individual-shared subspace and is a user-defined parameter. Y ¯ n T is a C × 268 matrix representing the subject-specific weight of common components. We can derive the common components spanning the individual-shared subspace ( A ¯ ) and corresponding weights ( Y ¯ n T ) using the COBE algorithm. Subsequently, we computed the matrix products A ¯ Y ¯ n T and A ˆ n Y ˆ n T ( A ˆ n Y ˆ n T = S n − A ¯ Y ¯ n T ) . A ¯ Y ¯ n T is a 268 × 268 matrix depicting the projection of the n -th subject's AFC matrix onto the individual-shared subspace, whereas A ˆ n Y ˆ n T is a 268 × 268 matrix representing the projection of the n -th subject's AFC matrix onto the individual-specific subspace. An essential parameter is the C. We investigated C values ranging from 2 to 9 and identified the most stable parameter (C = 4) as the optimal number of common components. Details are presented in the Supplementary Information (Methods S4) . Common components in the individual-shared subspace We derived common components using COBE and associated them with specific networks by applying thresholds based on ROI weights. Each component was assigned a threshold of 20%, and the average number of surviving ROIs for each network was determined. A higher count signifies a greater contribution from the network. Correlation analysis for AFC in the individual-specific subspace We computed the Frobenius norm of the AFC matrix in the individual-specific subspace (FAFC) across the entire brain and examined its correlation with clinical symptoms ( Xiaolong Shan et al., 2024 ). Furthermore, we investigated the relationship between FAFC of specific subnetworks and clinical symptoms. Higher FAFC values indicate more pronounced individual differences. Predicting clinical symptoms using AFC in the individual-specific subspace Multiple linear regression analysis was employed to assess the predictive influence of FAFC in the individual-specific subspace on clinical symptoms. FAFC of specific subnetworks served as independent variables, while clinical features were considered dependent variables. Ethics The research has obtained approval from the Human Investigations Committee of Hangzhou Normal University, and the experiment strictly adheres to the Code of Ethics outlined by the World Medical Association (Declaration of Helsinki). Prior to the scan, all participants provided written informed consent. Results Common components in the individual-shared subspace We determined that the decomposition remained stable with C = 4, leading us to focus on the solution with four common components in this study. Common component 1 exhibited associations with the FPN, LN, VN-I, and VN-II ( Fig. 2A ), while common component 2 was associated with the FPN, MN, VN-I, VN-II, and Ce ( Fig. 2B ). Similarly, common component 3 corresponded to the MFN, DMN, MN, and VAN ( Fig. 2C ), and common component 4 was associated with the FPN, DMN, VN-I, VN-II, and BGN ( Fig. 2D ). Overall, a consistent abnormal pattern within the executive control and visual brain areas persisted across all four common components. Relationships between AFC in the individual-specific subspace and clinical symptoms We calculated FAFC for IGD subjects across the whole brain and observed a negative correlation between FAFC in IGD and CERQ scores, as well as BAS subscores (CERQ: r = −0.139, p = 0.026; BAS-Reward Responsiveness: r = −0.153, p = 0.008; BAS-Drive: r = −0.145, p = 0.013; BAS-Fun Seeking: r = −0.156, p = 0.007). We further explored the correlation between FAFC of IGD within subnetworks and clinical symptoms. We found FAFC within MFN, FPN, and DMN was negatively associated with CERQ scores (MFN: r = −0.143, p = 0.022; FPN: r = −0.154, p = 0.014; DMN: r = −0.185, p = 0.003). FAFC within MFN and FPN exhibited a negative association with BIS scores (MFN: r = −0.125, p = 0.033; FPN: r = −0.139, p = 0.017). FAFC within MFN, FPN, and DMN showed negative associations with BAS-Reward Responsiveness scores (MFN: r = −0.162, p = 0.005; FPN: r = −0.149, p = 0.010; DMN: r = −0.129, p = 0.028). FAFC within MFN, FPN, DMN, and LN was negative association with BAS-Drive scores (MFN: r = −0.133, p = 0.022; FPN: r = −0.121, p = 0.038; DMN: r = −0.123, p = 0.036; LN: r = −0.122, p = 0.037). FAFC within MFN, FPN and DMN showed a negative association with BAS-Fun Seeking scores (MFN: r = −0.133, p = 0.023; FPN: r = −0.136, p = 0.020; DMN: r = −0.140, p = 0.016) ( Fig. 3 and supplementary information ). Predicting clinical symptoms using AFC in the individual-specific subspace Building upon the results of correlation analysis, we further investigated the predictive potential of FAFC within specific networks for clinical symptoms. We found that FAFC within the DMN could predict BAS- Fun Seeking scores. The regression model for BAS- Fun Seeking was represented by y = − 0.133 x + 0.055 , R 2 = 0.016, p = 0.016 ( Fig. 4A ). Similarly, FAFC within the MFN could predict BAS-Reward Responsiveness and Drive scores. The regression model for BAS-Reward Responsiveness was represented by y = − 0.096 x + 0.034 , R 2 = 0.023, p = 0.005 ( Fig. 4B ), whereas the regression model for BAS-Drive was represented by y = − 0.059 x + 0.025 , R 2 = 0.014, p = 0.022 ( Fig. 4C ). FAFC within the FPN could predict BIS scores, with the regression model represented by y = − 0.079 x + 0.033 , R 2 = 0.016, p = 0.017 ( Fig. 4D ). Discussion We utilized COBE to analyze AFC matrices in IGD and disentangled the individual-shared subspace and individual-specific subspace of AFC in IGD. Our findings reveal abnormal patterns within the executive control and visual brain areas in the individual-shared subspace. Additionally, AFC within specific networks in the individual-specific subspace shows promise in predicting clinical symptoms. The abnormal pattern of AFC within brain regions associated with executive function and visual processing in individual-shared subspace Four common components spanning the individual-shared subspace displayed distinct abnormal patterns. Particularly noteworthy is the consistency of the abnormal pattern within the executive function network (MFN or FPN) and the vision-related brain region (VN-I or VAN) across all four shared components. The medial frontal and frontoparietal networks represent key components among the resting-state networks involved in regulating executive control processes ( Weng, Huang, Lee, &amp; Ho, 2021 ). Executive control systems are postulated to facilitate the regulation of both cognitive and behavioral responses to motivational impulses, allowing individuals to exert restraint over impulses and modulate engagement in reward-driven behaviors ( Nee, 2021 ). Executive dysfunction is prevalent among individuals with IGD ( J. Lee, Lee, Namkoong, &amp; Jung, 2020 ; Soares et al., 2023 ; M. Wang, H. Dong, H. Zheng, X. Du, &amp; G. H. Dong, 2020 ; W. R. Zhou et al., 2021 ). Cognitive behavioral models of IGD propose that weakened inhibitory control over seeking rewards behaviors contributes to the perpetuation of addictive behaviors ( G. Dong &amp; Potenza, 2014 ). Accordingly, numerous experimental studies have reached similar conclusions. For example, studies employing various paradigms have demonstrated deficient brain activation in regions associated with executive control (e.g. dorsolateral prefrontal cortex and the orbitofrontal cortex) ( G. Dong, Li, Wang, &amp; Potenza, 2017 ). Additionally, abnormal functional connectivity (FC) has been documented between brain areas responsible for executive control and reward processing in individuals with IGD ( G. Dong, Lin, Hu, Xie, &amp; Du, 2015 ; M. Wang et al., 2022 ). Moreover, the “unsuccessful attempts to control the participation in Internet games” and “continued excessive use of Internet games despite knowledge of psychosocial problems” criteria outlined in the DSM-5 both indicate impaired executive control function among individuals with IGD ( Carlisle, 2021 ). The vision-related brain region (VN-I or VAN) includes the middle occipital gyrus and inferior temporal gyrus. The occipital lobe serves as the primary visual processing center within the mammalian brain, encompassing the majority of the anatomical area of the visual cortex ( Kojima &amp; Suzuki, 2010 ; Tam et al., 2008 ). The inferior temporal gyrus is a component of the upper tiers within the ventral stream responsible for audio and visual processing ( Lewald, Staedtgen, Sparing, &amp; Meister, 2011 ). Individuals with IGD tend to spend prolonged periods staring at computer screens, which can lead to prolonged visual attention and subsequent impairment of visual functions ( G. Dong, Huang, Du, &amp; Functions, 2012 ; Konstantinidis &amp; Guex-Crosier, 2016 ). These changes are often detectable in relevant brain regions. For example, individuals with IGD exhibit reduced activity in the inferior temporal gyrus ( G. Dong &amp; Potenza, 2016 ; Zhang et al., 2023 ) and decreased ReHo in occipital, temporal, and parietal brain regions ( G. Dong et al., 2012 ). Overall, the findings of our study align with those of previous research. Furthermore, we provide evidence for abnormal executive and visual functions, underscoring the common impairment of these two brain regions in individuals with ASD. The predictive effect of FAFC within the DMN on BAS-Fun Seeking scores We used FAFC to characterize the alterations of AFC in the individual-specific subspace in IGD. FAFC in the whole brain was negatively associated with clinical symptoms (CERQ and BAS scores). A lower FAFC indicated more severe symptoms. These associations suggest that FAFC may reflect the neural substrates underlying the clinical symptoms of IGD. However, these findings are preliminary and exploratory, and further validation is needed to confirm their robustness. Additionally, we explored how FAFC within specific networks correlated with clinical symptoms. Consequently, we employed regression analysis to further investigate their predictive relationship. The predictive effect of FAFC was within the DMN on BAS- Fun Seeking scores. BAS reflects the active response of the neural system toward signals of reward ( Carver &amp; White, 1994 ). People exhibiting a heightened BAS are prone to participating in addictive behaviors driven by positive reinforcement ( O’Connor, Stewart, &amp; Watt, 2009 ). Fun seeking signifies the individual's inclination and preparedness to pursue new rewards ( O’Connor et al., 2009 ). The medial prefrontal cortex (mPFC), posterior cingulate cortex (PCC), and precuneus are recognized as major components of the DMN ( Qin &amp; Northoff, 2011 ). DMN is associated with self-cognitive processing ( Raichle, 2015 ) and internal mental activity ( Andrews-Hanna, 2012 ). Davis's cognitive-behavioral model of internet addiction suggests that dysfunctional self-reference and negative cognitions are significant factors in the development and persistence of IGD ( Davis, 2001 ). Individuals with IGD who exhibit a high degree of addiction tend to experience severe negative self-concept, negative emotions, and craving for games ( G. Dong, Li, Wang, Potenza, et al., 2017 ; Green, Delfabbro, &amp; King, 2020 ; Leménager et al., 2016 ). The aberrant function of the DMN may serve as the neurological underpinning of the aforementioned symptoms. For example, the mPFC, known for its involvement in self-reflection and emotional processing ( Andrews-Hanna, Smallwood, &amp; Spreng, 2014 ), exhibits disrupted effective connections in individuals with IGD, particularly affecting its connectivity to the amygdala and PCC ( M. Wang et al., 2022 ; W. Zhou et al., 2021 ). Regions mPFC and precuneus are highly activated in individuals with IGD during self-processing ( Choi et al., 2018 ). Moreover, individuals with IGD exhibit deficits in transitioning into the striatal-DMN network, potentially contributing to their inclination toward game reward-seeking behavior ( L. Wang, Zhang, et al., 2023 ). Consistent with our results, a previous study discovered that connectivity within the DMN and FC between the DMN and subcortical regions could predict craving responses to game cues ( Wei-ran Zhou et al., 2022 ). Hence, utilizing DMN neural activity indicators may offer a viable approach to predicting clinical symptoms related to reward-seeking (Fun-seeking) in individuals with IGD. FAFC within the MFN predicts BAS-reward responsiveness and drive scores Reward responsiveness refers to an individual's sensitivity towards present or anticipated reward stimuli, whereas reward drive pertains to the motivation to persistently pursue desired goals ( O’Connor et al., 2009 ). A region of the MFN includes the dorsal anterior cingulate cortex (dACC) and the pre-supplementary motor area ( Venkatraman, Rosati, Taren, &amp; Huettel, 2009 ). The individual neuronal firing rate in the dACC is sensitive to the reward value acquired, encompassing both acquisition and deprivation of secondary rewards (i.e. gaming ( W.-R. Zhou et al., 2021 )). In addition, lesions in the MFN typically diminish sensitivity to rewards, implying an association between the MFN and reward responsiveness ( Weinstein, 2023 ). Individuals with IGD commonly display a heightened sensitivity to gaming or game-related stimuli ( N. Zeng, M. Wang, H. Dong, X. Du, &amp; G. H. Dong, 2022 ; W.-R. Zhou et al., 2021 ), a trait that could potentially be identified via a decrease in FAFC within the MFN region. Moreover, both the medial and lateral prefrontal cortex are crucial in modulating reward motivation ( Kouneiher, Charron, &amp; Koechlin, 2009 ), with the dorsolateral prefrontal cortex serving to integrate and relay reward signals to the mesolimbic and mesocortical dopamine circuits, thereby instigating motivated behavior ( Weinstein, 2023 ). However, individuals with IGD commonly exhibit impaired circuits connecting the frontal area to the reward brain area (e.g. frontostriatal circuits), thereby impacting motivational behavior ( H. Dong et al., 2021 ; M. Wang, Zheng, et al., 2023 ; N. Zeng, M. Wang, H. Dong, X. Du, &amp; G.-H. J. C. s. Dong, 2022 ). For instance, prolonged, excessive gaming disrupts the integrity and functionality of the orbitofrontal cortex-caudate neural pathway, resulting in inadequate regulation of individual behavior by reward-driven motivations ( N. Zeng, Wang, Dong, Du, &amp; Dong, 2022 ). Expanding upon previous findings indicating a strong association between the MFN region and reward motivation, our study proposes that neural alterations (reduction of FAFC) in the MFN region may serve as predictors of reward motivation in individuals with IGD. The FAFC within the frontoparietal networks could predict behavioral inhibition system scores The BIS was perceived as a mechanism that inhibits behavior when confronted with cues indicating potential punishment ( Corr, 2004 ). Individuals with a heightened BIS may be susceptible to participating in addictive behaviors for negative reinforcement ( O’Connor et al., 2009 ). The FPN is primarily associated with inhibitory control ( W. Wang et al., 2018 ). Prolonged gaming behavior in individuals with IGD impairs executive function, resulting in ineffective inhibition of heightened cravings for gaming ( M. Wang, H. Dong, H. Zheng, X. Du, &amp; G.-H. Dong, 2020 ). Furthermore, individuals with IGD exhibit imbalanced FC between the executive control network and reward pathways ( G. Dong, Lin, &amp; Potenza, 2015 ). This imbalance suggests inefficiency or excessive processing within the reward/control circuitry in such individuals ( G. Dong, Lin, &amp; Potenza, 2015 ; von Deneen et al., 2022 ). In this study, we discovered that the inhibitory control behavior of individuals with IGD can be predicted by FAFC within FPN. This finding suggests that these neural signatures could serve as potential biomarkers for understanding inhibitive behavior function in IGD. Limitations While this study represents the inaugural application of the COBE algorithm to investigate the predictive significance of rsFC within individual-specific subspaces in IGD, several limitations merit consideration. Firstly, although our study utilized a substantial dataset from China, it is imperative to corroborate these results through replication studies encompassing large cohorts from varied geographical regions. Secondly, the absence of cognitive tasks evaluating impulsivity, cognitive flexibility, and decision-making in the current study is noteworthy. Future research efforts should encompass a broader spectrum of cognitive assessments to further elucidate the unique characteristics of IGD. Conclusions This study decomposed AFC of IGD into individual-shared subspaces and individual-specific subspaces. Within the individual-shared subspace, we identified common abnormalities in executive control and visual brain regions among individuals with IGD. Furthermore, our findings demonstrated that AFC in the individual-specific subspaces could effectively predict clinical symptoms in IGD. Overall, our study offers insights into the heterogeneity of IGD, underscores the predictive value of AFC in individual-specific subspaces for clinical symptoms, and establishes links between potential neural biomarkers and behavioral manifestations.
