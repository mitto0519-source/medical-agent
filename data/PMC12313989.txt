To view a copy of this licence, visit http://creativecommons.org/licenses/by-nc-nd/4.0/ . Abstract Internet gaming disorder (IGD) is recognized as a mental health issue. Traditional interventions have limitations, but mindfulness meditation (MM) shows promise due to its flexibility and social acceptance. Study 1, fMRI data from 61 IGD patients and 60 healthy controls (HCs) were compared to assess functional connectivity (FC). Study 2- a randomized clinical trial, 80 IGD patients underwent either an MM intervention (twice-weekly for 8 sessions) or progressive muscle relaxation (PMR) as a control (pre-registered-Chinese clinical trial registry, ChiCTR2300075869, September 18, 2023). Study 1 revealed abnormal FC within the executive control network (ECN) and between the ECN and reward network in IGD patients. Study 2 showed that MM enhanced FC within the ECN and frontostriatal pathway. MM refining the coupling between brain regions involved in executive control and reward processing. This enhancement improves top-down control over game craving. These findings suggest that MM can effectively treat IGD. Introduction Internet gaming disorder (IGD) is defined as an individual showing a persistent and recurrent pattern of abnormal gaming behavior to online gaming that leads to significant impairment or distress in personal, family, social, educational, occupational, or other important domains 1 , 2 . A recent meta-analysis involving over 400,000 participants in 33 countries showed an IGD prevalence rate of approximately 9.9%, with rates of 8.8% and 10.4% for adolescents and young individuals, respectively 3 . As observed for other addictive behaviors, IGD may lead to problems such as depression 4 and suicidal tendencies 5 . Recent studies indicate that IGD can impair functional brain activity, leading to issues such as abnormal low-frequency amplitude fluctuations in brain regions such as the anterior cingulate gyrus, middle frontal gyrus, and lingual gyrus 6 . Additionally, IGD is associated with abnormal functional connectivity (FC) in the precuneus 7 , reduced FC between the parahippocampal gyrus (PHG) and the prefrontal lobe 8 , abnormal internal striatal function 9 , abnormal internal executive control and reward networks 10 , and decreased FC within the frontal striatal pathway 11 , 12 . This indicates that IGD can negatively affect the daily life, behavior, and physical well-being of individuals. Therefore, it is urgent to explore effective intervention strategies for IGD. Despite an increased neurobiological understanding of IGD, treatment development in the behavioral, pharmacological, and neuromodulatory domains has remained slow. Current treatment strategies for IGD include cognitive behavioral therapy (CBT), drug therapy, and non-invasive brain stimulation. In recent years, non-invasive brain stimulation technology has been used in intervention research on IGD. According to existing studies, transcranial direct current point stimulation and magnetic stimulation can alleviate the severity of certain IGD symptoms 13 , 14 . However, few relevant studies can confirm its efficacy. Studies on the efficacy of drugs used for IGD treatment are inconclusive 15 . Bupropion and escitalopram are thought to relieve some symptoms 16 , 17 , but are still in the early stages of evaluation. CBT is a commonly used method 18 – 20 , and meta-analysis results show that it can effectively reduce IGD symptoms 21 , but evidence proving that CBT can reduce gaming time is insufficient. In summary, traditional therapies can be effective to a certain extent in intervening in IGD. However, traditional intervention methods are associated with certain limitations. Non-invasive brain stimulation is associated with certain side effects, including pruritus, burning sensation, and tingling at the intervention point 22 , 23 . Drug treatments for IGD carry risks, and currently used drugs were not specifically developed for IGD. Consequently, there are insufficient studies to prove that they do not cause side effects 24 . Most current CBT treatments for IGD are directly based on substance addiction models and do not address the characteristics of IGD 24 . Moreover, in clinical treatment, some IGD patients do not perceive their excessive gaming behavior as a disease, believing that it is not associated with negative effects. Hence, the probability of cooperation with treatment is low, and drop-out rates are high 25 . Therefore, it is difficult to widely apply traditional methods such as CBT, drug therapy, and non-invasive brain stimulation interventions in a population with IGD. Mindfulness meditation (MM) has garnered significant attention from the public and scientific community in recent times. It has been integrated into clinical interventions and scrutinized through neuroimaging studies 26 . When compared to other treatment modalities, MM stands out for its flexibility and societal acceptance, potentially enhancing individuals’ willingness to engage with and adhere to the practice. This would help overcome some obstacles to treatment associated with traditional interventions 2 , 27 . Moreover, the practice of MM may not be confined to treating clinical conditions alone; it could promote overall well-being among the general population 28 , 29 . However, MM has yet to be thoroughly investigated for its effectiveness and tolerability in treating IGD. Studies have shown that MM can improve individual executive control ability 30 and impaired executive control is a key issue in IGD. At present, MM has been widely used in substance addiction interventions. Meta-analysis shows that after MM intervention, individuals’ cravings for addictive substances decreased significantly, top-down control ability improved significantly, decision-making ability was enhanced, and negative emotions were reduced 31 . In addition, MM can effectively restructure the response of drug addicts to drug and natural reward cues 31 – 33 , with corresponding reductions in the FC between the subgenual anterior cingulate cortex (sACC), insula, and striatum 31 . Moreover, the posterior cingulate cortex is significantly activated in the face of drug addiction and is significantly correlated with the reduction in drug use 34 . Additionally, MM can enhance the FC between the default mode network and the prefrontal cortex 35 , 36 , and the self-control network, composed of the ACC and PFC, also restores normal functioning 37 , which further indicates that MM can restore top-down control ability. This indicates that MM may have a positive impact on the reward mechanism and top-down executive control ability of IGD patients with impaired functioning. Regional homogeneity (ReHo) is a resting state functional magnetic resonance imaging indicator used to measure the temporal synchronicity of BOLD signals in brain regions 38 . It has been proven to be a comparatively more reliable method for determining seed points compared to traditional methods 39 , 40 . Degree centrality (DC) is a measure of the degree of connectivity between a node and its neighbors that can effectively help us identify key nodes in the brain 41 . Therefore, the combination of DC and ReHo can effectively reflect the synchronicity of individual local brain and remote activities 42 . FC, as a method for analyzing brain image data, enables the analysis of the temporal correlation between neurophysiological measurements in two different brain regions 43 . Compared with DC and ReHo, it can reflect changes in brain activity more effectively. Therefore, in this study, changes in FC between pre-and post-treatment intervals were considered as indicators of treatment effectiveness. Therefore, in study 1, we randomly recruited two groups: individuals with IGD and healthy controls (HCs) that met the criteria, and performed magnetic resonance imaging (MRI) scans, followed by FC analysis. The purpose was to explore the abnormal FC in individuals with IGD compared to HCs and lay the foundation for study 2. We assumed that the executive control of IGD and the FC of reward-related brain regions were abnormal. In study 2, a group of IGD participants was randomly recruited and assigned to the MM and intervention control groups, and MRI scans were performed before and after the intervention. Subsequently, FC analysis was performed on the MM group and the results were compared with those of HCs (FC was the FC with abnormal IGD in study 1). This was followed by a comparison of FCs between the MM and intervention control groups. The purpose is to explore the effectiveness of MM intervention in IGD from different perspectives. It was assumed that MM could effectively ameliorate FC abnormalities within the execution control network and frontal striatal pathway in individuals with IGD. Methods Ethics This investigation adheres to the Ethical Code of the World Health Organization and was in accordance with the guidelines of the Declaration of Helsinki. The study protocol was ratified by the Human Research Committee of Yunnan Normal University. Each participant furnished written consent for participation prior to starting the experimental procedure. The protocol for the trial was pre-registered at the Chinese clinical trial registry ( Www.chictr.org.cn ; ChiCTR2300075869;September 18, 2023). Participant inclusion and exclusion criteria Potential IGD participants were instructed to complete the online internet addiction test (IAT; Www.netaddiction.net ) 44 , 45 . Individuals with scores &gt;50 were interviewed by a psychiatrist to diagnose IGD in accordance with the DSM-5 criteria 1 . We applied a threshold of at least 6 inclusionary criteria being met to ensure significant IGD 46 . Psychiatric disorders were also assessed using the Mini-International Neuropsychiatric Interview (MINI) 47 . Inclusion criteria for participants with IGD: (1) IAT score greater than 50 points 48 ; (2) DSM-5 score greater than or equal to 5 points; (3) the primary activity associated with internet gaming occurs online; and (4) there is a minimum of two consecutive years of recorded online game usage. Inclusion criteria for HCs: (1) IAT score below 40; (2) did not meet the criteria for IGD in DSM-5; (3) internet gaming is not the main activity on the internet; (4) no other substance addiction. Exclusion criteria for IGD affected individuals and HCs: (1) history of related mental or neurological disorders; (2) confirmed cognitive impairment or Beck Depression Scale scores 49 , 50 indicating depressive tendencies through the Mini-International Neuropsychiatric Interview 47 , 51 ; (3) history of surgery within one year, head injury, or cardiovascular disease; (4) suffering from claustrophobia; (5) implantation of metallic substances or tattoos in the neck area or the area above the neck. Design In the current study 1, 60 HCs and 61 IGD affected individuals were recruited as participants. The demographic data of the participants is displayed in the Supplementary Table 2 . All participants needed to undergo resting state magnetic resonance scanning. In study 2, after rigorous evaluation, 80 participants who fulfilled the eligibility criteria were randomly distributed into two groups: the mindfulness training and control groups. Members of the control group underwent progressive muscle relaxation (PMR) exercises. None of the participants had any knowledge of the existence of the other group. A total of 61 people completed the experiment (31 in the MM group and 30 in the PMR group), including 8 interventions and 2 scans. 19 were excluded due to incomplete participation in the experiment. The demographic details of both groups are presented in Table 1 . The complete flow of this research is presented in Fig. 1 . The raw data used in the study 2 are consistent with a previous study 52 , which was part of the present study, which found that MM can reduce craving by reshape the FC between executive control and default mode network in the IGD. However, this study explored the intervention effectiveness of MM on IGD through different aspects through two consecutive experiments. Behavioral assessment (pre- and post-MM/PMR training) All participants’ addiction levels were assessed before and after the training sessions using the DSM-5 criteria and IAT scores. A modified craving questionnaire based on the Tiffany Questionnaire for Smoking Urges was employed to assess participants’ game craving levels pre- and post-training, and one month after the experiment was concluded. Furthermore, the Five Facet Mindfulness Questionnaire was used to measure the participants’ mindfulness levels. Mindfulness training A typical mindfulness training course should span at least eight weeks, with 2.5–3.5 h of training per week. In this study, participants needed to undergo two MRI scans and training sessions. To ensure adherence, we condensed the mindfulness program to 4 weeks, with two weekly sessions. This adaptation enabled more participants to finish the study and lowered the attrition rate. Originally, mindfulness sessions were held for 10–20 attendees and guided by a professional trainer. However, sporadic dropouts resulted in an inadequate number of participants. Consequently, two certified professional mindfulness instructors were brought in to lead sessions, facilitating training sessions for groups of up to 40 individuals. The control group-PMR In the control group, participants underwent a similar number of sessions over an identical duration but were taught relaxation techniques. We assessed disparities in activities between the experimental and control groups, such as team interventions, group classes, and training camps. PMR was chosen due to its similarity to the MM technique. Developed by Dr. Edmund Jacobson in the early 1900s, PMR effectively reduces anxiety and stress while improving sleep quality. During PMR sessions, instructors monitored participants and addressed questions post-training, in a manner similar to that observed for meditation trainers. Details on PMR selection and implementation procedures are provided in the Supplementary Note 1 and Table 1 . Each session was conducted in a separate room with seating arrangements and yoga mats for sitting or lying down. There were no restrictions or additional requirements during the MM &amp; PMR training; participants were allowed to exhibit their usual gaming habits. Data collection and preprocessing The resting state was collected by 3.0T (GE Healthcare) magnetic resonance imaging. Scanning parameters were as follows: repetition time = 2000 ms, interleaved axial slices = 33, echo time (TE) = 30 ms, thickness = 3.0 mm, flip angle = 90°, field of view (FOV) = 220 × 220 mm, and matrix = 64 × 64. Data preprocessing is carried out in DPABI and includes processes such as removing the first ten time points, slice timing, head motion correction, removing covariates, normalizing by 2 mm, filtering (0.01–0.1), and Gaussian kernel smoothing to 6 mm 3 . ROI selection In study 1, we calculated the ReHo and DC values of the IGD and HC groups, conducted independent sample t -tests (False Discovery Rate (FDR) = 0.05), and performed overlapping calculations. Brain regions such as the left dorsal striatum (L-DS), bilateral middle frontal gyrus (L/R-MFG), medial superior frontal gyrus (mSFG), right insula (R-insula), and bilateral orbitofrontal cortex (L/R-OFC), with overlapping voxels &gt;50 would be used as ROIs for subsequent FC analysis, as shown in Fig. 2A . For continuity, study 2 used the same ROIs as in Study 1. Neural network analysis Neural networks are widely used as machine learning methods in various fields. These include backpropagation, convolutional, and multi-layer perceptron (MLP) type methods, with MLP being the most classic 53 . MLP analysis was performed in this study. In MLP modeling, data are split into the training (train the model) and test (evaluate the model) sets. The neural network model can also recognize the importance of features and predict the data. Statistical analysis In study 1, FC analysis between IGD and HCs involved a practical independent sample t -test. The significance level was p = 0.05 and multiple comparison correction was performed via FDR correction. In study 2, independent sample t -tests were used to analyze differential changes in individuals with MM group’s IGD versus HCs after the intervention. Repeated measures of ANOVA were used to detect changes in IGD FCs between MM and PMR interventions. FDR correction ( p = 0.05) was used to correct for multiple comparisons. Results Study 1 comparison of FC between the executive control network, reward network, and default model network in IGD affected individuals and HCs The results of an independent sample t- test showed that compared to HCs, IGD had abnormal FCs between the mSFG, OFC, MFG, PostCG, IFG, DS, caudate (CAD), amygdala, precuneus, and anterior cingulate cortex (ACC) ( p &lt; 0.001). Speific values are presented in Fig. 2B and the Supplementary Note 2 . Multi-layer perception classification Based on the study 1’s FC results, MLP machine learning was used to construct the classification prediction model. The results showed that the accuracy, precision (comprehensive), and recall (comprehensive) were 92%, and the f1 score was 0.92, indicating that the constructed model was very effective and could accurately distinguish between IGD affected individuals and HCs(Fig. 2C–E ). Model parameters can be found in the Supplementary Table 3 . Based on the study 2’s FC results, MLP machine learning was used to construct the model based on the FCs of the significant outcomes between IGD affected individuals post-intervention and HCs. It was found that classification accuracy was 36%, precision (composite) was 64%, the recall was 36%, and the f1-score (composite model score) was 0.444, indicating that the model showed poor performance and could not distinguish between IGD affected individuals and HCs (Fig. 3B–D ). Model parameters can be found in the Supplementary Table 4 . Study 2 FC before and after the intervention was compared with that of HCs An independent sample t- test found that all the FCs mentioned above were significantly different ( p ) from those of HCs before IGD interventions. However, after the intervention, there were no significant differences in FCs between the R-DS and the left precuneus ( t = −2.092, p = 0.11, Cohen’s d = 0.463), between the L-mSFG, the L-CAD ( t = 1.842, p = 0.061, Cohen’s d = 0.419), and the L-MFG ( t = 1.842, p = 0.069, Cohen’s d = 0.407), and between the L-OFC and the L-IFG ( t = −0.996, p = 0.322, Cohen’s d = 0.22) (Fig. 3A ). Study 2 Behavioral measures before and after intervention Mindfulness levels in the MM group increased significantly ( t [30] = 4.524, p &lt; 0.001, Cohen’s d = 7.364) after one month of training (Fig. 4D ). The results of repeated measures ANOVA showed significant interaction effects between groups and pre-post measurements in game cravings ( F = 22.558, p &lt; 0.001, η 2 = 0.277). In the further analysis, we observed a significant decrease ( t [30] = −8.808, p &lt; 0.001) in the game craving within the MM group after training (Fig. 4B ). However, a slight placebo effect was found in the PMR group, which showed a slight decrease in game craving after training ( t [29] = −1.8, p = 0.01). In the post-test, the MM group had significantly lower ( t [59] = −5.565, p &lt; 0.001) game craving compared with the PMR group. Similar results were found for the IAT (Fig. 4A ) and DSM-5 (Fig. 4C ) 52 . Changes in FC within the executive control network The results of repeated measures ANOVA showed significant interaction effects between groups and pre-post measurements in the FCs between the L-MFG, and R-MFG ( F = 21.066, p &lt; 0.001, η 2 = 0.263), and L-SFG ( F = 5.93, p = 0.039, η 2 = 0.091), the R-MFG to the R-SFG ( F = 7.102, p = 0.03, η 2 = 0.107), the L-mSFG and the L-IFG ( F = 6.331, p = 0.0225, η 2 = 0.097) and the R-OFC ( F = 10.535, p = 0.008, η 2 = 0.152) and bilateral postcentral gyrus (L: F = 9.114, p = 0.014, η 2 = 0.134; R: F = 9.748, p = 0.008, η 2 = 0.142) and left precentral gyrus ( F = 10.363, p = 0.005, η 2 = 0.149), the L-OFC and the R-IFG ( F = 5.548, p = 0.039, η 2 = 0.086) and the L-MFG ( F = 8.258, p = 0.018, η 2 = 0.123) and the L-mSFG ( F = 19.444, p &lt; 0.001, η 2 = 0.248) and bilateral precentral gyrus (L: F = 5.917, p = 0.023, η 2 = 0.091; R: F = 6.687, p = 0.012, η 2 = 0.102), the R-OFC and the L-MFG ( F = 21.952, p &lt; 0.001, η 2 = 0.271) and the R-OFC ( F = 6.818, p = 0.015, η 2 = 0.104) and the R-PCC ( F = 6.607, p = 0.033, η 2 = 0.101). Moreover, simple effect analysis showed that the above FCs in the MM group were significantly enhanced after an intervention compared with those observed before an intervention. Specific information is presented in Fig. 4E, F . Changes in FC within the frontostriatal pathway The results of repeated measures ANOVA show that both the group factor and pre-post measurement factor have significant interactive effects on the FC between the L-DS and the R-IFG ( F = 7.556, p = 0.008, η 2 = 0.114) and L-SFG ( F = 14.832, p &lt; 0.001, η 2 = 0.201), the L-MFG and the right parahippocampal gyrus (R-PHG) ( F = 12.14, p = 0.003, η 2 = 0.171), the L-mSFG and bilateral PHG (L: F = 8.604, p = 0.01, η 2 = 0.127; R: F = 7.88, p = 0.021, η 2 = 0.118), the L-OFC and the right substantia nigra reticulata ( F = 12.589, p = 0.002, η 2 = 0.176), as well as the R-OFC and the right insula ( F = 5.207, p = 0.039, η 2 = 0.081). Further simple effect analysis revealed that the FCs were significantly enhanced after and before intervention in the MM group, while the difference was not significant in the PMR group. The results of simple effect analysis are presented in Fig. 4G . Changes in FC between the executive control network and default model network The results of repeated measures ANOVA indicated that the effects of the interactions between group and pre-post measurement factors on FC were significant between the L-OFC and the left precuneus ( F = 11.638, p = 0.003, η 2 = 0.165), as well as between the R-OFC and bilateral precuneus (L: F = 10.237, p = 0.008, η 2 = 0.148; R: F = 9.276, p = 0.006, η 2 = 0.136). Further simple effect analysis revealed that the FC showed significant enhancement after and before an intervention in the MM group. These results are presented in Fig. 4G . Discussion Current studies have shown that the FCs between important nodes (mSFG, IFG, ACC, MFG, OFC) and related brain regions (postcentral gyrus) in the executive control network of IGD are abnormal. MFG, IFG, ACC, and OFC play an indispensable role in the executive control process 54 – 59 . The amygdala 60 and striatum (CAD, putamen, nucleus accumbens) 61 , 62 are important nodes in the reward network. In addition, the insula, which plays an important role in reward processing, is considered to play a key role in addiction 63 , 64 . The activation levels in each brain region were significantly decreased in the prefrontal cortex owing to excessive game playing in individuals with IGD 65 . IGD could reduce the FCs between the OFC and the whole brain 66 , and reduce the activation levels in the ACC, frontal lobe, and other brain regions 67 , leading to impaired executive function. Our previous study has found a significant reduction in FCs between the frontal lobe and striatum in individuals with IGD 12 . Previous studies have shown that IGD significantly reduces the FC between the OFC and amygdala and is negatively correlated with impulsivity 68 . This study found that abnormal connectivity in the executive control and reward-related brain regions in individuals with IGD could indicate impaired and disrupted executive control function and enhance individual impulsivity. Our results showed that MM significantly enhanced the FC between the R-MFG and the right postcentral gyrus, as well as between the L-OFC and the L-IFG in individuals with IGD. There was no significant difference in the FC between reward and executive control-related brain regions in individuals with IGD post-intervention. Although the postcentral gyrus did not directly participate in the execution control process, it is an important perception area that can process and integrate sensory information 69 . However, the execution function is necessary to make subsequent decisions based on important information generated via perception 70 . Previous discussions have shown that IGD significantly reduces FCs between the OFC and other brain regions. Previous studies have shown that impaired central posterior gyrus function can lead to individuals experiencing abnormalities in receiving, processing, and integrating information, and being unable to guide sustained behaviors, such as attention, reward, and regulation, which are ultimately associated with addiction 71 . Therefore, the results of this study indicated that MM could effectively improve executive control ability and restore brain functions, thereby reducing game cravings. This study found that MM can lead to significantly enhanced FCs between the MFG, mSFG, SFG, IFG, OFC, precentral gyrus, and postcentral gyrus in individuals with IGD, compared to those with PMR. The prefrontal cortex is considered an important node in the ECN 54 , playing a crucial role in numerous cognitive functions 72 . The SFG, as an important part of the prefrontal lobe, plays a crucial role in the executive control process 54 , 73 . The medial frontal gyrus and IFG are considered key areas for high-risk related selection 74 and top-down control 75 . Further research has shown that when IGD affected individuals focus on game rewards, the activation rates in the medial frontal lobe and IFG are decreased significantly 65 . In addition, IFG has been shown to play an important role in impulse suppression in IGD 76 . The OFC is considered an important brain region in addiction and has been proven to play a crucial role in cognitive functions such as executive control and decision-making 77 , 78 . Previous studies have shown that the OFC of IGD significantly reduces FC, along with other regions of the brain, leading to impaired executive control function and the inability to suppress game cravings 66 . Our previous research also indicates that the internal FC is significantly reduced in the ECN in individuals with IGD, which can lead to impaired executive control function 79 – 81 . Therefore, in this study, MM restored the executive control function and reduced game cravings by enhancing the internal FCs of the ECN. Compared with PMR, MM significantly enhanced the FCs between the prefrontal cortex (SFG, mSFG, MFG, IFG, OFC) and reward-related brain areas (DS, PHG, insula, SN_pr) in IGD. The striatum is strongly associated with impulsivity and reward systems, which partly explains why all games have rewards as outcomes 82 . Moreover, previous studies have found that IGD leads to a significantly reduced FC between the prefrontal cortex and striatum, which is closely related to the degree of addiction and craving, and leads to impaired executive control function and an inability to suppress cravings 12 , 83 , 84 . Previous studies have found that cravings are mainly affected by multiple regions in the frontostriatal pathway, including the striatum, OFC, PHG, and insula 85 , 86 . Our previous studies have suggested that bilateral PHG abnormalities are associated with addictive memory formation and that the reduced connectivity between the PHG and prefrontal lobe is considered the underlying mechanism of IGD 8 . Therefore, in this study, MM enhanced the FCs of brain regions related to the prefrontal and striatal pathways, to enhance executive control function and further reduce the extent of game cravings in individuals. The present study found that MM could significantly enhance the FC between the bilateral OFC and precuneus. The precuneus is an important node in the default mode network and is thought to be involved in many cognitive processes 87 , 88 . Our previous study found that the precuneus is sensitive to IGD severity, which negatively affects its connectivity with the prefrontal lobe and inability to control game cravings, contributing to persistently problematic gaming behaviors 7 . Craving is an important factor in the initiation and maintenance of addiction. DSM-5 defines craving as a strong desire to participate in addictive behaviors 1 , and craving is related to the severity of addiction 89 . In addition, craving is considered as an indicator of the presence and severity of behavioral addiction-related impairment in the diagnosis of addiction 90 . Therefore, in this study, MM enhanced the FC between the OFC and the precuneus, which in turn could restore impaired executive control ability and inhibit craving, thereby reducing the frequency of problematic gaming behavior. There are several limitations associated with this study. First, this study recruited only adults and not adolescents, who are relatively more susceptible to internet gaming addictions. Subsequent studies should expand the age range of participants. Second, only FC analysis was performed in this study, and the directed changes in connectivity between the two brain regions remained unknown. Subsequent studies should conduct effective connectivity analyses to explore the potential mechanisms further. Finally, in this study, only healthy individuals undergoing PMR interventions were included in the control group, and subsequent studies can use multiple intervention groups for comparison to identify groups exhibiting the optimal effects post-intervention. This study demonstrated the effectiveness of the MM intervention for IGD from several perspectives. The mechanism involves reshaping the FC within the ECN, between the frontostriatal pathway, and between the ECN and default model network, thereby restoring the individual’s top-down executive control ability and ultimately reducing game cravings. These results provide a solid foundation for further exploration of the mechanism by which the MM intervention can affect addiction and the intervention model for IGD.
