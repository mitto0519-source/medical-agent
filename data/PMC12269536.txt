ABSTRACT The sources and routes of HEV infections in industrialized countries are not fully defined. Occitanie in Southern France is an hyperendemic area but the relative extent of contaminated food and water consumption is unknown. We used validated assays to determine IgG and IgM seroprevalence among 3480 French blood donors living in Occitanie and compared the data with those obtained about twelve years ago. Epidemiological information was collected using a specific questionnaire. HEV IgG seroprevalence increased from 39.1% in 2011% to 47.8% in 2023. Unlike 12 years ago, the wastewater treatment method and tap water consumption were linked to HEV infection. Consumption of bottled water is now a risk factor for HEV infection. Overall IgM seroprevalence was 1.3%. The high prevalence of anti‐HEV IgG in Occitanie cannot be explained by eating habits alone. Water consumption and treatment also appear to be important factors of HEV infections in Southern France. epidemiology HEV risk factors seroprevalence water Multivariate analysis identified several characteristics that were independently associated with the presence of anti‐HEV IgG (Table 2 ). After adjustments on age, gender and administrative area, the dietary risk factor for a past HEV infection was the consumption of raw pork liver sausages. This type of sausage contains at least 30% pig liver and is eaten uncooked after traditional drying. What was more surprising was that drinking bottled or tap water was associated with a higher rate of anti‐HEV IgG. It was also found that the use of septic tanks to treat wastewater was associated with a higher HEV seroprevalence. We constructed a directed acyclic graph using the variables included in the initial multivariate analysis and found a direct link between the HEV IgG seroprevalence and the use of septik tank, the consumption of raw liver sausages, the consumption of tap or bottled water (Figure 2A ). The consumption of raw liver sausages influenced both the location of the donors (department), the IgG seroprevalence and the gender (more male consumption). This therefore implied that the consumption of raw liver sausages is a confounding factor for the link between sex and HEV IgG seroprevalence and department and HEV IgG seroprevalence. This explains that after adjustment for the consumption of raw liver sausages the link between IgG seroprevalence and gender on the one hand and IgG seroprevalence and male gender on the other hand have disappeared (Table 2 final mulltivariate analysis). 3.3 Increased HEV IgG Seroprevalence HEV IgG seroprevalence in Occitanie significantly increased from 39.1% in 2011 [ 9 ] to 47.8% in 2023 ( p &lt; 0.01). HEV seroprevalence remained stable in 8 of the 13 administrative areas of Occitanie but increased in the other five. HEV seroprevalence significantly increased in Gard (22.5% in 2011 vs. 33.7% in 2023, p &lt; 0.01), Haute‐Garonne (46.3% in 2011 vs. 61.3% in 2023, p &lt; 0.01), Lot (23.6% in 2011 vs. 41.1% in 2023, p &lt; 0.01), Tarn (36.5% in 2011 vs. 54.7% in 2023, p &lt; 0.01) and Tarn‐et‐Garonne (44.6% in 2011 vs. 60% in 2023, p &lt; 0.01) (Figure 1 ). 3.4 Factors Associated With an Increase in HEV IgG Seroprevalence To explore the factors associated with the increase in seroprevalence in five administrative areas (Gard, Haute‐Garonne, Lot, Tarn and Tarn‐et‐Garonne), we analyzed the food and water consumption variables that changed between 2011 and 2023 according to the administrative area (those with increased seroprevalence and those with stable seroprevalence). We used two multivariate models (Table 3 ) which showed that in administrative areas where HEV IgG seroprevalence remained stable, only the consumption of foods that were not associated with HEV seroprevalence varied (Table 3A ). On the other hand, in the administrative areas with an increase in HEV seroprevalence between 2011 and 2023, we observed an increase in the consumption of deer and wild boars, and also of bottled water. Very interestingly, we also observed an increase in wastewater treatment by septic tanks (Table 3B ). We constructed a directed acyclic graph using the variables included in the initial multivariate analysis and found a link between the increase of HEV IgG seroprevalence and the use of septik tank, the consumption of boar and the consumption of bottled water (Figure 2B ). The consumption of boars is a confounding factor for the link between the increase in IgG seroprevalence and the consumption of tap water, which explains why we did not find a link between increase in IgG seroprevalence and tap water in the final multivariate model after adjustment for wild boar meat consumption. The consumption of roe deer is linked to the use of a septic tank but not directly to the increase in seroprevalence, it is therefore the use of a septic tank which could lead to an increase in HEV IgG seroprevalence. 4 Discussion This serologic survey showed that the prevalence of anti‐HEV IgG among French blood donors in Occitanie increased from 39.1% in 2011 to 47.8% in 2023. The HEV IgG seroprevalence in 2023 was linked to the consumption of tap water, but also bottled water and the use of septic tanks to treat wastewater. In administrative areas with an increase in HEV seroprevalence, HEV IgG seroprevalence was linked to the consumption of deer, wild boars, bottled water and the use of septic tanks to treat wastewater. A meta‐analysis showed that HEV IgG seroprevalence in the human population varied according to the assays [ 17 ]. The observed heterogeneity was attributed to the assay employed (23%), the geographical region (23%) and the study cohort (7%). Based on the Wantai assay which is the most commonly used immunoassay and which has excellent sensitivity and specificity as previously reported [ 18 ], the IgG seroprevalence in Occitanie after age‐standardization is very close to the IgG seroprevalence noted in hyperendemic low‐income countries such as Bangladesh (50%) and Nepal (47%) [ 19 ]. In Europe, seroprevalence rates using the Wantai assay range from less than 5% to 44% at the country level [ 7 ]. The highest seroprevalence rate was reported in blood donors in Poland [ 20 ]. Germany, the Netherlands, France and Switzerland have an HEV IgG seroprevalence of 20% to 30% [ 8 , 17 , 21 , 22 ] while seroprevalence rates are lower than 20% in Spain, Italy, Denmark, the United Kingdom and Norway [ 23 , 24 , 25 , 26 , 27 ]. Interestingly, hot spots of HEV IgG seroprevalences have been reported in specific areas such as Central Italy [ 28 ], Northern Italy [ 7 , 29 ], Southern France [ 9 ] and West Central Poland [ 30 ]. In all of these studies, as in Occitanie both in 2011 and 2023, HEV IgG seroprevalence increases with age, which indicates cumulative exposure to HEV. In the present study, although we recorded higher rates in men (52.2%) vs. women (42.5%), the difference was not statistically significant in the multivariate analysis. This is in line with previous studies performed in French blood donors living in the hyperendemic regions in southern France [ 31 ] and in other industrialized countries [ 19 , 32 , 33 ]. Overall, these results could suggest that exposure to HEV is not directly related to gender but rather to individual behavior such as differences in dietary habits. We found that HEV infection was linked to the consumption of liver sausages, as was the case in 2011 [ 9 ]. However, other dietary risk factors that were identified in 2011, such as offal and mussels, were no longer found to be consumption risk factors. On the other hand, we identified three variables linked to water that are risk factors for HEV infection: the consumption of tap water, the consumption of bottled water, and the wastewater treatment method (septic tank vs. centralized sanitation). These findings were supported by stratified analysis according to whether the administrative area was affected by an increase in HEV IgG seroprevalence between 2011 and 2023. In these areas, the use of septic tanks as a wastewater treatment method as well as the consumption of bottled water and tap water had increased. The use of a septic tank is restricted to the household that owns it, but the tank sanitation process is still carried out by companies who move from one tank to another during the same day. The HEV can pass from one pit to another via this process. It can then spread into the environment via incidents in the pits such as infiltration through the ground or the rise of sludge. In high income countries, HEV‐3 zoonotic transmission from animal reservoirs such as pigs, wild boars and deer is generally acknowledged [ 34 ]. In these countries, HEV is primarily acquired through porcine routes, either through the consumption of liver products or through direct contact with infected animals [ 8 , 17 , 35 ]. However, while waterborne routes of HEV transmission are rarely documented in developed countries, they cannot be excluded. The identification of seven cases of hepatitis E virus infection in a rural French hamlet in April 2015 led to investigations which confirmed clustering and identified waterborne infections due to a fault in the water supply to the hamlet [ 36 ]. This small outbreak was only identified because there were a few symptomatic infections, but in the vast majority of cases HEV infections are asymptomatic and remain undiagnosed [ 6 ]. HEV‐3 has been found in untreated and treated wastewater, drinking water, surface water, groundwater and other aquatic environments in a number of countries including European countries where the prevalence has been estimated at 12.2% [ 37 , 38 ]. However, most studies have not determined whether the detected nucleic acids are associated with complete infectious particles. Exposure to HEV‐contaminated water may therefore be riskier in some areas of high‐income countries than it was initially thought. As is the case in low‐income countries, exposure to contaminated water may be a major risk factor in HEV epidemiology. The study of French blood donors in 2011 found that drinking bottled water was associated with a lower frequency of anti‐HEV IgG [ 8 ]. This has now been contradicted by our study which suggests that drinking bottled water has become a risk factor for HEV infection, as has drinking tap water. This could be explained by a variation in collection and/or control methods. At the end of April 2024, a multinational company was forced to destroy two million bottles after bacterial contamination of fecal origin [ 39 ]. This had also been highlighted in a report by the National Health Security Agency for food, the environment and work, which confirmed the widespread fecal pathogen contamination of certain natural mineral springs operated by this group in France. However, the presence of enteric viruses was not analyzed. A reinforcement of water quality controls of both bottle and tap water should be implemented in France. Current HEV transmission dynamics is indicated by the prevalence of anti‐HEV IgM. The 1.3% prevalence of anti‐HEV IgM in 2023 using the Wantai automated test is in agreement with the high frequency of HEV RNA detection in blood donors from Occitanie since the introduction of systematic screening in France (2.3/1000) [ 40 ] and the high frequency of HEV RNA detection in a wastewater treatment plant from Occitanie [ 41 ]. A strength of our study lies in the longitudinal monitoring of factors associated with HEV seroprevalence in the same type of population sampled 12 years apart and interviewed with the same questionnaire. The sample size is large and the populations of donors were comparable. Another strength of our study lies in the use of two different statistical methods, a multivariate log‐binomial regression model and directed acyclic graphs. The two methods converge in their results. However, there are several limitations. First, no direct analysis was performed of the virus in the environment or in drinking water and the sources of bottled water that were consumed were not investigated. We plan to analyse surface water and groundwater samples in Occitanie areas with high and low IgG seroprevalence. These samples as well as tap and bottled drinking water will be tested for HEV RNA using standardized methods [ 41 , 42 , 43 ]. Second, the infectivity of the virus in water was not determined and the infectious dose is still unknown. Third, the formulation used in the questionnaire such as one per week or month or year or never created confusion among many blood donors and non exclusive responses were obtained. This is why we chose to reclassify the responses as 0/1. In addition, socioeconomic status, access to heathcare, and urban versus rural living conditions were not included in the questionnaire. These factors could influence both dietary habits and water consumption patterns. Lastly, HEV IgG seroprevalence trends were probably influenced by environmental, behavioral, or public health changes. However, these factors were not analysed in the present study. In conclusion, our study demonstrates that HEV IgG seroprevalence has increased significantly in 12 years, even in an already hyperendemic region of France. Although dietary habits play a major role in the transmission of HEV, contaminated water seems to be an important factor in the epidemiology of HEV infections in this French region. Our data underscore the importance of environmental surveillance in implementing effective measures to limit HEV transmission by water which must be considered to be as high‐risk as contaminated food products.
