Abstract Background Biallelic PLA2G6 mutations are associated with early onset autosomal recessive parkinsonism, exhibiting a broad spectrum of clinical heterogeneity. Objective To comprehensively characterise the clinical, imaging and genetic features of PLA2G6 -related parkinsonism. Methods We report 14 new cases of PLA2G6 -related parkinsonism in Southwest China and conduct a systematic literature review. Results Among the 14 patients in our cohort, 16 PLA2G6 variants were identified, including seven novel and nine previously reported variants. The mean age at symptom onset was 26.50±6.57 years. The most common initial presentation was parkinsonism (9/14, 64.3%), followed by gait disturbance (6/14, 42.9%) and psychiatric symptoms (1/14, 7.1%). A literature review identified 118 patients with PLA2G6 -related parkinsonism, with a mean age at onset of 24.53±8.84 years. The most common initial clinical features included parkinsonism (61/117, 52.1%), cerebellar signs (46/85, 54.1%), cognitive impairment (65/92, 70.7%) and psychiatric symptoms (80/93, 86.0%). Subgroup analysis showed that the mean age at symptom onset was older in Chinese patients (26.65±7.08 years) compared with those of European ancestry (20.83±9.79 years) (p=0.016). Additionally, patients of European ancestry showed delayed parkinsonism 5.35±8.14 years after onset. Iron deposition was reported more frequently in patients of European ancestry (10/16, 62.5%) than that in Chinese patients (6/37, 16.2%) (p=0.0002). WHAT THIS STUDY ADDS We report the largest cohort of patients with PLA2G6 -related parkinsonism in China, identifying seven novel variants. Through a comprehensive literature review, we revealed the phenotypic and genotypic spectrum of PLA2G6 -related parkinsonism, finding that Chinese patients predominantly present with early parkinsonism, whereas patients of European ancestry more frequently exhibit non-parkinsonian symptoms at onset and a higher prevalence of brain iron deposition. HOW THIS STUDY MIGHT AFFECT RESEARCH, PRACTICE OR POLICY This study provides more insight into the broad clinical spectrum on patients with PLA2G6 -related parkinsonism, which may help the earlier and more accurate diagnosis of PLA2G6 -related parkinsonism and guide clinicians in tailoring treatment plans to individual patients. Introduction PLA2G6 -associated neurodegeneration is a neurodegenerative disorder with great clinical heterogeneity. 1 The PLA2G6 gene encodes the enzyme calcium-independent phospholipase A2 beta, which is critical for maintaining cell membrane integrity, regulating mitochondrial function, modulating fatty acid oxidation and mediating calcium signalling. 2 3 The associated phenotype of PLA2G6 could be classified into three types according to the age of disease onset, all autosomal recessive in inheritance pattern, including infantile neuroaxonal dystrophy, characterised by psychomotor regression or delay between 0.5 and 3 years of age, 4 atypical neuroaxonal dystrophy with prominent language difficulty and autistic-like features between 1.5 and 6.5 years of age, 5 also known as neurodegeneration with brain iron accumulation type IIB, and autosomal recessive early onset dystonia-parkinsonism manifesting onset in the second to third decades of life. 6 Since the first case reported from Indian ethnicity with recessive dystonia-parkinsonism caused by the PLA2G6 mutation, 7 more studies suggest that PLA2G6 -related parkinsonism is complex and heterogeneous on clinical characteristics and imaging findings. 8 10 The prototypical manifestation of early onset PLA2G6- associated dystonia-parkinsonism encompasses a spectrum of movement disorders, including parkinsonism, dystonia, cerebellar ataxia and manifestations of psychiatric or cognitive decline. Brain MRI findings have revealed that the majority of patients have cerebellar atrophy and iron deposition in the globus pallidus internus (GPi) and substantia nigra (STN). 10 Considering its wide phenotypic spectrum, even patient with the same variant in the same pedigree present with varied phenotypes. In this study, we report 14 unpublished patients with PLA2G6 -associated parkinsonism identified at our centre. Furthermore, we perform a comprehensive literature review on PLA2G6 -related parkinsonism, including clinical manifestations, imaging characteristics, genetics, response to therapies and ethnic differences. Methods Patients and assessment Fourteen unpublished cases of PLA2G6 -associated parkinsonism identified by clinical symptoms and genetic analysis came from the Department of Neurology, West China Hospital. The demographic data, clinical data and neurological examination findings were collected. Motor assessments were assessed by the Unified PD Rating Scale (UPDRS) part III 11 and the Hoehn and Yahr (H&amp;Y) stage, 12 which was conducted without withdrawing any antiparkinsonian medications (either being drug ‘ON’ or ‘OFF’). The burden of non-motor symptoms was assessed using the Non-Motor Symptoms Scale. 13 Frontal Assessment Battery (FAB) 14 and Montreal Cognitive Assessment (MoCA) 15 were used to assess the frontal lobe and cognition function of patients when the scores of FAB ≤14 or MoCA ≤24 were considered to be cognitive impairment. 16 17 Hamilton Depression Scale (HAMD, 24 items), 18 Hamilton Anxiety Scale (HAMA) 19 and Beck Depression Inventory (BDI) 20 were used to assess patients’ psychiatric symptoms. Anxiety or depression was defined as the score of HAMD/HAMA ≥7 or BDI ≥5. The quality of life and sleep problems were assessed using the 39-item Parkinson’s Disease Questionnaire (PDQ-39) 21 and Parkinson's disease sleep scale—validation of the revised version (PDSS‐2). 22 Genetic analysis Genomic DNA was collected from peripheral blood leucocytes using a standard phenol-chloroform procedure. Genetic analysis was conducted using whole-exome sequencing (WES) and validated by Sanger sequencing. The reference sequence is PLA2G6 ( NM_003560.4 ). LoF variants were defined as frameshift, stop-gain and splicing variants, 23 while rare missense variants were defined as variants with a median allele frequency &lt;0.1% in the Exome Aggregation Consortium-East Asian and Genome Aggregation Database-East Asian. Bioinformatics tools were used to predict the pathogenicity of the identified variants including PolyPhen-2, 24 MutationTaster, 25 Sorting Intolerant From Tolerant (SIFT) 26 and Protein Variation Effect Analyzer. 27 Combined Annotation Dependent Depletion 28 and Mutation Assessor were also used. 29 Mutations were finally classified according to the American College of Medical Genetics and Genomics guidelines. 30 Search of the literature Then, we conducted a comprehensive and systematic search on PubMed and China National Knowledge Infrastructure (CNKI) online databases until April 2023. All the cases carrying biallelic PLA2G6 mutations with individual information were included to analyse. We collected sex, ethnicity, symptom(s) at onset, age at parkinsonism onset, psychiatric and cerebellar symptoms/signs, findings from the laboratory, neuroimaging, family history, genotype and treatment response. We also performed subgroup analyses between Chinese patients and patients of European ancestry to explore the ethnic differences. Statistical analysis The continuous variable was presented as mean±SD. To compare two groups of continuous variables, a two-sample t-test or Welch’s t-test was used when the data were in accordance with normal distribution; otherwise, Mann-Whitney U was used. For the (multivariate) frequency distribution of the variables, the Fisher’s exact test was used. The statistical software was GraphPad Prism V.6.0. All statistics were two-tailed and significance was set at p&lt;0.05. Results Cases of PLA2G6 -related parkinsonism in our centr Demographic and clinical characteristics The demographic and clinical data of 14 patients with PLA2G6 -associated dystonia-parkinsonism from our centre are shown in online supplemental table 1 . The mean age at symptom onset was 26.50±6.57 years, ranging from 16 to 36 years. Initial manifestations included parkinsonism (9/14, 64.29%), gait disturbance (6/14, 42.86%) and psychiatric problems (1/14, 7.14%). Patients who presented with gait and psychiatric problems at onset subsequently developed parkinsonism. Bradykinesia and rigidity were reported in all cases (14/14), and 8/14 (57.1%) patients had resting tremor affecting the upper limbs. Dystonia, predominantly of the extremities, was documented in 6/14 (42.8%) of cases. All patients showed gait disturbance during the disease, of which three cases (cases 1, 4 and 14) showed ataxic gait and others showed parkinsonism-associated gait disturbance (foot dragging and festination). The mean UPDRS part III score was 34.43±16.19, and the mean H&amp;Y stage was 2.18±1.03. Cerebellar signs were observed in 8/14 (57.1%) patients, including ataxic gait, abnormal finger-nose-finger test and clumsy coordination. Dysarthria was present in 10/14 (71.4%) of the patients, with three individuals (cases 1, 3 and 4) developing severe dysarthria and choking during follow-up. Notably, dysarthria was the initial presenting symptom in four patients (cases 1, 3, 11 and 12). Anosmia/Hyposmia was observed in 2/14 (14.2%) patients. Sleep disturbances, including fragmented sleep, vivid dreams and insomnia, were reported by 9/14 (64.2%) of the cohort, with a mean PDSS-2 score of 10.69±7.78. Autonomic dysfunction, manifested as urinary frequency and constipation, was noted in 9/14 (64.2%) patients. Orthostatic hypotension and sexual dysfunction were not reported. The mean score of the non-motor scale was 22.64±18.71 points. Cognitive deficits were reported in 9/14 (64.2%) patients during the early course of the disease. The mean MoCA and FAB scale scores were 24.00±3.11 and 15.92±2.10, respectively, at the first assessment. Case 3 (no. 2572) showed a decrease from 27 to 19 on the MoCA within 2 years of follow-up. Psychiatric comorbidity was prevalent in 10/14 (71.4%) patients, mainly characterised by depression and anxiety. In addition, other affective symptoms were noted in individual cases: case 4 (no. 10128) showed irritability and case 6 (no. 6485) showed apathy. The mean scores on the HAMD, BDI and HAMA were 12.15±8.76, 8.14±5.71 and 11.50±12.34, respectively. The PDQ-39 was used to assess the quality of life and gave a mean score of 40.00±31.77, suggesting considerable variability between patients. Two cases (cases 1 and 12) reported poor sports performance and grades with poor fine motor skills ever since childhood, whereas the developmental milestones of other patients were unremarkable. Treatment is aimed at relieving symptoms and preventing complications. 31 In patients with PLA2G6 -associated dystonia-parkinsonism, dopaminergic agents are usually prescribed to alleviate the parkinsonism and dystonia. In 13 patients, motor symptoms improved significantly in the early phase of treatment. However, levodopa-induced complications, including dyskinesia, motor fluctuations and the wearing-off phenomenon, were reported in 9/13 (69.23%) (cases 1, 2, 3, 4, 6, 7, 8, 10 and 14) after an average treatment duration of 2.61±2.23 years, even at levodopa doses of 300 mg/day or less. Amantadine was administered in three cases (cases 2, 3 and 6) to control dyskinesia, but the response was mild. Two patients (cases 4, 5) received bilateral deep brain stimulation (DBS) of the GPi with a good response. At follow-up, four individuals were able to walk independently at a mean of 5.0±2.16 years from disease onset (range 3–14 years). In contrast, one patient (case 1) became wheelchair dependent after 3 years, another (case 3) succumbed to sepsis 4 years after onset and two patients (cases 7 and 9) discontinued participation in the study. Imaging characteristic Brain MRI was available for 11 patients, with longitudinal follow-up imaging available for two individuals (cases 1 (figure 1A, B) and 3 ( figure 1C,D )) over a period of 4 years. In case 3, susceptibility weighted imaging (SWI) sequences showed mild iron deposition (as shown in figure 1C,D ). Cerebral atrophy was observed in both cases 1 and 3 ( figure 1 ). The most striking sign in our centre was cerebellar atrophy affecting the cerebellar hemisphere (figure 1A, B). Cerebellar atrophy was found in all nine patients at the initial examination. In two cases where follow-up MRI was available, generalised cerebral atrophy, particularly of the frontotemporal lobe, and progressive cerebellar atrophy were seen ( figure 1 ). However, iron deposition in case 3 did not change significantly after 4 years. Genetic characteristic Among 14 patients identified from our centre, 10 patients from different pedigrees did not report a positive family history and four patients (cases 4 and 5 and cases 11 and 12) from the same pedigrees reported parental consanguinity ( figure 2 ). WES analysis revealed that 12 patients carried compound heterozygotes and two patients carried homozygotes ( online supplemental table 1 ). These 14 cases carried 16 different variants, including seven novel variants, c.1463T&gt;C(p.Val488Ala), c.691G&gt;C(p.Gly231Arg), c.2277-1G&gt;C, c.278C&gt;G(p.Pro93Arg), c.877T&gt;G(p.Trp293Gly), c.1961G&gt;T(p.Gly654Val), chr22:38503814-38512292 del first reported in this study. Of the 16 variants, 12 of which were missense mutations, two frameshift mutations, one splicing mutation and one transcript end. All variants identified in the study were either absent or extremely rare (&lt;0.0001) in public genetic databases. Prediction tools including PROVEAN, SIFT, PolyPhen-2 and MutationTaster predicted the majority of the variants to be deleterious (10/15), damaging (10/15) or disease-causing (15/15) ( online supplemental table 2 ). Literature review on PLA2G6 -related parkinsonism To comprehensively review PLA2G6 -related parkinsonism, we searched the PubMed and CNKI databases for all available English and Chinese literature. A total of 118 patients (including 14 patients from our centre) with PLA2G6 -related parkinsonism from 51 studies were identified ( table 1 ), including 40 Chinese (including Taiwanese), 17 Indian, 16 Japanese, seven Pakistani, six Korean, five Iranian, four Saudi, one Turkish, one Kuwaiti, 18 patients of European ancestry and three unspecified Caucasian patients. 7 932 Clinical manifestations The cohort included 61 female patients. Among the 112 patients with available data, the mean age at symptom onset was 24.53±8.84 years. Parkinsonism (bradykinesia, tremor, foot drag) was the most common presentation among the patients, reported in 61/117 (52.14%). Mean disease duration at last assessment was 7.94±7.06 years. Parkinsonism (61/117, 52.14%), psychiatric problems (24/117, 20.51%), gait or balance problems (23/117, 19.66%) and cognitive issues (5/117, 4.27%) were the most common manifestations at onset. The mean age of parkinsonism, either isolated or combined with other neurological/psychiatric features, started at 25.95±6.88 years and delayed parkinsonism after 1.87±4.46 years onset. Dystonia was presented in 69/97 (71.13%) cases. When specified, dystonia was reported to affect the limbs only (26 cases) or to be generalised (15 cases). Pyramidal signs were detected in 57/75 (76.00%) subjects. Reduced muscle strength was mentioned in five cases (5/40, 12.50%). Cerebellar signs were observed in 54.12% cases, including mild dysmetria, gait ataxia and gaze-evoked nystagmus. Gait abnormalities were presented early in the disease course, reported in 85/85 (100%). When details were available, gait problems were described as having parkinsonian (31 cases), ataxic (18 cases), pyramidal (11 cases), dystonic (six cases) features, either alone or in combination. Freezing of gait was reported in 12 patients. Postural impairment was consistently observed in 69/74 patients, leading to loss of independent walking at the mean age of 26.97±6.44 years. Cognitive impairment was documented early in the disease course in 65/92 (70.65%) patients. Psychiatric issues were presented in 80/93 (86.02%) cases, including depression in 47/58 (81.03%), anxiety in 34/44 (77.27%) and signs of frontal lobe impairment in 44/58 (75.86%) patients. Hallucinations were reported in 17/40 (42.50%) cases. Delusions were presented in 11/32 (34.38%) cases. Sleep disturbances, including sleep fragmentation and acting out dreams, were mentioned in 18/33 (54.55%) patients. Autonomic symptoms were also common in patients, with symptoms of bladder overactivity and/or urinary incontinence reported in 37/54 (68.52%) cases and constipation in 14/30 (46.67%) cases. Orthostatic hypotension was mentioned in two cases (10.53%). Eye movement abnormalities were reported in 29/59 (49.15%) cases, most commonly presenting as fragmented pursuit and/or a reduced vertical gaze range in the vertical plane. Dysarthria was described in 46/53 (86.79%) cases. Swallowing difficulties were reported in 18/33 (54.55%) cases. Imaging characteristics Brain MRI was available in 111/118 (94.07%) patients. Cerebral atrophy was reported in 30/90 (33.33%) cases, characterised as mild to moderate in severity and either generalised or predominantly involving the frontotemporal lobes. Mild to marked cerebellar atrophy affecting the vermis and/or the hemispheres was observed in 53/109 (48.62%) cases. Iron deposition was reported in 29/109 (26.61%) cases, primarily localising in the globus pallidus, substantia nigra and putamen. White matter T2 hyperintensities were reported in five cases. Genetics Positive family history was reported in 45/90 (50.00%) cases. Parental consanguinity was reported in 31/80 (38.75%) cases. Overall, 52/118 (44.07%) patients carried homozygous variants in PLA2G6 , whereas segregation analysis revealed that 66/118 (55.93%) were compound heterozygotes. The number of PLA2G6 -related variants was reported to be 84. Missense variants were predominant in PLA2G6 -related parkinsonism. Most PLA2G6 mutations were presented in only one pedigree. The most frequent variant was c.2222G&gt;A. Response to therapies Parkinsonism responded to levodopa in 98/107 (91.59%) cases. Levodopa-induced dyskinesias were reported in 67/82 (81.71%) cases and appeared within the first year of treatment in most cases, even with low levodopa doses (≤300 mg/day). Two patients (cases 4 and 5) with PLA2G6 variants received GPi-DBS at our centre with a good response to dyskinesia and motor symptoms. To date, eight patients have been reported to have undergone DBS surgery, including four patients who underwent bilateral DBS of the STN with excellent outcome, 4042 44 and three patients who underwent bilateral DBS of the GPi with improvement of motor symptoms. 10 45 Ethnic differences Subgroup analyses between Chinese patients (n=40) and patients of European ancestry (n=18) were performed to investigate ethnic differences ( table 1 ). The age at symptom onset of Chinese patients (26.65±7.08) was older than that of European patients (20.83±9.79) (p=0.016). Comparison of age at symptom onset by age group is shown in figure 3A . Meanwhile, symptoms at onset were quite different between the two subgroups ( figure 3B ). The most common manifestation at onset was parkinsonism (including bradykinesia, tremor and foot drag) reported in 26/40 (65.00%) Chinese cohort, whereas psychiatric problems (including depression, delusions and aggressive behaviour) were reported as the most common onset symptom in the European cohort (7/18, 38.89%). Besides, we noticed that patients of European ancestry showed delayed parkinsonism 5.35±8.14 years after onset. Iron deposition on T2/SWI sequences was reported more frequently in patients of European ancestry (10/16, 62.50%) than in 6/37 Chinese patients (16.22%) (p=0.0002). The more frequent variant was c.991G&gt;T in Chinese patients, while c.238G&gt;A in patients of European ancestry. Detailed ethnic differences on clinical manifestations, imaging characteristics and genetics are provided in online supplemental table 3 . Discussion In the present study, we reported 14 unpublished patients with PLA2G6 -associated dystonia-parkinsonism in Southwest China and summarised the clinical, imaging and genetic characteristics of these 14 patients. Moreover, by searching online databases including English and Chinese literature and merging data of patients from our centre, a total of 118 PLA2G6 -associated parkinsonism patients were identified. We then performed a comprehensive literature review on PLA2G6 -related parkinsonism and conducted the subgroup analyses to explore the ethnic differences. From our centre, 14 cases manifested onset symptoms between the late second to fourth decades of life. All cases presented motor symptoms at onset except for case 6 and case 12, which showed personality/behaviour changes with the youngest age of onset. Psychiatric features were frequently observed early in the disease course, presenting anxiety, depression and irritability of patients at our centre. Cognitive dysfunction might be one of the non-motor symptoms of parkinsonism because the caregivers of cases 1, 4 and 6 reported the cognitive impairment fluctuated with rigidity, tremor and levodopa-induced dyskinesia. All patients showed gait disturbance, including ataxia, festination and foot-dragging. Dysarthria was also frequently observed early in the disease course, even showing as the first symptom (cases 1, 3, 11 and 12). Consistent with a previous study, 10 patients from our centre also showed excellent initial responses to levodopa; however, early onset levodopa-induced dyskinesia, motor fluctuations and the wearing-off phenomenon were reported after 2 years of dopaminergic treatment. It is the levodopa-induced dyskinesia that interfered severely with patients’ quality of life and aggravated psychiatric and cognitive issues. Two patients (cases 4 and 5) with PLA2G6 variants received GPi-DBS from our centre with a good response to dyskinesia and motor symptoms. So far, it is reported that eight patients underwent DBS operation, among which four patients underwent bilateral DBS of the subthalamic nucleus (STN) with excellent outcome, 4042 44 and three patients underwent bilateral DBS of the GPi with improvement of motor symptoms. 10 45 More studies are needed to validate whether DBS is a potential option for PLA2G6 -associated parkinsonism. Cerebellar atrophy was detected on MRI in all cases at the initial assessment. In the three cases (cases 1, 3 and 12) with follow-up MRI available, progressive cerebellar and cerebral atrophy was observed. In addition, genetic ablation of PLA2G6 in mice resulted in cerebellar atrophy, characterised by loss of Purkinje cells, reactive astrogliosis, microglial activation and upregulation of proinflammatory cytokines. 46 Magrinelli et al suggested that cerebellar atrophy may reflect higher residual iPLA2β activity and could also correlate with disease duration. 10 Mild iron deposition was detected in two cases (cases 1 and 3). By Comparison of MRI from the initial assessment and follow-up 4 years later from case 3 showed no evident changes in iron deposition, while progressive cerebral and cerebellar atrophy was observed. Among the 14 patients identified from our centre, 12 carried compound heterozygous variants and two carried homozygous variants. We reported 16 distinct variants and seven novel variants, including five missense mutations, one splice_acceptor_variant and one transcript end (c.1463T&gt;C, p.Val488Ala; c.691G&gt;C, p.Gly231Arg; c.2277-1G&gt;C; c.278C&gt;G, p.Pro93Arg; c.877T&gt;G, p.Trp293Gly; c.1961G&gt;T, p.Gly654Val; chr22:38503814-38512292 del). All variants identified here were rare and predicted to have strong pathogenicity, or resulted in deleterious amino acid changes, based on the public gene database. Missense variant c.691G&gt;C(p.Gly231ArgI) was documented in an early ataxia and subsequent parkinsonism case 47 and also in a Caucasian female child with ataxia at 36 months of onset 31 ; c.481C&gt;T(p.Arg161Cys) was reported in two PLA2G6 -associated reviews 48 49 ; c.1945C&gt;T(p.Arg649Cys) was also previously reported in the review 49 ; c.1898C&gt;T(p.Ala633Val) was reported in a German parkinsonism case 10 and c.991G&gt;T(p.Asp331Tyr) was documented in many Chinese parkinsonism cases. 6 8 33 36 50 Additionally, we compared the characteristics of the two groups of patients with homozygous and compound heterozygous mutations from our centre. We found that patients with compound heterozygous mutations had an earlier age of disease onset but higher quality of life assessed by the PDQ-39 scale ( online supplemental table 4 ). Due to the small number of patients in our centre, a larger sample size is needed to prove this. Furthermore, by combining data from our series with previous studies, we conducted a comprehensive literature review on PLA2G6 -related parkinsonism and identified ethnic differences between Chinese patients and those of European ancestry through subgroup analyses. We reported several phenotypic and genotypic heterogeneities in clinical features, imaging findings and genetic information between Chinese patients and those of European ancestry. Patients of European ancestry with PLA2G6 mutations had disease onset approximately 5 years earlier than Chinese patients and predominantly presented with non-parkinsonian symptoms, mainly psychiatric manifestations and cognitive decline. And they developed parkinsonism including rigidity, tremor 5 years after onset. Interestingly, the age of onset of parkinsonism was similar between these two populations. Given the predominance of non-parkinsonian symptoms, particularly psychiatric manifestations, observed in European patients with PLA2G6 mutations, clinicians should be cautious not to misdiagnose these symptoms as primary psychiatric disorders and initiate potentially harmful treatments, such as antipsychotics, especially in the presence of extrapyramidal features. Comprehensive genetic testing and a detailed collection of family history should be undertaken before initiating clinical pharmacological interventions. As patients with younger onset often present with gait abnormalities characteristic of ataxia, it is important to distinguish these presentations from spinocerebellar ataxia. In patients presenting with parkinsonism as the first symptom, genetic testing is recommended to confirm the diagnosis. After diagnosis, it is important to maintain stable blood dopamine levels throughout treatment. Although no established guidelines or consensus exist for managing severe dyskinesia or diminishing medication response in patients with PLA2G6 mutations, cases from our centre and previous studies have reported symptomatic improvement following GPi-DBS. This intervention may represent a potential therapeutic option for these patients. However, further studies are required to substantiate its efficacy. According to current follow-up data, cognitive decline and cerebellar signs were found more frequently in patients of European ancestry. Longer follow-up was needed to find out whether Chinese patients develop non-motor symptoms in the advanced stage of the disease. Iron deposition on T2/SWI sequences was more frequent in patients of European ancestry than that in Chinese patients. Therefore, in patients with early onset PD, more sophisticated imaging should be recommended to detect positive imaging symptoms. The c.991G&gt;T(p.D331Y) mutation was almost exclusively found in Chinese patients, suggesting a common founder effect. Patients with homozygous p.D331Y mutations exhibit levodopa-responsive, early onset parkinsonism. 6 In vitro experiments have shown that the p.D331Y mutation results in reduced enzyme activity. 8 The heterozygous allele c.2222G&gt;A(p.Arg741Gln) has been observed in 12 out of 30 610 individuals (0.0005%) in the South Asian population (India, Pakistan and Saudi Arabia), but it is not detected in most other populations. 51 This variant is the most frequent mutation found in Indian cases of PLA2G6 -related parkinsonism, suggesting possible geographical clustering. The observed genotype-phenotype heterogeneity between Chinese and European populations may be influenced by differences in the genetic architecture, such as variations in mutation frequencies or allelic distributions of PLA2G6 mutations. Additionally, modifier genes prevalent in specific populations may alter the expression or penetrance of these mutations, contributing to phenotypic variability. Environmental factors, including dietary habits, toxin exposure or epigenetic modifications, could also play a role in modulating the clinical manifestations. 2 52 Further studies are needed to elucidate the complex interplay between genetic and environmental factors underlying these differences. This study has several limitations. First, the retrospective review of medical histories may have introduced reporting biases, such as underestimation or overestimation of symptoms by patients or caregivers. Additionally, the findings are partly based on limited follow-up data, and longer, more comprehensive follow-up is needed to fully understand the progression of PLA2G6 -associated parkinsonism. Second, all the unpublished cases were from Southwest China Hospital, while it was already the largest reported cohort of PLA2G6 -associated parkinsonism so far. Third, the biological effects of the identified variants were not experimentally validated, as we relied solely on bioinformatic predictions. Conclusion In summary, PLA2G6 -related early onset, autosomal recessive dystonia-parkinsonism is a phenotypic spectrum. Besides common motor symptoms, early psychiatric manifestations and cognitive impairment could not be ignored. Cerebral and cerebellar atrophy with or without iron deposition might be a radiological point to detect PLA2G6 -related parkinsonism. Genetic tests were suggested to instruct the diagnosis. Clinicians should pay more attention to the patients presented with psychiatric disorders at onset and avoid initiating potentially harmful treatments (ie, antipsychotics) in the presence of extrapyramidal involvement. There is no specific medication for PLA2G6 -related parkinsonism. Early, severe levodopa-induced dyskinesias have a serious negative impact on these young patients. More studies and treatment guides are eagerly needed to give clinicians promising disease-modifying strategies and instruction.
