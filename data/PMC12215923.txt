Abstract To analyze the changing trend of HIV/AIDS-related mortality before and after the implementation of the “Prevention and Treatment of HIV/AIDS” policy in Guilin from 1996 to 2020, and to evaluate its effect on HIV/AIDS prevention and treatment. The data of people with HIV reported in Guilin up to December 31, 2020, were obtained from the Chinese Center for Disease Control and Prevention (CDC), and the two rounds of “Prevention and Treatment of HIV/AIDS” policy intervention boundary points were taken in 2010 and 2015, respectively. The interruption time series (ITS) analysis was used to analyze the trend of HIV/AIDS-related mortality before and after the implementation of the HIV/AIDS Prevention and Treatment Project. From 1996 to 2020, the HIV/AIDS mortality rate decreased in Guilin, and the HIV/AIDS mortality rate of farmers was higher than the overall HIV/AIDS mortality rate. The ITS method analysis results showed that after the implementation of two rounds of “Prevention and Treatment of HIV/AIDS” policy, HIV/AIDS mortality rate showed a downward trend. Between 2010 and 2015, the overall decline in HIV/AIDS mortality rate exhibited a slope of -2.217 ( P &lt; 0.01). For individuals with an education level of high school or below, the decline in HIV/AIDS mortality rate during this period had a slope of -2.110 ( P &lt; 0.01). Among the farmer population, the decline in HIV/AIDS mortality rate from 2010 to 2015 was even more significant, with a slope of -2.510 ( P &lt; 0.01). From 2015 to 2020, the overall HIV/AIDS mortality rate continued to decline, showing a slope of -0.230 ( P &lt; 0.01). Between 2015 and 2020, for those with an education level of high school or below, the decline in HIV/AIDS mortality rate had a slope of -0.225 ( P &lt; 0.01). In contrast, the decline in HIV/AIDS mortality rate among farmers from 2015 to 2020 was steeper, with a slope of -0.319 ( P &lt; 0.01). From 2010 to 2020, the HIV/AIDS mortality rate in Guilin has shown a decreasing trend, indicating that the implementation of the “Prevention and Treatment of HIV/AIDS” policy has achieved good results. Introduction Acquired Immune Deficiency Syndrome (AIDS), an infectious disease caused by the Human Immunodeficiency Virus (HIV), severely damages the body’s immune system and is associated with a high mortality rate 1 – 3 . According to global statistics in 2020, approximately 37.7 million people were infected with HIV 4 . As of the end of 2020, there were a total of 1.053 million people infected with HIV in China, with a cumulative number of reported deaths at 351,000. Among them, Guilin has reported over 14,000 cumulative HIV/AIDS cases, with a high proportion of deaths accounting for 36.64% of all cases. Compared to Fangchenggang, where the proportion of deaths among HIV-infected individuals is 10.77% 5 , and Guigang, where the proportion is 11.90% 6 , HIV-infected individuals in Guilin exhibit a higher mortality rate related to HIV/AIDS. Despite the widespread adoption of Antiretroviral Therapy (ART) in Guilin, not all HIV-infected individuals are able to access and sustain standard treatment in a timely manner. Various factors may contribute to the lack of or irregular treatment among some HIV-infected individuals, leading to ineffective disease control. Additionally, the issue of drug resistance increases treatment difficulty, reduces efficacy, and makes disease management more challenging. Economic pressure also poses a significant obstacle, potentially hindering HIV-infected individuals from continuously accessing necessary medical services and medications. In terms of resource allocation and accessibility to medical services, especially in remote areas, there may be imbalances that affect the overall effectiveness of HIV/AIDS prevention and control. In the prevention and control of HIV/AIDS in Guilin, women, children, and key populations (such as migrants and sex workers) are disproportionately affected, necessitating targeted interventions. Among them, farmers, as a vulnerable group impacted by factors such as mobile employment and limited healthcare access, exhibit significant disparities in HIV infection and mortality risks. Due to occupation-related migration, low health awareness, and uneven coverage of rural medical services, HIV/AIDS-related mortality among farmers may serve as a critical indicator of the effectiveness of grassroots prevention and treatment efforts. A systematic analysis of mortality trends in this population will not only help identify gaps in interventions but also provide a scientific basis for developing targeted strategies, such as tailored screening programs and cross-regional treatment referral mechanisms. To effectively curb the spread of HIV/AIDS epidemic, the Guangxi Zhuang Autonomous Region launched an important provincial-level initiative—the “Prevention and Treatment of HIV/AIDS Program”—in 2010. Under this policy framework, Guilin responded promptly and comprehensively implemented all related requirements, actively strengthening efforts to prevent and control HIV/AIDS throughout the city. The main contents of the “Prevention and Treatment of HIV/AIDS” policy include: (1) Promote knowledge on HIV/AIDS prevention, carry out comprehensive social security management related to HIV/AIDS in accordance with the law, strengthen cross-departmental cooperation, and address the challenges of identifying and controlling high-risk sources of infection; (2) For high-risk behavioral groups prone to HIV/AIDS infection, comprehensive intervention measures including warning health education and condom promotion shall be implemented; (3) Improve the organizational leadership system for HIV/AIDS prevention and control in schools, and strengthen sexual health education and HIV/AIDS prevention education on campus; (4) Increase the coverage and quality of antiviral treatment and strengthen the follow-up management of HIV/AIDS patients; (5) Strengthen the construction of the HIV/AIDS prevention and control network at the grassroots level and enhance its prevention and control capacity; (6) Continuously enhance the public’s awareness of HIV/AIDS prevention and control, deepen the achievements of comprehensive social governance of HIV/AIDS, consolidate the achievements made in blocking blood transmission and controlling the spread of injecting drugs, promote the progress of eliminating mother-to-child transmission, curb the spread trend of sexual transmission routes, and discover, treat and manage HIV/AIDS patients to the greatest extent; (7) Reduce social discrimination against them, improve the quality of life of patients, and keep the HIV/AIDS epidemic at a low prevalence level continuously. Two rounds of projects have been carried out in Guilin, but there has been no systematic quantitative evaluation of the effectiveness of this policy, suggesting that it is necessary to adopt effective evaluation tools to reasonably assess the effectiveness of the ‘HIV/AIDS prevention and treatment project’ in Guilin. Public health intervention measures are often carried out among a certain group of people, using their before-and-after comparisons, and using infection rates, sickness rates, case mortality rate, etc. as effect evaluation indicators. In the past, researchers usually used the control group before the intervention and the observation group after the intervention and used statistical methods such as t-test and chi-square test for analysis 7 . However, these methods cannot control the pre-existing trends of time series data and easily ignore the impact of periodicity of onset 8 . Time series-based projection modeling, by modeling pre-intervention data to project post-intervention data, and comparing the projected values with the true values to judge the effect of the intervention; this method well fits the pre-existing trend of time series data by extracting the past time series information 9 . Although the method of evaluating the effect of interventions based on time-series predictive modeling can well fit the pre-existing trend of time-series data and address the impact of the cyclical nature of morbidity, this method cannot objectively reflect the existence of long- and short-term effects of public health interventions, and it is not possible to make accurate parameter estimations 10 . Interrupted time series analysis (ITSA) is a quasi-experimental design that evaluates the effectiveness of an intervention at a defined point in time 11 . And, ITS analysis refers to the use of statistical modeling to assess whether an intervention has had an impact on outcome indicators by collecting outcome data at multiple points in time before and after the intervention and analyzing trends in outcome indicators before and after the intervention. ITS can provide a more accurate and intuitive description of the instantaneous effects and long-term trends of policy interventions using routinely collected data in the absence of parallel control data and has good application in assessing changes in hospitalization costs, infection incidence, and healthcare costs before and after the implementation of relevant policies 12 – 14 . Currently, this method is often applied in health policy evaluation 15 – 18 , but there are fewer studies evaluating the impact of the Prevention and Treatment of HIV/AIDS policies on the mortality of People with HIV. Methods Data sources Data were downloaded from the China Disease Control and Prevention Information System until December 31, 2020, and a total of 14,062 HIV-infected patients with a current address in Guilin and HIV/AIDS-related deaths were screened for inclusion in the study. An interrupted time-series analysis was carried out on the data for the period 1996–2020, with 2010 and 2015 as the cut-off points for the first and second rounds of interventions under the “Prevention and Treatment of HIV/AIDS” policy, respectively. This study used segmented interrupted time series analysis: the years 2010–2015 were analysed as both the post-intervention phase of the first round and the pre-baseline period of the second round, through the construction of two independent models (first round: 1996–2009 vs. 2010–2015; second round: 2010–2015 vs. 2016–2020). The study subjects signed an informed consent form, the study was approved by the Medical Ethics Committee of Guilin Medical University (No. GLMC20240340), and all research was performed in accordance with the relevant guidelines/regulations. Method To specifically evaluate the implementation effects of the “Prevention and Treatment of HIV/AIDS” policy, we first analyzed the HIV/AIDS mortality rate among all People with HIV. Given that Guilin placed particular emphasis on enhancing public awareness of HIV/AIDS prevention and emphasized the breadth and effectiveness of promotional education during the initial stages of the policy, we further screened a subset of 14,062 People with HIV, specifically targeting those who were farmers and those with an education level of high school and below, to conduct a dedicated analysis of their HIV/AIDS mortality rate. The farmer and people with HIV with an education level of high school and below were selected because epidemiological data demonstrate this group has: (1) significantly higher HIV prevalence rates compared to other populations; (2) relatively poorer healthcare access and health literacy; and (3) well-documented disparities in HIV/AIDS mortality rate as evidenced in existing literature. To quantify the changes in the HIV/AIDS mortality rate in Guilin before and after the implementation of the “Prevention and Treatment of HIV/AIDS” policy, we employed Interrupted Time Series (ITS) analysis, conducting segmented regression analysis on the two periods before and after the policy implementation to comprehensively assess the actual effectiveness of the policy. The number of HIV/AIDS deaths mainly due to AIDS-related deaths was selected from the database, and the mortality rate was used as an indicator for evaluating the effectiveness of the policy of the “Prevention and Treatment of HIV/AIDS” in combating HIV/AIDS. Discussion Since the implementation of the “Prevention and Treatment of HIV/AIDS”, Guilin has achieved certain results in the prevention and treatment of HIV/AIDS, the rights and interests of People with HIV have been safeguarded, the HIV/AIDS help system has been improved, and the knowledge of HIV/AIDS prevention has been spread in a wide range. The results of this study show that by the end of 2020, the overall HIV/AIDS mortality rate among People with HIV whose current address is Guilin is lower than the HIV/AIDS mortality rate among those whose occupation is farmer and whose education level is high school or below. This phenomenon may be attributed to the early-stage HIV/AIDS epidemic in Guilin, where most infected individuals were farmers and those with high school and lower education levels population with limited HIV/AIDS awareness. Due to inadequate knowledge about the disease, these groups often failed to receive appropriate medical care and support services. Consequently, farmers and individuals with lower educational attainment exhibited a higher AIDS-related mortality rate 22 , 23 . In particular, farmers and groups of People with HIV with high school and lower literacy levels tend to be of lower socio-economic status and may be exposed to additional life stresses, health risks and inadequate health services. These unfavourable conditions may make them more vulnerable to the threat of HIV/AIDS, and once infected, they may not be able to access timely and effective treatment due to limited medical resources and economic conditions, leading to increased mortality. Farmers and groups of People with HIV with an education level of high school and below may have relatively limited knowledge and understanding of HIV/AIDS. With limited access to information and relatively low education levels, they may lack sufficient knowledge of HIV/AIDS prevention and awareness of self-protection. This increases their risk of contracting HIV/AIDS and they may not be able to take the right measures to control their condition after infection, which in turn increases the mortality rate. Farmers and groups of People with HIV with an education level of high school and below may face greater difficulties in accessing health services. They may have difficulty in accessing timely and effective HIV/AIDS testing and treatment services due to their remote location, lack of transport and uneven distribution of medical resources. This results in their inability to receive timely diagnosis and treatment after infection, which increases mortality rate. Farmers and the group of People with HIV with an education level of high school and below may have some high-risk behaviours and lifestyles, such as unsafe sex, sharing syringes or needles, and mother-to-child transmission. These behaviours may increase their risk of contracting HIV/AIDS. At the same time, they may not be able to take effective measures to prevent the occurrence of these high-risk behaviours due to hygienic conditions and health awareness, thus further increasing the mortality rate. In the course of implementing the policy of the “Prevention and Treatment of HIV/AIDS” in Guilin, a number of specific measures have been taken to improve the accessibility of medical care for farmers and groups with an education level of high school or below. Firstly, by strengthening publicity and education, Guilin has continued to carry out HIV/AIDS prevention and treatment publicity and education activities ‘in communities, enterprises, hospitals, campuses and families’, holding lectures, distributing publicity materials, broadcasting educational videos, and conducting ‘face-to-face’ campaigns in villages and households, in such places as schools, communities, and health-care institutions. Through holding lectures in schools, communities, medical institutions and other places, distributing publicity materials, broadcasting educational videos and going to villages and households to conduct ‘face-to-face’ publicity, the public’s knowledge of HIV/AIDS and awareness of prevention have been raised. Secondly, almost all medical institutions above the second level and township health centres in Guilin have the capacity to test for antibodies to HIV/AIDS, and pilot HIV/AIDS screening work has been carried out in conjunction with basic public health services such as medical check-ups for the elderly. In addition, Guilin has fully implemented policies such as free counselling and antibody testing, free antiretroviral treatment, free mother-to-child interruption, free schooling for orphaned children, and care and assistance for People with HIV through a ‘one-stop’ care and assistance mechanism, in order to alleviate the burden of medical treatment on patients. Together, these measures have provided strong support for improving the accessibility of health care for farmers and groups with an education level of high school or below. Reported rates of HIV/AIDS-related deaths were compared with rates of HIV/AIDS-related deaths among people living with HIV/AIDS with an education level of high school and below and among people living with HIV/AIDS who were farmers by occupation, using ITS analysis. The results show a reduction in HIV/AIDS-related deaths after the first round of implementation of the "Prevention and Treatment of HIV/AIDS" policy in 2010; In particular, there has been a significant decrease in the rates of deaths among People with HIV whose occupation is farmer and whose education level is high school or below; After the second round of implementation of the “Prevention and Treatment of HIV/AIDS” policy in 2015, the rates of HIV/AIDS-related deaths has been slow to change, and is still on a downward trend, remaining at a low level. The likely reason for this is: the implementation of the "Prevention and Treatment of HIV/AIDS" has led Guangxi to continuously strengthen the promotion of HIV/AIDS awareness and education among susceptible populations, expand testing and antiretroviral treatment coverage. With the support of the “Prevention and Treatment of HIV/AIDS” policy, Guilin has set up a number of additional HIV nucleic acid testing sites, where Guilin residents can be tested for HIV through local medical institutions, community health service centers, or disease prevention and control agencies, and the testing process follows strict privacy protection measures to ensure the security of personal information. If the test result is positive, the medical and healthcare institution will conduct further diagnosis and assessment, including CD4 + T-lymphocyte count and viral load determination. Based on the assessment results, the doctor will formulate a personalized and free treatment plan, which usually includes anti-retroviral therapy (ART), etc. The designated medical institutions in Guilin will provide standardized free treatment services to ensure that patients receive effective treatment. In addition to medication, Guilin also focuses on psychological care and social support for people living with HIV. Medical and health institutions and social organizations will provide psychological counseling, health education and other services to help patients better cope with the challenges posed by the disease. At the same time, Guilin also actively promotes the popularization and dissemination of knowledge about HIV prevention and treatment to raise public awareness and understanding of the disease. Guilin citizens can find out their infection status by going to a healthcare facility for an HIV test. If they are diagnosed with HIV infection, they can apply for free treatment eligibility from designated medical institutions. The application process requires the provision of personal identification, test reports and other relevant materials. The designated medical institution will formulate a personalized treatment plan according to the patient’s specific situation and treatment needs, and provide free treatment services accordingly. As of 2020, Guilin has established 42 Voluntary Counseling and Testing (VCT) clinics, covering 6 districts and 11 counties (cities) throughout the city. Susceptible populations can consult and undergo testing at nearby clinics. The establishment of VCT clinics has facilitated HIV/AIDS counseling and testing, promoted the dissemination of HIV/AIDS prevention knowledge, and to a certain extent, reduced the mortality rate among HIV/AIDS patients 24 . The second round of implementation of the “Prevention and Treatment of HIV/AIDS” policy, strengthening the results of the first round of implementation of the “Prevention and Treatment of HIV/AIDS”, further optimising the prevention and treatment mechanism, and further improving the grassroots prevention and treatment network. Knowledge about HIV/AIDS prevention has been widely disseminated, and rural residents and people with a low level of literacy have been provided with information and education on the prevention and treatment of HIV/AIDS; they have gained a better understanding of the disease and are actively cooperating with treatment, and the rates of deaths due to HIV/AIDS-related illnesses has continued to remain at a relatively low level. The results of the ITS analyses show a slow decline in the overall HIV/AIDS mortality rate after both the first and second rounds of implementation of the “Prevention and Treatment of HIV/AIDS” policy. However, although the death rates among farmers and People with HIV with an education level of high school or below have dropped considerably compared with the period before the implementation of the policy of the “Prevention and Treatment of HIV/AIDS”, it has shown an upward trend and fluctuates in some periods. This may be since farmers and people with a low level of education do not pay enough attention to the prevention and treatment of HIV/AIDS and that the failure of these two groups to take the initiative in seeking advice on HIV/AIDS and receive timely treatment is an important factor in the high level of HIV/AIDS mortality rate in these two groups 22 , 25 . Before 2005, the rates of HIV/AIDS-related deaths was lower than it was after the implementation of the policy of the “Prevention and Treatment of HIV/AIDS”, probably because that was the early stage of the development of the HIV/AIDS epidemic in Guilin, when there were fewer People with HIV. The widespread spread of HIV/AIDS has been accompanied over time by an increasing rate of death from the disease. Domestic and international studies have confirmed that publicizing and implementing information on HIV prevention can promote people’s initiative to go for counseling and testing and to cooperate with antiretroviral treatment when they are found to be infected 26 . The policy of the “Prevention and Treatment of HIV/AIDS” has thus contributed to the dissemination of knowledge about HIV/AIDS prevention and has been effective in reducing the rate of HIV/AIDS-related illnesses and deaths. Comparing the policies of the “Prevention and Treatment of HIV/AIDS”, the “Four Free Services One Care” 20 and the “People’s War against HIV/AIDS” 27 in Yunnan Province, we find that these similar policies have achieved remarkable results in different contexts, as evidenced by the significant decline in HIV/AIDS mortality. We find that these similar HIV/AIDS prevention and treatment strategies have achieved remarkable results under different implementation contexts, with the common manifestation being a significant decrease in the HIV/AIDS mortality rate. This common result is strong evidence that effective HIV/AIDS prevention and treatment policies can significantly improve the living conditions of HIV/AIDS patients and effectively reduce their mortality rate. The ITS method of analysis has a number of limitations in assessing the impact of policy interventions on HIV/AIDS mortality that may have important implications for the interpretation and reliability of the study results. The ITS method lacks data on a control group over the same time period, which prevents a clear distinction between the impact of interventions and other factors occurring at the same time on the outcome. For example, other policy changes, environmental factors, or improvements in socio-economic conditions may have occurred simultaneously with the intervention and thus contributed to the reduction in mortality 20 . The ITS methodology assumes that unmeasured confounders change relatively slowly, whereas in reality, time-varying confounders (e.g., the implementation of other policies or a public health event) may have a significant effect on the outcome variable. In this case, single-group ITS may underestimate or overestimate the true effect of an intervention 28 . Therefore, in subsequent research, more complex statistical models and more detailed analytical methods can be employed to assess the effectiveness of the intervention measures. In addition, another limitation of the ITS approach is its dependence on time-series data. If a significant trend in the outcome variable already existed before the implementation of the intervention, a single set of ITS may not be able to accurately distinguish the effect of the intervention from the natural trend on the outcome. To overcome these limitations, future studies may consider using more sophisticated statistical models, such as two-group ITS designs, to better control for the effects of confounding factors by introducing a control group. The decline in the mortality rate of People with HIV is the result of a synergy of factors, of which the policy of the “Prevention and Treatment of HIV/AIDS” has played a key, but not exclusive, role. Socio-economic factors have also played an important role, with higher incomes and better access to medical resources enabling patients to receive treatment and care in a more timely manner. Advances in medical technology, such as more accurate diagnostic methods and more effective treatments, have also had a positive impact on reducing mortality. At the same time, effective management of non-HIV/AIDS-related causes of death, such as cardiovascular disease, liver disease and non-HIV/AIDS-defined malignant tumours, is also an important aspect of mortality reduction. Social support and the reduction of discrimination should also not be overlooked, as they can improve treatment adherence and the psychological state of patients, thus indirectly reducing the risk of death. This study has its limitations. Due to the evolving standards for antiretroviral therapy (ART) based on CD4 + T lymphocyte counts, each HIV-infected individual initiates ART at different CD4 + T levels. When HIV-infected individuals with higher baseline CD4 + T levels start ART, their immune function can be reconstituted or repaired under the action of antiretroviral drugs, leading to better treatment outcomes 29 . Consequently, the survival time of HIV-infected individuals is extended, and HIV/AIDS-related mortality decreases, which may introduce bias in the study results. In future research, it is necessary to distinguish HIV-infected individuals with different CD4 + T levels and analyze changes in their mortality rate. In reviewing this study, we realized a key study limitation, which was the failure to fully control for the impact of potential confounders beyond CD4 + T counts on mortality. To improve the accuracy and completeness of the study, it is important to consider these additional confounders in greater depth in subsequent studies and to design more refined statistical models to dissect trends in mortality. The “Prevention and Treatment of HIV/AIDS” not only focuses on the promotion of HIV/AIDS prevention knowledge but also emphasizes prevention, control, care, and support for key regions and populations, which are not analyzed in this paper. In the future, further evaluations of the “Prevention and Treatment of HIV/AIDS” policy will be conducted in terms of strengthening surveillance of key populations and regions, improving the care and support system, and other aspects. In summary, there is a positive correlation between the implementation of the “Prevention and Treatment of HIV/AIDS” policy and the reduction in mortality rate among HIV-infected individuals. After the implementation of the second round of the policy, the HIV/AIDS-related mortality rate in Guilin has consistently remained at a low level, fully demonstrating the significant achievements of the “Prevention and Treatment of HIV/AIDS”. With the change in the form of HIV/AIDS transmission, the focus of the next round of the “Prevention and Treatment of HIV/AIDS” should be continuously adjusted to continuously raise public awareness of HIV/AIDS prevention and treatment, deepen the effectiveness of the comprehensive social management of HIV/AIDS prevention and treatment, improve the quality of life of people living with HIV/AIDS, and continue to keep the HIV/AIDS epidemic at a low-prevalence level.
