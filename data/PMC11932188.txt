Assistant Professor, Dept of Psychiatry, Government Medical College, Ernakulam The workshop aims to provide an overview of the development and validation of psychiatric measurement tools, which are essential for both research and clinical practice. It will cover three main areas: 1) Development of the Instrument : The first step involves identifying priority measurement areas. Participants will learn how to create items through literature reviews, qualitative research, and expert consensus. The workshop will address ensuring face validity and content validity, demonstrating how to calculate indices of content validity and modify preliminary tools based on cognitive interviews. 2) Establishing Construct Validity : Participants will explore methods to test the preliminary instrument for construct validity. This includes assessing internal consistency reliability and conducting factor analysis, with guidance on modifying items and domains based on the findings. 3) Psychometric Properties : The workshop will explain key concepts such as convergent, criterion, and predictive validity, along with various forms of reliability. It will also cover how to establish criterion validity for categorical and continuous instruments, including the use of ROC curves to determine optimal cut-offs. Additionally, presenters will discuss sample size requirements for each stage of the development process, ensuring that participants gain a comprehensive understanding of creating reliable and valid measurement tools in psychiatry. This structured approach will equip attendees with the knowledge necessary to develop effective assessment instruments that can enhance both research outcomes and clinical assessments. Adult ADHD: Diagnostic Challenges, Comorbidity Management, and Forensic Psychiatry Implications Dr Prasanth Ampalam 1 , Dr Raj Harsh 2 1. Consultant Psychiatrist &amp; Associate Clinical Director, Devon Partnership Trust, UK 2. Consultant Psychiatrist, Birmingham and Solihull NHS Mental Health Foundation Trust, UK Adult Attention-Deficit/Hyperactivity Disorder (ADHD) is increasingly recognized as a persistent and often debilitating condition, with prevalence estimates around 2.5-4.5% in adults. Diagnosing ADHD in adults is challenging due to symptom overlap with other psychiatric and medical conditions, along with the potential for late-onset presentations. Effective diagnosis requires a thorough clinical interview, validated rating scales, and historical corroboration to confirm the persistence of symptoms since childhood. Comorbidities such as mood disorders, substance use disorders, and personality disorders are common and often complicate treatment, necessitating a tailored approach to manage overlapping symptoms and risks. Forensic psychiatry adds complexity, as ADHD may impact behaviors relevant to criminality, responsibility, and risk of recidivism, requiring careful assessment to discern symptom-driven behavior from other motivations. Management strategies emphasize pharmacological interventions like stimulants and non-stimulants, along with cognitive-behavioral therapy to address executive functioning deficits. In forensic settings, a balanced approach is essential to account for both therapeutic needs and public safety considerations, making the accurate assessment and management of adult ADHD crucial in multidisciplinary forensic evaluations. Changing nature of Child and Adolescent Psychiatry inpatient care in the United Kingdom Dr. Abhay Rathore Consultant Psychiatrist, Dept of Child &amp; Adolescent Psychiatry, Beacon, CAMHS, Leicester The landscape of Child and Adolescent Psychiatry inpatient care in the United Kingdom has undergone significant changes in recent years. This evolving practice reflects broader systemic, social, and policy-level shifts, particularly in response to new service models and the COVID-19 pandemic. This presentation aims to analyze the evolving nature of inpatient care within Child and Adolescent Psychiatry in the UK. Drawing on my experience as a consultant psychiatrist, I will explore key factors driving these changes, including: The impact of the COVID-19 pandemic on service demands and patient profiles, the establishment of community-based services such as Crisis Resolution Teams and Intensive Community Support Teams and the role of Provider Collaboratives in influencing referral patterns from outpatient services. I will also examine variations across different inpatient settings, including CAMHS Psychiatric Intensive Care Units (PICUs), Specialist Eating Disorder Units (SEDUs), and General Adolescent Units (GAUs). Data from the past three years, provided by the NHS England Provider Collaborative, will be analysed to identify trends in referral rates and patterns. Preliminary analysis indicates a sustained and gradual reduction in inpatient referrals, likely linked to the increased availability of intensive community support services. The introduction of enhanced community services has resulted in measurable changes in referral patterns to inpatient care. This presentation will consider whether these trends reflect s transient response to systemic disruptions, such as the pandemic, or signify a permanent shift in service delivery models. District Mental Health Programme (BBMP), Bengaluru, Karnataka This specialized workshop is designed to enhance healthcare providers’ skills in suicide prevention, risk assessment, and brief intervention for individuals who have attempted suicide. Focusing on emergency department (ED)-initiated interventions successfully implemented in select hospitals across Bangalore, the workshop adopts an evidence-based approach to provide immediate support and reduce the risk of repeat suicide attempts among survivors. The workshop seeks to improve participants’ abilities to identify and assess suicide risk, develop effective risk formulations, apply brief intervention techniques, and enhance collaboration among healthcare teams. Participants will explore the impact of suicide on public health and the vital role of healthcare providers, along with an overview of the ED-initiated intervention model. Various techniques for identifying cases in the ED and conducting effective risk assessments will be discussed. Participants will learn about brief intervention models, effective techniques, and successful case studies from Bangalore hospitals. The workshop will also address caregivers’ needs, strategies for providing support, and enhancing communication between healthcare providers and caregivers. There will be interactive Practice Sessions - Role plays, simulation exercises, group discussions, and case study analyses will facilitate real-world application of learned concepts. By the end of the workshop, participants will be equipped to conduct comprehensive suicide risk assessments, implement effective interventions, support caregivers, and integrate ED-initiated interventions into clinical practice. Scale Development and Validation in Psychiatry and Behavioural Sciences - A Primer Dr. Snehil Gupta 1 , Dr. Sandeep Grover 2 , Dr. Vikas Menon 3 1. Associate Professor, Dept of Psychiatry, AIIMS, Bhopal 2. Professor, Dept of Psychiatry, PGIMER, Chandigarh 3. Professor, Dept of Psychiatry, JIPMER, Puducherry Scale development and validation are critical to the work in the health, social, and behavioral sciences. It is a thrust area of research, as per the Indian Council of Medical Research. Tool development is vital to assess a particular phenomenon or practice in each culture or context. Not attending to nuances related to tools’ development can affect the validity and reliability of the research. However, the set of scale development and evaluation techniques can be confusing, jargon-filled, inconsistent, unfamiliar, and resource-intensive. Further, it is often not a part of graduate training. This workshop aims to demonstrate the process of scale development which goes through three phases in a straightforward way to facilitate the development of new, valid, and reliable scales and to help improve existing tools, with support from the literature and presenters’ experience. This primer shall equip researchers, academicians, and practitioners to understand the methodology of scale development/ validation, facilitating our understanding of a range of health, social, and behavioural outcomes. The integration of Artificial Intelligence (AI) and Virtual Reality (VR) into mental health care has transformed therapeutic approaches, particularly in exposure therapy. VR creates safe, immersive environments for patients to confront fears and trauma, while AI personalizes treatment and monitors progress. This workshop will focus on the practical applications of AI and VR in exposure therapy for conditions such as PTSD, anxiety disorders, specific phobias, and OCD. The main objectives of the workshop would be to introduce participants to the scientific principles and evidence supporting VR-based exposure therapy, explore how AI enhances therapy by tailoring interventions and analyzing patient response, equip clinicians with practical skills to implement AI-VR technologies in their practice, address logistical and technical challenges in integrating these tools into mental health care and foster innovation by encouraging the adoption of cutting-edge technology. The workshop would begin with an introductory lecture on the Overview of AI and VR technologies in mental health followed by Hands-On Sessions on setting up VR equipment for exposure therapy and using AI tools for therapy customization. All successful clinical cases utilizing AI-VR exposure therapy would be reviewed. There would also be a discussion on ethical considerations, data security, patient consent, and implementation barriers.
