Background A comprehensive strategy encompassing universal testing and strengthened treatment was promoted to address the severe HIV epidemic in Liangshan. This study aimed to assess the impact of this strategy on HIV treatment continuum. Methods Interrupted time series (ITS) analysis was utilized to evaluate the impact of a comprehensive strategy on HIV treatment outcomes in Liangshan from 2014 to 2021 utilizing data from China's HIV/AIDS Comprehensive Response Information Management System (CRIMS). Poisson or quasi-Poisson segmented regression models were applied to estimate the trends and immediate changes in key outcomes, including the proportion of 30-day antiretroviral therapy (ART) initiation, the proportion of 6-month follow-up, and the proportion of viral suppression, following the strategy's implementation in January 2018. The analysis was stratified by sex, age, transmission route, and region, with areas classified as key areas and non-key areas based on an HIV prevalence threshold of 1%. Results A total of 37,888 people living with HIV (PLHIV) were identified during the study period, with an increase in diagnoses following comprehensive strategy implementation. Most cases involved males, aged 15–34 years, residents of key areas, and individuals infected through heterosexual transmission. There was a significant increase of 2.50-fold in 30-day ART initiation (incidence rate ratio [IRR] 2.501, 95% CI: 1.694–3.694), 34.0% in 6-month follow-up (1.340, 95% CI: 1.234–1.456), and 17.7% in viral suppression (1.177, 95% CI: 1.068–1.296) in the first month of comprehensive strategy implementation. Over time, all three indicators displayed an upward trend, and by December 2021, their predicted values significantly increased, exceeding the expected levels by 60.3% (IRR 1.603, 95% CI: 1.108–2.318), 62.9% (1.629, 95% CI: 1.430–1.855), and 50.6% (1.506, 95% CI: 1.223–1.855), respectively. Subgroup analysis revealed that while the strategy's effects on these outcomes were generally consistent, the magnitude of improvement varied. Residents of key areas exhibited more significant increases in 30-day ART initiation, while residents of non-key areas, particularly those aged ≥ 60 years, displayed smaller increases in the proportion of 6-month follow-up, and no significant change in the proportion of viral suppression, with a slightly lower average. Conclusions The implementation of a comprehensive strategy substantially improved timely ART initiation, follow-up care after ART and viral suppression for PLHIV in Liangshan, with consistent enhancements across various subpopulations. It is imperative to strengthen comprehensive strategy in non-key areas of Liangshan, and to promote these strategies in other similar regions in order to enhance health benefits for patients. Initially, driven by injecting drug use, the local epidemic has shifted toward heterosexual transmission, broadening its impact beyond people who inject drugs (PWID) to the general population, with HIV prevalence in the key counties exceeding 1% [ 1 – 3 ]. The region continues to demonstrate suboptimal treatment outcomes, characterized by significantly lower rates of viral suppression and medication adherence compared to national benchmarks. These challenges are compounded by multiple structural barriers, including inadequate healthcare infrastructure, a shortage of trained medical professionals, and economic constraints [ 4 , 5 ]. These factors contribute to compromised health outcomes among people living with HIV (PLHIV), ongoing HIV transmission and elevated mortality rates [ 6 – 8 ], underscoring the urgent need for targeted interventions to improve treatment efficacy and compliance, and ultimately reduce HIV incidence and mortality. Early initiation of antiretroviral therapy (ART) not only extends patient survival and improves quality of life [ 9 ] but also serves as a key preventive strategy against HIV transmission [ 10 – 12 ]. In 2016, the World Health Organization (WHO) advocated for the universal test and treat (UTT) policy, and China also adopted it nationwide in July 2016 to promote early treatment, following the ART eligibility threshold for CD4 + T cells of 500 cells/µl in 2014 [ 13 , 14 ]. Despite concerted efforts and some progress made in controlling the HIV epidemic in Liangshan, the local situation still remains uncontained [ 1 , 4 ]. In September 2017, a comprehensive strategy was implemented, accompanied by the launch of the “First Phase Action Plan for HIV Prevention and Health Poverty Alleviation in Liangshan Prefecture” (referred to as the “Action Plan”) [ 15 ]. The comprehensive strategy encompassed universal HIV screening and a series of enhanced measures along the HIV treatment continuum [ 1 , 16 ]. Assessing the real-world impact of the comprehensive strategy on care cascade outcomes is essential for optimizing future HIV prevention and control policies. Although the proportions of HIV screening, ART coverage, and viral suppression have increased following the Action Plan [ 1 ], there is a lack of comprehensive evaluation of its impact on the HIV treatment continuum. Several UTT trials have reported significantly improved outcomes across the HIV care cascade, but comprehensive measures in these studies are not typically available for real-world program delivery [ 17 – 21 ]. Therefore, it is crucial to quantify the specific contributions of this comprehensive strategy in real-world settings, including its impact on trend change. This study aimed to measure the effect of a comprehensive strategy in Liangshan on the HIV treatment continuum, focusing on metrics such as ART initiation within 30 days after diagnosis, follow-up within 6 months after ART initiation, and viral suppression among newly diagnosed PLHIV. Methods Study design Using data from China's HIV/AIDS Comprehensive Response Information Management System (CRIMS) [ 22 , 23 ], we conducted an interrupted time series analysis to assess the effect of the comprehensive strategy of the Action Plan on HIV treatment outcomes among PLHIV diagnosed in Liangshan between January 1, 2014, and December 31, 2021. Baseline information was collected for each patient, including sex, date of birth, residence, route of transmission, time of HIV diagnosis, time and result of CD4 testing and time of ART initiation. Following treatment initiation, patients were required to undergo clinical follow-up at least once a month for the first three months and every three months thereafter, along with viral load testing at least once per year, in accordance with national guidelines [ 24 , 25 ]. These treatment management data were recorded in the National Free Antiretroviral Treatment Program (NFATP), a CRIMS subsystem [ 26 , 27 ]. Comprehensive strategy The comprehensive strategy implemented under the action plan focused on universal HIV testing and enhanced measures across each step of the HIV treatment continuum, particularly in four key counties: Butuo, Zhaojue, Yuexi, and Meigu (designated as key areas). The core components of the strategy included the following: 1) Universal HIV screening was integrated into the free health examination program for residents aged 18 months and above in areas severely affected with HIV. This initiative achieved a screening rate of 98% in the resident population of high-epidemic counties in 2018, through extensive investigations and tracking at the township and village levels. In key areas, additional initiatives such as Provider-Initiated HIV Testing and Counseling (PITC) were implemented, achieving 100% coverage among inpatients and 70% among outpatients in key departments of medical institutions [ 1 ]. 2) Enhanced measures along the HIV treatment continuum, such as establishing a management system to streamline the process from diagnosis to treatment, incorporating policies like the treat-all policy, seamless patient referrals, precision management models, and targeted interventions for key populations. Specifically, the “Three Lines and One Network Base” technical support system integrates three specialized prevention and control entities (CDC, medical institutions, and maternal and child health centers) with a robust grassroots network, ensuring improved coordination and service delivery. Additionally, the “1 + M + N” township prevention system clarifies responsibilities across all levels of township health personnel, strengthening accountability and continuity of care. These strategies were particularly strengthened for subgroups such as PLHIV among women of childbearing age, pregnant women and HIV serodiscordant couples [ 1 , 16 ]. Outcomes Primary outcomes included the monthly proportions of 30-day ART initiation, 6-month follow-up, and viral suppression before and after the Action Plan. These indicators assess both short- and long-term patient outcomes, reflecting the continuum of HIV treatment. Proportion of 30-day ART initiation refers to the percentage of patients who initiated ART within 30 days of diagnosis, which measures how quickly eligible patients begin ART after diagnosis. The time interval of ART initiation was calculated by subtracting the date of ART initiation from the date of HIV diagnosis. Proportion of 6-month follow-up refers to the percentage of patients who attended at least one clinical visit within 3 to 6 months after starting ART, reflecting their retention on ART. Proportion of viral suppression refers to the percentage of patients with viral suppression (viral load ≤ 400 copies/mL) among patients with effective viral load testing (performed more than half a year after ART initiation), reflecting the effectiveness of treatment. Outcomes were stratified by sex (males vs. females), age (0–14, 15–34, 35–59, and ≥ 60 years), route of transmission (heterosexual, injection drug use and others, such as mother-to-child, homosexual, blood transfusion) and region (key areas vs. non-key areas). The regional stratification of Liangshan was based on an HIV prevalence threshold of 1%. Four counties demonstrating HIV prevalence above 1% were classified as key areas, while the remaining 13 counties were non-key areas [ 1 ]. Statistical analysis Descriptive statistics summarized demographic characteristics before and after the implementation of the Action Plan. Continuous variables are presented as medians and interquartile ranges (IQRs), while categorical variables are described using frequencies and proportions. Changes in PLHIV characteristics before and after the Action Plan were assessed using the χ 2 test for categorical variables and the Kruskal‒Wallis test for continuous variables. An interrupted time series (ITS) analysis was conducted to evaluate the comprehensive strategy’s impact on the outcome indicators. Poisson or quasi-Poisson segmented regression models were used to quantify the strategy’s immediate impact and its influence on trends over time. Considering the potential delay in the full adoption of a new policy, we set January 2018 as the implementation date. The quasi-Poisson model was used to capture overdispersion in the outcomes. The model incorporated a time variable, a dummy variable for the strategy’s implementation, and an interaction term between time and the dummy variable. This approach facilitated the assessment of the strategy’s effectiveness by centralizing time at different time points [ 28 – 30 ]. The change trend following the Action Plan is the sum of the two coefficients of time and time-dummy interaction in the model. The model before the Action Plan predicted outcomes after January 2018 in the absence of the comprehensive strategy, representing the counterfactual scenario. Expected levels were defined as the expected numbers from the counterfactual model at the end of outcome analysis [ 30 , 31 ]. The incidence rate ratio (IRR) was calculated by comparing fitted numbers from the model with expected numbers from the contemporaneous counterfactual. The Newey‒West method adjusted for autocorrelation and heteroscedasticity [ 32 , 33 ] provides a 95% confidence interval (CI) for the IRR, with the lag taking the optimal value calculated through the automatic bandwidth selection procedure. Separate models were established for sex, age, transmission route, and region, adjusting for seasonality and long-term disease trends. Seasonal variation was adjusted using a Fourier term consisting of two sine/cosine pairs in the model [ 32 ]. Sensitivity analysis without considering seasonality was performed. All the statistical tests were two-sided, with P &lt; 0.05 considered to indicate statistical significance. All data analyses were performed using SPSS 29.0 and R 4.2.3 (R Foundation for Statistical Computing, Vienna, Austria; see appendix). Results Between January 1, 2014, and December 31, 2021, a total of 37,888 PLHIV were diagnosed in Liangshan, with 17,755 cases diagnosed before the Action Plan (2014–2017) and 20,133 cases after the Action Plan (2018–2021). Most patients were males (58.5%), aged 15–34 years (51.9%), resided in key areas (54.8%), and contracted HIV through heterosexual transmission (65.3%). Among these characteristics, the proportions of males, individuals aged 15–34 years, and those residing in key areas declined after the Action Plan, while the proportion of those who acquired HIV through heterosexual contact increased by 18.7%. The median age at diagnosis was 32 years (IQR: 26–40), remaining stable before and after the Action Plan (Table 1 ). The median CD4 count at diagnosis decreased from 392 cells/μl (IQR: 272–541) before the Action Plan to 354 cells/μl (IQR: 235–514) after the Action Plan. Following the implementation of the comprehensive strategy in 2018, there was a noticeable increase in the number of cases (Fig. 1 ). Proportions of 30-day ART initiation The median time from diagnosis to ART initiation was 107 days (IQR: 29–357), which decreased from 376 days (IQR: 104–808) before the Action Plan to 46 days (IQR: 17–132) after the Action Plan. The median monthly proportion of 30-day ART initiation among newly diagnosed cases increased significantly from 6.4% (IQR: 4.2–10.5) before the Action Plan to 48.6% (IQR: 37.1–56.1) after its implementation. For patients eligible for ART, before the Action Plan, the median time to ART initiation was 326 days (IQR: 89–695), and the median proportion initiating ART within 30 days was 7.6% (IQR: 4.8–10.6). Following the action plan in 2018, the median time to ART initiation decreased to 46 days (IQR: 17–132) and the median proportion of 30-day ART initiation increased to 48.6% (IQR: 37.1–56.1). These values were equivalent to the corresponding metrics in the overall PLHIV population due to universal eligibility for ART among PLHIV. Quasi-Poisson segmented regression analysis showed that among all newly diagnosed patients, the proportion of 30-day ART initiation increased by 2.38-fold (IRR 2.384, 95% CI: 1.629–3.488) in the first month after the Action Plan compared to the counterfactual trend. For those patients eligible for ART, the increase was 2.50-fold (IRR 2.501, 95% CI: 1.694–3.694). The proportion showed an upward trend before the Action Plan (IRR 1.023, 95% CI: 1.018–1.028) and continued to increase by 1.3% per month (1.013, 95% CI: 1.003–1.024) after the Action Plan (Table 2 and Fig. 2 ). Stratified analysis revealed significant increases across all groups in the first month after the Action Plan. By the end of 2021, the predicted proportion exceeded the expected level by 60.3% (IRR 1.603, 95% CI: 1.108–2.318). The predicted proportions were particularly significant for females (IRR 2.080, 95% CI: 1.304–3.316) compared to males (1.289, 0.713–2.329), individuals aged 35–59 years (1.775, 95% CI: 1.011–3.115), residents of key areas (1.961, 95% CI: 1.060–3.626), and individuals infected by heterosexual transmission (1.623, 95% CI: 1.077–2.444, Table 2 ). The results were consistent in the sensitivity analysis not considering seasonality (Additional file Table S1). Proportions of 6-month follow-up Among those newly diagnosed PLHIV who initiated ART, the median monthly proportion of 6-month follow-up increased from 77.4% (IQR: 72.2–82.3) before the Action Plan to 95.4% (IQR: 94.2–96.5) after the Action Plan (analysis up to June 2021). Poisson segmented regression analysis indicated that the proportion at 6-month follow-up increased by 34.0% (IRR 1.340, 95% CI: 1.234–1.456) in the first month after the Action Plan. The trend decreased by 0.4% per month (IRR 0.996, 95% CI: 0.994–0.998) before the Action Plan and slightly increased (1.001, 95% CI: 1.000–1.002) after the Action Plan. By the end of the analysis, the predicted proportion increased by 62.9% (IRR 1.629, 95% CI: 1.430–1.855) compared to the expected level (Table 3 and Fig. 2 ). The results were consistent across all subgroups both after the Action Plan and at the end of the analysis (Table 3 ). Sensitivity analysis without accounting for seasonality showed consistent results (Additional file Table S1). Proportions of viral suppression Among those newly diagnosed PLHIV who initiated ART, the proportion of patients who received viral load testing increased from 61.6% (5088/8256) before the Action Plan to 97.9% (85 213/87 066) after the Action Plan. For those receiving viral load testing, the median monthly proportion of viral suppression increased from 65.5% (IQR: 59.4–75.8) before the Action Plan to 81.6% (IQR: 77.0–85.8) after the Action Plan. Quasi-Poisson segmented regression analysis indicated that the proportion of viral suppression increased by 17.7% (IRR 1.177, 95% CI: 1.068–1.296) in the first month after the Action Plan. There was no significant trend before the Action Plan (IRR 0.998, 95% CI: 0.993–1.003), but an increase of 0.4% per month (1.004, 95% CI: 1.002–1.005) was observed after the Action Plan. By the end of the analysis, the proportion of viral suppression was predicted to increase by 50.6% (IRR 1.506, 95% CI: 1.223–1.855) compared with the expected level (Table 4 and Fig. 2 ). The results were consistent in the sensitivity analysis not considering seasonality (Additional file Table S1). According to the stratified analysis, the proportion of viral suppression significantly increased for males (IRR 1.174, 95% CI: 1.043–1.321), whereas the change was not significant for females (1.032, 95% CI: 0.852–1.252) (Table 4 ). Key areas and non-key areas Compared to key areas, non-key areas had higher proportions of cases among males (64.2% vs. 53.9%), individuals aged 35–59 years (40.9% vs. 31.1%) and 60 years and above (9.7% vs. 1.6%), and those who contracted HIV through heterosexual transmission (73.2% vs. 58.8%, Additional file Table S2). Before the Action Plan, key areas had lower median monthly proportions for 30-day ART initiation, 6-month follow-up and viral suppression than non-key areas. After the Action Plan, these indicators increased in both key and non-key areas. Despite this increase, the proportion of 30-day ART initiation in key areas (44.4%, IQR: 32.3–51.1) remained significantly lower than in non-key areas (55.8%, IQR: 34.0–61.7, P = 0.004). Similarly, the proportion of 6-month follow-up in key areas (93.5%, IQR: 91.1–95.1) was slightly lower than in non-key areas (97.5%, IQR: 95.7–98.8, P = 0.023). Conversely, the proportion of viral suppression in key areas (82.9%, IQR: 77.2–87.7) did not differ significantly from that in non-key areas (80.5%, IQR: 77.5–86.3, P = 0.547). Quasi-Poisson segmented regression analysis revealed that the increases in all three indicators during the first month following the Action Plan were more pronounced in key areas compared to non-key areas (Additional file Table S3, Fig. S1). Stratified analysis indicated that in key areas, the increase in the proportion of 30-day ART initiation was more prominent among males (IRR 3.442, 95% CI: 2.068–5.730) than among females (2.833, 95% CI: 1.771–4.530), while in non-key areas, the pattern was reversed, with a more significant increase among females (2.300, 95% CI: 1.578–3.352) compared to males (2.079, 95% CI: 1.465–2.949). The highest immediate increase in key areas occurred among individuals aged 35–59 years (IRR 3.471, 95% CI: 2.155–5.588), which was greater than the corresponding increase in non-key areas among those aged ≥ 60 years (2.596, 95% CI: 1.386–4.862). The immediate increase in the proportion of 6-month follow-up was relatively consistent across age groups in key areas. In contrast, in non-key areas, this increase was least significant among individuals aged 60 and above (IRR 1.114, 95% CI: 1.025–1.210) compared to the increase in key areas (1.395, 95% CI: 1.221–1.595). No significant immediate increase in the proportion of viral suppression was observed in either key or non-key areas (Additional file Table S3). Discussion Our study assessed the impact of implementing a comprehensive strategy on short- and long-term treatment outcomes among newly diagnosed PLHIV in routine settings. The findings revealed significant increases in the proportions of 30-day ART initiation by 2.5-fold, 6-month follow-up by 34.0%, and viral suppression by 17.7% in the first month after implementation. These results underscore the substantial and immediate impact of the comprehensive strategy on the HIV treatment continuum. After the Action Plan, the three indicators continued to improve, consistently keeping higher than expected at the end of the study period, thereby highlighting the sustained effectiveness in the three outcomes of the comprehensive strategy over prior approaches, including the treat-all policy. Improvements in the three indicators were more pronounced in key areas than in non-key areas, with notable differences among subgroups. These discrepancies emphasize the need for targeted comprehensive strategies, especially in non-key areas. Sensitivity analysis confirmed that seasonal changes did not affect these outcomes. Several factors could explain these findings. First, the comprehensive strategy including universal HIV testing and enhanced treatment measures effectively bolstered the execution of the treat-all policy, ensuring full coverage of treatment services. Second, the establishment of a robust case tracking management system and the introduction of an innovative township-based model for precise management enhanced patient retention within the HIV treatment continuum [ 34 ]. The average interval from diagnosis to treatment for PLHIV, before the Action Plan, was greater than the national average of 70 days from 2014 to 2015 [ 35 ]. This discrepancy can be attributed to the unique challenges faced by Liangshan Prefecture, a remote, impoverished area with a significant HIV epidemic and a greater proportion of PWID among PLHIV [ 1 , 27 ]. After the Action Plan, particularly after one year of universal screening, this interval significantly decreased, approaching the national level of 24 days from 2016 to 2020 [ 35 ]. The comprehensive strategy dramatically transformed the local HIV treatment landscape, improving the timeliness of ART initiation, and increasing the treatment coverage rate from 41 to 94%, largely due to the expanded use of ART for all PLHIV [ 1 ]. The significant improvement in the speed and coverage of ART initiation is consistent with findings from other studies examining universal treatment [ 13 , 17 – 19 ]. Our study showed a greater immediate increase of 2.5-fold in the proportion of 30-day ART initiation compared to the 12.6% increase reported under the treat-all policy in China [ 13 ]. However, by the end of the study period, the proportion remained lower than that observed in Rwanda and other regions of Africa, as well as a few countries of Asian with the policies of UTT and “rapid initiation” [ 18 , 36 , 37 ]. This can be attributed to several factors. The positive impact of an intensified campaign of promotion and publicity and a robust government leadership model with sound organizational systems within the comprehensive strategy [ 1 ] were counterbalanced by the fact that the implementation of the “rapid initiation” policy in China was later than other countries [ 38 – 40 ] and disruptions caused by the COVID- 19 pandemic, which resulted in a decline in ART initiation in 2021 [ 30 ]. Subgroup analyses revealed consistent improvements across genders, similar to findings from comprehensive measures implemented in regions such as South Africa [ 41 ]. Notably, older patients (&gt; 35 years) showed superior improvement in both immediate and sustained changes compared to younger patients, consistent with related studies [ 18 ]. The proportion of 6-month follow-up exhibited a notable improvement, both in the immediate and extended term. These results were more satisfactory than those observed in other studies [ 18 , 20 , 42 , 43 ]. The immediate surge, along with the steady accumulation to higher proportions at the end of our analysis, surpassing those in Rwanda and Uganda [ 18 , 20 ]. A study conducted in 25 countries across different regions globally has revealed a decline in ART retention following the implementation of UTT [ 19 ]. While the comprehensive strategy included co-interventions aimed at the HIV treatment continuum, such as the treatment management system and precision management model, specifically, the “Three Lines and One Network Base” technical support system and the “1 + M + N” township prevention system (detailed introductions provided in the Methods) [ 1 , 16 ], which focused on key populations, including those who were mobile or lost to follow-up, and led to a reduction in ART cohort attrition (including death, loss to follow-up, and transfer out). The absence of such comprehensive measures in some studies may explain their less pronounced improvements [ 43 ]. Stable short-term follow-up was ensured for patients with different characteristics, reducing disparities, and with a particular focus on preventing mother-to-child transmission, resulting in significant improvement in the child group [ 44 ]. A significant improvement in viral suppression was also observed, reversing the temporal trend and resulting in a 50.6% increase by December 2021 compared to the expected level. This aligns with findings from other studies showing an increase in viral suppression rates after the Action Plan [ 1 , 16 ]. The strategy emphasized ART initiation as a critical target for HIV control, expanded HIV testing through measures such as free health check-ups in high-epidemic counties, and enhanced patient tracking into treatment programs. Diverse treatment management measures targeting retention in care, including individualized management by village-level health workers and the “from mouth to stomach” strategy of monitoring medication intake to ensure correct and standardized medication, improved retention and adherence [ 42 ]. Additionally, increasing HIV viral load monitoring coverage and frequency, coupled with convenient “color code” classification management based on result of viral load test [ 1 , 16 ], played a crucial role in these outcomes [ 45 ]. These comprehensive measures ensured robust ART initiation, follow-up, and viral suppression rates, showcasing the efficacy of a well-coordinated, multifaceted approach in managing HIV in high-burden regions. Despite these successes, gaps remain that require continued implementation of targeted comprehensive strategies. Differences in the improvement of treatment indicators between key and non-key areas were observed. More pronounced improvements were seen in key areas, accompanied by a decline in the proportion of cases, despite having a higher proportion of PWID among PLHIV. Especially, males in key areas, historically less likely to timely ART initiation [ 14 ], have received more attention and shown greater improvements. These improvements can be largely attributed to the more intensive interventions in key areas, including population-based universal HIV testing and awareness-raising activities. In contrast, non-key areas, characterized by distinct patient characteristics and treatment outcomes, require more targeted interventions. Both males and patients over 60 years old in non-key areas have shown higher proportions of cases. However, males in these areas demonstrated less improvement in timely ART initiation, and older patients experienced suboptimal progress in both 6-month follow-up and viral suppression rates. Additionally, the timeliness of ART initiation remained a need for substantial enhancements, especially in key areas. Compared to reports from other countries [ 18 , 36 , 43 ], the rapidity of ART initiation still lags behind. This underscores the importance of persistently executing comprehensive strategies that emphasize earlier ART initiation, which shortens the time to viral suppression and helps reduce HIV transmission [ 46 ]. Furthermore, a decline in CD4 counts among newly diagnosed cases was noted, and with a reduction in younger cases, which may be related to an increase in late diagnoses, resulting from the universal HIV screening. This trend is further evidenced by the surge in case numbers in 2018, reflecting the effectiveness of population-based HIV screening, with a 98% of testing rate across severely affected counties at that time [ 16 ]. Simultaneous screening and prevention of multiple diseases have the potential to enhance the strength and sustainability of comprehensive strategies, thereby contributing to solving the challenging issues [ 1 , 16 ]. Our study provides evidence for the effectiveness of this comprehensive strategy in a HIV highly endemic area in China by applying a quasi experimental ITS analysis to quantitatively evaluate the short- and long-term impacts on ART care and management. We obtained robust results with adjustments for long-term trends and seasonal variations utilizing data from the complete population over many consecutive years in Liangshan. This information can help other regions take measures to improve the quality of ART. However, our research has several limitations. First, we primarily evaluated the impact of the comprehensive strategy on treatment management rather than on diagnosis and detection. Despite the sharp increase in the number of newly diagnosed patients following the implementation of universal HIV screening in the Action Plan, we did not observe a corresponding increase in the CD4 count at diagnosis, which is consistent with other research results [ 13 , 18 , 43 ]. This highlights the necessity for further evaluation of the long-term impact of this comprehensive strategy on early diagnosis, particularly with expanded screening efforts. Second , the final two years (2020–2021) of our study period coincided with the COVID- 19 pandemic, which may have influenced the effectiveness of the comprehensive strategy in Liangshan. Despite these challenges, our findings suggest that the strategy’s impact has remained robust, supported by proactive government measures to mitigate the pandemic’s effects on HIV prevention and treatment [ 47 ]. Additionally, the proportion of viral suppression reported was among those with available viral load results, rather than among all patients on ART, which could have overestimated the proportion [ 45 ]. However, the proportion of patients for whom viral load data was unavailable after the Action Plan was exceedingly low (less than 0.3%). The comparable proportions of viral suppression observed in relevant studies [ 1 , 45 ] suggest that our significant results are not overestimated. Conclusions In summary, the implementation of a comprehensive strategy in Liangshan, encompassing universal testing and enhanced treatment measures, significantly improved timely ART initiation, retention in ART, and effectiveness of ART, demonstrating both the immediate and long-term effects of the three indicators. Our results indicate that context-specific strategies addressing regional and population disparities can increase timely ART initiation and treatment continuity. This study provides valuable insights for other regions aiming to control HIV epidemics through comprehensive, treatment-focused strategies. Further research is necessary to explore the impact of such strategies on early patient detection and treatment outcomes to inform broader application.
