Abstract Introduction REAL6 is a randomized, multi-centre, open-labelled, active-controlled parallel group phase 3 trial ( NCT04970654 ) investigating once-weekly somapacitan, a reversible albumin-binding growth hormone (GH) derivative, in Chinese children with GH deficiency (GHD). Methods 110 treatment-naïve patients were randomized 2:1 to somapacitan (0.16 mg/kg/week) or daily GH (Norditropin ® ; 0.034 mg/kg/day) for 52 weeks. Annualized height velocity (HV; cm/year) at week 52 was the primary measurement. Additional assessments included HV standard deviation score (SDS), height SDS (HSDS), bone age, insulin-like growth factor-I (IGF-I) SDS, and observer-reported outcomes. Results Estimated mean HV at week 52 was 11.0 and 10.4 cm/year for somapacitan and daily GH, respectively. Non-inferiority of somapacitan compared to daily GH was confirmed. Changes in HVSDS, HSDS, bone age, and IGF-I SDS from baseline to week 52 were similar between groups. At week 52, mean (SD) IGF-I SDS was within intended reference range (−2.0 to +2.0) and comparable between groups: +0.5 (1.4) for somapacitan versus +0.1 (1.2) for daily GH. Somapacitan was well tolerated with a safety profile consistent with the well-known safety profile of daily GH. A low proportion of injection site reactions were reported for somapacitan (2.7%), with no reports of injection site pain during the 52-week treatment period. Disease burden was reduced from baseline to week 52 for both treatments. Somapacitan reduced treatment burden compared to daily GH. Conclusions Efficacy and safety profiles were comparable for Chinese children with GHD treated with somapacitan or daily GH. Both treatments similarly reduced disease burden, while treatment burden was reduced with somapacitan. Plain Language Summary Our body makes a “growth hormone (GH)” that helps children grow. Some children have a condition called “growth hormone deficiency (GHD)” because their bodies do not make enough GH. These children grow slowly compared with other children their age. Doctors can help children with this condition grow by prescribing a medicine containing the GH molecule, but this requires injects under the skin every day. The REAL6 study looks into a new GH medicine called “somapacitan” that only needs to be injected once a week. To somapacitan with a daily GH medicine that doctors can prescribe right now, doctors recruited 110 children with GHD in China. None had gone through puberty or taken GH medicines before the study started. The children were randomly divided into two groups: in group A, the doctors treated 75 of the children with somapacitan and in group B, the doctors treated 36 children with a daily GH medicine (Norditropin ® , Novo Nordisk). After treating the children for 1 year, the doctors found that children taking somapacitan once a week grew just as well as children taking the daily GH medicine. The safety of somapacitan was similar to the daily GH medicine. Side effects with somapacitan were mostly mild and like those seen in other studies looking into GH medicines in children. Overall, somapacitan was effective and well tolerated in this study, meaning somapacitan might be a valuable alternative for treating Chinese children with GHD. Keywords Growth hormone deficiency Growth hormone replacement therapy Long-acting growth hormone Somapacitan Height This study (Clinical Trial Registration: NCT04970654 ) was supported by the sponsor, Novo Nordisk. The sponsor was involved in the study design, collection, analysis, interpretation, and presentation of data. Participants were examined at weeks 0 (baseline), 4, 13, 26, 39, and 52; dosing was calculated based on body weight at these visits. Efficacy measurements, adverse event (AE) recording, and safety laboratory measurements took place at these time points. Patients Prepubertal children with a confirmed diagnosis of GHD and no prior exposure to GH therapy and/or IGF-I treatment were enrolled. Informed consent was obtained in writing from the parents and/or the child’s legally acceptable representative, and child assent was obtained as age appropriate. Key inclusion criteria included: for girls – Tanner stage 1 for breast development (no palpable glandular breast tissue) and aged 2.5–10 years at screening; for boys – testes volume &lt;4 mL and aged 2.5–11 years at screening. For all children: GH- or IGF-I treatment-naïve with confirmed GHD diagnosis determined by two different GH stimulation tests performed 12 months prior to randomization (peak GH ≤10.0 ng/mL using WHO International Somatropin 98/574 standard), impaired height (defined as at least 2.0 SD below mean), impaired height velocity (HV) (annualized HV at screening &lt;7 cm/year for participants between 2.5 and 3 years and &lt;5 cm/year for participants aged 3 and above), bone age (BA) less than chronological age (CA), and IGF-I levels &lt;−1.0 SDS. Children with any clinically significant abnormality likely to affect growth or the ability to evaluate growth with standing measurements were excluded. Other exclusion criteria included: children born small for gestational age; diabetes mellitus diagnosis; attention deficit hyperactivity disorder (ADHD) diagnosis; concomitant administration of treatments that may affect growth, such as methylphenidate for treatment of ADHD; and prior history or presence of malignancy and/or intracranial tumour. Eligible participants were randomly assigned using a study-specific, interactive web-based response system. Randomization was stratified by age (&lt;6; ≥6 years), sex (boys; girls), and GH peak concentration (&lt;7.0; ≥7.0 ng/mL). Treatment adherence during the trial was monitored by electronic diaries. The date, time, and injection dose of the trial drug as well as any missed doses were recorded. Study Drug Administration and Dose Selection Both treatments were administered s.c. according to the FDA/EMA/PDMA approved administration route for Norditropin ® and Sogroya ® . Somapacitan was provided as 5 mg/1.5 mL, 10 mg/1.5 mL, and 15 mg/1.5 mL pre-filled pen injectors of the FlexPro ® family, while daily GH was provided using Norditropin FlexPro ® 10 mg/1.5 mL. Objectives and Endpoints Primary Endpoint The primary endpoint was annualized HV (cm/year) at week 52, which was calculated based on standing height measured with a stadiometer at baseline and week 52. We note that assessors performing height measurements were blinded to treatment allocation. Other Efficacy Endpoints Supportive secondary efficacy endpoints included change from baseline to week 52 in HV standard deviation score (SDS), height SDS (HSDS), and BA versus CA ratio. HSDS was derived using Chinese general population standards [ 9 ]. In case of HVSDS, Prader standards were used [ 10 ]. BA (radiograph of left hand and wrist) was centrally assessed as previously described [ 11 ]. PD Endpoint IGF-I analyses were performed based on serum concentrations of IGF-I determined by a central laboratory using a commercially available assay kit (Immunodiagnostic Systems Immunoassay [IDS-iSYS assay]). Normal reference ranges for the calculation of SDS were based on the publication by Bidlingmaier et al. [ 12 ]. To provide information on IGF-I fluctuations for somapacitan treatment, samples were collected 1–4 days after somapacitan dosing to characterize peak levels (at weeks 4 and 26), 4–6 days after somapacitan dosing to capture expected weekly average levels (at week 52), and on day 7 before the next dosing to assess trough levels (at weeks 13 and 39). Weekly average IGF-I SDS was calculated using PK/PD modelling as previously described [ 8 ]. Safety Assessments Safety was assessed by the incidence of AEs, which were summarized by treatment, Medical Dictionary for Regulatory Activities (MedDRA) system organ class, and MedDRA preferred term. Other safety measures included injection site reactions evaluated at every visit from visit 2 (week 0), occurrence of anti-somapacitan and anti-GH antibodies, incidence of technical complaints, and changes from baseline to week 52 in clinical safety laboratory parameters, including haematology, biochemistry, hormones, fasting lipids, fasting glucose, fasting insulin, and glycated haemoglobin. Assessment of antibodies against somapacitan or daily GH was performed using a validated antibody-binding assay [ 13 ]. Observer-Reported Outcomes Questionnaires were completed by the parent/caregiver to evaluate the full spectrum of GH treatment impacts and burden as well as overall improvements. Growth Hormone Deficiency-Child Impact Measure (GHD-CIM) is a validated questionnaire developed in line with FDA guidance [ 14 ] to assess the impact of GHD on children with GHD [ 15 ]. GHD-CIM was measured at baseline, week 26, and week 52. Additional validated questionnaires, also developed in line with FDA guidance [ 14 ], included the Growth Hormone Deficiency-Child Treatment Burden (GHD-CTB) and Growth Hormone Deficiency-Parent Treatment Burden (GHD-PTB) [ 16 ], were completed at week 26 and 52. GHD-CTB and GHD-PTB measure the burden of GH treatment on children with GHD and parent(s)/caregiver(s), respectively. Statistical Analysis Two analysis populations were defined: the full analysis set included all randomly assigned participants (used for efficacy outcome analyses) and the safety analysis set included all participants exposed to one or more doses of trial product (used for safety outcome analyses). HV at week 52 was analysed using a mixed model for repeated measurements with treatment, sex, age group, GH peak group and sex by age group interaction term as factors, and baseline height as a covariate, all nested within week as a factor. The pre-specified non-inferiority threshold was −2.0 cm/year. Changes in HSDS, HVSDS, and IGF-I were analysed using the same analysis model with the exception that baseline HSDS, baseline HVSDS, and IGF-I were used, respectively, as covariates in the model instead of baseline height. Change in bone age was analysed using an ANCOVA model on change in BA/CA assessed at week 52, and the model included treatment, sex, age group, GH peak group and sex by age group interaction term as factors and BA/CA at screening as a covariate. Observer-reported outcomes were analysed using a mixed model for repeated measurement, including the same factors as the primary analysis as well as the baseline assessment for GHD-CIM. Safety endpoint changes from baseline to week 52 in glucose metabolism parameters were analysed using descriptive statistics. All AEs with onset after the first administration of treatment and with start date up until 14 days after last dose or until week 52 (whichever comes first) were included and analysed using descriptive statistics. Results Study Population and Patient Disposition One hundred and ten prepubertal, treatment-naïve Chinese children with GHD were randomized and exposed to somapacitan ( n = 74) or daily GH ( n = 36), and 103 participants completed the 52 weeks of treatment. Three and four children in the somapacitan and daily GH groups, respectively, discontinued treatment prematurely and were withdrawn from the trial. Reasons for withdrawal included: withdrawal by parent/guardian ( n = 3); other ( n = 3); lost to follow-up ( n = 1). Of these, one participant in the somapacitan group discontinued trial treatment due to an AE of adenoidal hypertrophy exacerbation (described below). Demographics and baseline characteristics were largely similar between treatment groups ( Table 1 ). According to diary, adherence was high for both treatments. Mean and median adherence for somapacitan were 95.3% and 100.0%, respectively. Mean and median adherence for daily GH were 94.0% and 98.8%, respectively. Efficacy Results Primary Endpoint: HV From baseline to week 52, similar increases in annualized HV were observed with both treatments ( Fig. 2 ). Estimated mean HV after 52 weeks of treatment was numerically higher for somapacitan compared to daily GH (11.0 cm/year for somapacitan and 10.4 cm/year for daily GH). The estimated treatment difference (ETD) was 0.6 cm/year (95% confidence interval: [−0.2; 1.3]). Non-inferiority of somapacitan compared to daily GH was confirmed (non-inferiority margin: −2.0 cm/year). Other Growth-Related Assessments Secondary growth-related parameters also increased similarly between groups, with numerically higher mean change differences in HVSDS and HSDS for somapacitan versus daily GH. Estimated changes from baseline to week 52 in HVSDS were 9.01 and 8.23 for somapacitan and daily GH, respectively (ETD: 0.77 [−0.17; 1.71] 95% CI), while estimated changes from baseline to week 52 in HSDS were 1.23 and 1.10 for somapacitan and daily GH, respectively (ETD: 0.13 [−0.02; 0.28] 95% CI). The observed mean HSDS for both groups entered the normal reference range for height (−2.0 to +2.0 SDS) during the study period ( Fig. 3 a). Numerically, 58 (81.7%) participants treated with somapacitan and 24 (72.7%) participants treated with daily GH were within this range at week 52. BA to CA ratio advanced similarly for somapacitan and daily GH groups (ETD: −0.01 [−0.05; 0.03] 95% CI), and the ratio stayed below one for the majority of participants. IGF-I SDS Observed mean IGF-I SDS remained within the intended reference range (−2.0 to +2.0 SDS) throughout the study, including at peak sampling times (weeks 4 and 26) and trough sampling times (weeks 13 and 39) for somapacitan. For somapacitan at week 52, IGF-I samples were collected 4–6 days after dosing to estimate weekly average IGF-I. Similar mean (SD) and median IGF-I SDS were observed between treatment groups at week 52: +0.50 (1.4) and +0.36 for somapacitan compared to +0.10 (1.2) and +0.26 for daily GH ( Fig. 3 b). Change in mean (SD) IGF-I SDS from baseline to week 52 was also similar between groups: 2.09 (1.28) and 1.73 (1.00) for somapacitan and daily GH, respectively (ETD: 0.41 [−0.08; 0.89] 95% CI). The PK/PD model-derived mean (SD) average weekly IGF-I SDS for somapacitan was within intended reference range: +0.73 (0.76). Safety Results The number of participants with reported AEs was 68 (91.9%) and 31 (86.1%) for somapacitan and daily GH groups, respectively ( Table 2 ). Most AEs were mild or moderate in severity (99.1%) and assessed unlikely to be related to the trial product (87.3%). In total, eight (10.8%) participants reported 11 serious AEs in the somapacitan group, while 1 participant (2.8%) reported a serious AE in the daily GH group. Except for one serious AE in the somapacitan group (oedema: mild severity and full recovery was reported; possibly related to trial product), all serious AEs were assessed unlikely to be related to the trial product. The most common AEs observed in more than 10% of the participants in either group were illnesses common in children, including: upper respiratory tract infection, pyrexia, cough, COVID-19, respiratory tract infection, and bronchitis. There were no deaths. One AE of adenoidal hypertrophy exacerbation (the patient had a history of adenoidal hypertrophy) in the somapacitan group led to premature discontinuation of trial product. The event was non-serious, of mild severity, and assessed by the investigator as possibly related to trial product. One participant had two consecutive IGF-I values &gt;+2.5 SDS in the somapacitan group. No dose adjustment was needed as this was at end of study, ensuring patient safety. No other participants had elevations of IGF-I SDS &gt;+2.0 on two consecutive samples during treatment. Two (2.7%) and four (11.1%) of the participants in the somapacitan and daily GH treatment groups, respectively, reported injections-site reactions. No participants reported injection site pain during the 52-week treatment period. Antidrug antibodies were detected in 17 (23.0%) participants treated with somapacitan and 7 (19.4%) participants treated with daily GH. Of these, 5 (6.8%) and 3 (8.3%) children in the somapacitan and daily GH groups, respectively, had at least two consecutive positive antibody samples. No impact on growth response, PK/PD, or AEs was observed for children with positive antidrug antibody samples in either group. There were no clinically relevant findings related to glucose metabolism (i.e., change in fasting plasma glucose and glycated haemoglobin). Observer-Reported Outcomes The change in disease burden, as assessed by change in GHD-CIM scores from baseline to week 52, showed a high degree of similarity in reduced disease burden between treatment groups ( Fig. 4 a). Treatment burden (GHD-CTB and GHD-PTB) was not assessed at baseline since enrolled participants were treatment-naïve. Results from these treatment burden assessments at week 52 favoured somapacitan over daily GH across all domains ( Fig. 4 b, c), with the difference being statistically significant for reduction in treatment burden for GHD-PTB interference score and GHD-PTB total score. Discussion Non-inferiority in HV after 52 weeks of treatment was demonstrated for somapacitan compared to daily GH in treatment-naïve, prepubertal Chinese children with GHD. Somapacitan was well tolerated and shared a similar efficacy and safety profile to the well-known profile for daily GH. First-year growth response after initiating GH therapy can be variable and due to a complex number of factors (e.g., disease severity, age, adherence to therapy) [ 17 ]. It has been suggested that differences in genetics or race/ethnicity may also be linked to varying therapy responses [ 18 , 19 ]. However, results for somapacitan-treated Chinese children with GHD presented in the current REAL6 study demonstrate similar efficacy, safety, and IGF-I in this population as were previously observed for the global phase 3 REAL4 trial population [ 5 ]. Moreover, a recent subgroup analysis of 30 Japanese children with GHD from REAL4 revealed comparable efficacy, safety, IGF-I, and disease/treatment burden outcomes for somapacitan and daily GH [ 20 ], while an analogous sub-analysis of somapacitan-treated adults with GHD in the phase 3 REAL1 study showed similar efficacy and safety in Japanese patients compared to the global population [ 21 ]. Recent evidence suggests that baseline gene expression signatures detected in blood are much better than clinical variables for predicting first-year growth response to GH therapy [ 22 ]. We note that a numerically higher increase in HV after 52 weeks of treatment was observed for somapacitan compared to daily GH in REAL6, which was not the case for the 52-week main phase of the global REAL4 trial [ 5 ]. Participants receiving daily GH in REAL4 were switched after 52 weeks to once-weekly somapacitan (switch group), while participants in the somapacitan group continued to receive somapacitan. Regardless of the change in treatment regimen, both groups followed their growth trajectory during the second year of the REAL4 study (week 52–104), indicating that baseline characteristics [ 23 ] and baseline gene expression patterns [ 22 ] are likely foundational factors for treatment response, rather than a difference in response to daily GH versus once-weekly somapacitan. Clinical guidance recommends maintaining IGF-I within the intended reference range (−2.0 to +2.0 SDS) and avoiding sustained values above +2.0 SDS [ 7 ]. The IGF-I profile during LAGH treatment differs from that observed with daily GH [ 24 ], with serum IGF-I typically increasing 1–2 days after LAGH administration before decreasing prior to the next injection. Modest and transient IGF-I levels above +2.0 SDS have not been linked to AEs and may be acceptable. In practice, the IGF-I profile for LAGHs like somapacitan requires that clinicians account for both dosing and blood sampling times when estimating average weekly IGF-I levels. We therefore recommend that clinicians familiarize themselves with a published tool developed by Kildemoes et al. for estimating weekly average IGF-I SDS and/or IGF-I concentrations in somapacitan-treated paediatric patients [ 8 ]. In the current study, we observed that mean peak and trough IGF-I SDS for somapacitan-treated children stayed within the intended reference range (−2.0 and +2.0 SDS) throughout the study. The observed mean IGF-I SDS at week 52 for somapacitan (+0.5), when samples were collected 4–6 days after somapacitan dosing, aligned well with the model-derived mean IGF-I weekly average SDS (+0.73). Importantly, similar efficacy results obtained in this study for somapacitan and daily GH were accompanied by similar mean change from baseline to week 52 in IGF-I SDS. Disease and treatment burden were evaluated in the current study. In the context of disease burden, changes from baseline to week 52 showed that treatment with somapacitan reduced disease burden in Chinese children with GHD to a degree comparable with the daily GH regimen. These results are in line with comparable efficacy results between treatment groups observed in the current REAL6 study and are consistent with GHD-CIM findings in the global REAL4 study [ 5 ]. Daily injections represent a significant treatment burden for children receiving daily GH therapy as well as for their parents/caregivers [ 25 ]. Treatment burden assessments reported here showed a consistent trend favouring somapacitan over daily GH, with statistically significant results observed for GHD-PTB interference and total scores, consistent with findings from previous studies [ 5 , 26 ]. We attribute reduced treatment burden to fewer injections with somapacitan and notably few injection site reactions for somapacitan reported in the current REAL6 study (2.7%), including no reports of injection site pain. This conclusion is in line with patient-preference questionnaire results from REAL3 and REAL4 where nearly all parents/caregivers in groups switching from daily GH to somapacitan had a “strong” or “very strong” preference for the once-weekly somapacitan treatment regimen (with none favouring daily GH over once-weekly somapacitan) [ 23 , 27 ]. The combination of a desirable therapeutic profile and reduced treatment burden are expected to deliver an overall positive treatment experience and may facilitate acceptability for Chinese children with GHD and their caregivers. The trial was limited by lack of blinding which was not possible since this would require a placebo, which is not considered ethical in paediatric populations. A strength of the current study was that height assessments were performed in an assessor-blinded manner to limit bias, as was also the case with the global phase 3 REAL4 study [ 5 ]. As expected in a controlled clinical trial, adherence in this study was high in both groups. In conclusion, non-inferiority in HV for once-weekly somapacitan compared to daily GH was demonstrated with similar safety and IGF-I response in treatment-naïve Chinese children with GHD following 52 weeks of treatment. These results align well with observations from the global phase 3 REAL4 study [ 5 ] and meet expectations from medical and research societies for a LAGH replacement therapy to establish a less burdensome treatment regimen that matches the well-known efficacy and safety profile of daily GH.
